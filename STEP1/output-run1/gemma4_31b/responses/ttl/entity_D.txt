### Building Analysis
The building is a modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017) with an existing **EPC score of 87.0**, which places it in the **A Label** category. It is already highly energy-efficient, featuring a heat pump, a high-end Ventilation System D (mechanical supply and exhaust), and existing PV panels.

Most building elements—including the walls, the floor above the basement, and the flat roof extension—are already **Well Insulated**. The primary thermal weakness identified is the **Main Gable Roof**, which is currently only **Moderately Insulated**.

### Proposed Renovation Path
Given that the owner's motivation is to further improve the EPC and the building is already performing at a high level, the goal is to address the "weakest link" in the thermal envelope to achieve maximum efficiency with minimal waste of capital.

#### Step 1: Upgrade Gable Roof Insulation
*   **Action:** Implement `reno:InsulateGableRoof`.
*   **Rationale:** This is the only major building element not rated as "Well Insulated." Improving the roof insulation will reduce heat loss through the top of the house, directly contributing to a lower energy score.
*   **Practical Considerations:** 
    *   **Vapour Barrier:** As per the ontology, a vapour barrier must be installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.
    *   **Watertightness:** Ensure the roof underlayment is completely air- and watertight.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** With a unit price of **60/m²** and a **low nuisance level**, this is the most cost-effective way to move the building closer to an A+ label.

#### Step 2: Glazing Assessment (Optional/Long-term)
*   **Action:** Evaluate `reno:ReplaceGlazing` (Double $\rightarrow$ Triple Glazing).
*   **Rationale:** The current windows have double glazing and wooden frames in good condition (State value: 8). While triple glazing would further reduce energy loss, the marginal gain in EPC is significantly lower than the roof insulation.
*   **Recommendation:** Because the building is already in the "A" category, replacing windows now is **not cost-effective**. This should only be considered if the window frames reach the end of their lifespan or if the owner has a surplus budget specifically for reaching a near-zero energy target.

The building is already an excellent performer. To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, **the only necessary structural intervention is the insulation of the Main Gable Roof.**
