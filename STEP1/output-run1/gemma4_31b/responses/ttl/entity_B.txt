Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a mid-19th-century attached building with an EPC score of **262.0 (C Label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC. 

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. The most critical error in renovations is upgrading heating systems before reducing the heat demand of the building envelope.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Demand)
The building has significant heat loss through uninsulated elements. These should be addressed first to ensure that any future heating system is correctly sized and efficient.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (RoofExtension):** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority area for heat loss. 
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:InsulateFlatRoof`.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is not insulated. This is a cost-effective way to eliminate "cold feet" and reduce base heat loss.
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:InsulateFloorAboveBasement`.
3.  **External Wall Insulation (Wall_Front & Wall_Rear):** Both walls are "Full Brick" (meaning no cavity for injection) and not insulated. 
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:InsulateWallExterior`. While higher in cost and nuisance, this provides the most significant jump in EPC performance.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation
Increasing insulation creates an airtight seal, which can lead to moisture and mold issues if ventilation is not upgraded from the current natural system (System A).

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation:** Move from Ventilation System A to a more controlled system to maintain indoor air quality.
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:ReplaceVentilationSystem` (Targeting System C or D).

#### Phase 3: Low-Carbon Heating Transition
Now that the heat demand is lowered, the current boiler and radiator system can be replaced. The owner has explicitly indicated a reason to change the emission system.

5.  **Low-Temperature Emission System:** Heat pumps are inefficient when paired with traditional high-temperature radiators.
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:PlaceLowTemperatureEmission` (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators).
6.  **Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal potential is `false` and there is no heat grid, an air-to-water system is the most sustainable choice.
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC score.

7.  **Solar PV Installation:** The building has confirmed potential for PV.
    *   *Suggestion:* `reno:PlacePVPanelsOnRoof`.

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By following this sequence, you avoid "over-specifying" the heat pump (buying a unit larger than necessary because the walls were leaky), which reduces both initial capital expenditure and monthly energy bills.
