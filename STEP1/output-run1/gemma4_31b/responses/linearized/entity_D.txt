To further maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score while remaining cost-effective, I propose the following targeted renovation path.

### 1. Primary Priority: Thermal Envelope Optimization
The only significant thermal weak point identified is the **RoofMain (Gable Roof)**, which is currently rated as **"ModeratelyInsulated."**

*   **Proposed Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** Heat loss through the roof is one of the most significant sources of energy waste. Upgrading this from "Moderately" to "Well" insulated will provide the most immediate impact on reducing the heating load.
*   **Practical Note:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation problems, as per the ontology's point of attention.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. This is a targeted upgrade to the only underperforming structural element.

### 2. Secondary Priority: Glazing Upgrade
The current windows use **Double Glazing** and are in good state. To push the energy performance toward the absolute maximum:

*   **Proposed Action:** **Replace Glazing (Upgrade to Triple Glazing)**
*   **Reasoning:** While double glazing is efficient, triple glazing further reduces U-values and improves thermal comfort, reducing the reliance on the heat pump during peak winter.
*   **Practical Note:** Since the frames are wooden and in "GoodState," it may be possible to replace only the glazing units rather than the entire window frame, which is more cost-effective.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate. The marginal gain is lower than the roof insulation because the building is already efficient, but it is necessary for long-term operational excellence.

### 3. Tertiary Priority: Emission System Optimization
The building already has a Heat Pump, but the emission system is listed generally as a "SpaceHeater."

*   **Proposed Action:** **Place Low Temperature Emission System**
*   **Reasoning:** Heat pumps operate at peak efficiency when paired with low-temperature emission systems (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators). If the current system is high-temperature, the heat pump must work harder, consuming more electricity.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate to High (depending on existing infrastructure). This ensures the "Generation System" (Heat Pump) operates at its lowest possible energy consumption.
