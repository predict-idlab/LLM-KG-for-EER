Since you already have the "gold standard" of systems—a **heat pump** and **Ventilation D** (double-flow ventilation with heat recovery)—the marginal gains from further renovations will be smaller than in a traditional home. To improve the EPC further while remaining cost-effective, we must shift focus from "major overhauls" to "precision optimization."

The only identified weak point in your building envelope is the **moderate insulation of the pitched roof**.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

Given that the house is already highly efficient, the most cost-effective strategy is to follow the **"Envelope First, Systems Second"** logic, focusing on the one remaining thermal bridge.

#### Phase 1: High-Impact Envelope Optimization (Immediate Priority)
**Target: The Pitched Roof**
Since the flat roof and floors are already well-insulated, the pitched roof is your primary source of heat loss (via convection and radiation).
*   **Action:** Upgrade the pitched roof insulation from "moderate" to "excellent." 
*   **Method:** Depending on the current structure, add a layer of high-performance insulation (such as PIR boards or blown-in cellulose/mineral wool) to reach a U-value closer to modern passive house standards.
*   **Why:** Heat rises. Improving the attic/roof insulation is the most cost-effective way to lower the workload on your heat pump and further drop the EPC score.

#### Phase 2: Precision Sealing & Thermal Bridging (Low Cost, High ROI)
**Target: Air-tightness and Micro-leaks**
Even with Ventilation D, small air leaks can occur around window frames or attic hatches.
*   **Action:** Conduct a professional blower-door test or a thermal camera scan.
*   **Method:** Use high-quality sealing tapes or expanding foam to close any identified gaps in the building envelope.
*   **Why:** This ensures your Ventilation D system is managing the airflow exactly as designed, preventing uncontrolled drafts and improving the efficiency of the heat pump.

#### Phase 3: Energy Management & Storage (Operational Optimization)
**Target: PV Integration and Consumption**
Since you already have PV panels and a heat pump, the goal is to maximize **self-consumption** rather than just production.
*   **Action:** Implement a Smart Energy Management System (HEMS).
*   **Method:** 
    1.  **Smart Control:** Integrate your heat pump with your PV system to "overheat" the house slightly (thermal storage) during peak sun hours.
    2.  **Battery Storage (Optional):** Consider a home battery if your electricity tariff structure favors self-consumption over feed-in tariffs.
*   **Why:** This doesn't change the EPC score as much as insulation does, but it drastically reduces your operational costs.
