Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is already a high-performance home (constructed in 2017 with an EPC score of 87). Most elements, including the walls, floor, and flat roof, are already "Well Insulated." 

To satisfy the owner's motivation to further improve the EPC label while remaining cost-effective, I propose the following targeted renovation path.

### 1. Priority Upgrade: Gable Roof Insulation
The main gable roof is the only structural element currently rated as **"Moderately Insulated."** Improving this to "Well Insulated" is the most effective way to reduce thermal bridging and energy loss.
*   **Action:** Insulate the gable roof.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Low unit price (60/m²) and low nuisance level.
*   **Technical Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation problems. Ensure the roof underlayment is completely air- and watertight.
*   **Sequence:** This should be done first as it addresses the primary remaining thermal weakness.

### 2. Secondary Upgrade: Glazing Optimization
The building currently uses double glazing. To push the EPC score lower (towards an A+ label), upgrading the thermal performance of the windows is the next logical step.
*   **Action:** Replace existing double glazing with **Triple Glazing**.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Since the wooden window frames are in "Good State" (value 8), it is significantly more cost-effective to **replace the glazing only** rather than replacing the entire window unit.
*   **Technical Point of Attention:** Before proceeding, verify that the existing wooden frames have the necessary thickness and structural strength to support the heavier triple-pane glass.
*   **Sequence:** Execute after roof insulation, as this is a higher-cost intervention with a higher nuisance level.

### 3. Operational Efficiency: Solar Heat Management
Because the house is already highly insulated and equipped with a heat pump, it is susceptible to overheating during summer months, which can increase cooling costs.
*   **Action:** Install external sunshades.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Low fixed cost (750) and low nuisance.
*   **Technical Point of Attention:** Opt for **outdoor blinds** rather than indoor blinds, as they are significantly more efficient at blocking solar gain before it enters the building.
*   **Sequence:** Final step; this is a finishing touch to optimize long-term operational performance.
