Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a historic attached building (1832) with an EPC score of **262 kWh/m²year (C Label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to **improve the EPC**.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. We must reduce the building's heat demand through insulation before upgrading the heating generation system; otherwise, the new system will be oversized and inefficient.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Shell)
The most significant energy losses are occurring through non-insulated elements.
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Roof Extension):** The extension roof is currently "Not Insulated." This is a priority as heat rises and flat roofs are often major sources of thermal leakage.
    *   *Suggestion:* `InsulateFlatRoof` (Ordering: 25).
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is "Not Insulated," leading to cold drafts and heat loss into the basement.
    *   *Suggestion:* `InsulateFloorAboveBasement` (Ordering: 49).
3.  **Insulate Exterior Walls (Front & Rear):** Both full-brick walls are "Not Insulated" and have no historical protection, making them eligible for upgrades.
    *   *Suggestion:* `InsulateWallExterior` (Ordering: 40). This is more effective than interior insulation for preventing thermal bridging.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation
Once the building is sealed with insulation, ventilation must be upgraded to prevent condensation and ensure healthy indoor air.
4.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** The current System A (natural supply/exhaust) is basic.
    *   *Suggestion:* `ReplaceVentilationSystem` (Ordering: 52). Moving toward a System C or D will improve thermal comfort and efficiency.

#### Phase 3: Low-Carbon Heating Transition
With the heat demand significantly reduced by Phase 1, we can now replace the outdated generation system.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable at this site, an Air-Water heat pump is the most efficient alternative.
    *   *Suggestion:* `PlaceAirWaterHeatpump` (Ordering: 67).
    *   *Note:* Since the owner has a reason to change the emission system (radiators), they should consider `PlaceLowTemperatureEmission` (Ordering: 70) to maximize the heat pump's COP (Coefficient of Performance).

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC score.
6.  **Install Solar PV Panels:** The building has confirmed potential for PV.
    *   *Suggestion:* `PlacePVPanelsOnRoof` (Ordering: 85).

### Cost-Effectiveness Analysis
*   **Avoids Oversizing:** By insulating first, you can install a smaller, cheaper heat pump.
*   **High Impact:** Addressing "Not Insulated" elements (Roof Extension, Walls, Floor) provides the highest immediate drop in kWh/m²year.
*   **Practicality:** The absence of historical protection on walls allows for exterior insulation, which is the most energy-efficient method.
