Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, here is the proposed renovation path. The strategy follows the **"Fabric First"** principle: we first minimize energy loss through the building envelope before upgrading technical systems and adding renewable energy sources.

### **Phase 1: Building Envelope (Energy Loss Reduction)**
*Goal: Reduce the heating demand of the 1862 structure by sealing the thermal envelope.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** 
    *   **Action:** Inject insulation into the four cavity walls (Front, Rear, Left, Right).
    *   **Justification:** All walls are currently "Not Insulated" and have no historical protection. This is the most cost-effective upgrade (Unit Price: 22/m²) with low nuisance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:**
    *   **Action:** Install insulation in the floor above the crawl space.
    *   **Justification:** This prevents cold air from the crawl space from entering the living areas, significantly improving thermal comfort and EPC score.
3.  **Upgrade Window Glazing:**
    *   **Action:** Replace **Single Glazing** with Double or Triple Glazing.
    *   **Justification:** The existing wooden frames are in "Good State," making it more cost-effective to replace only the glazing (Unit Price: 200/m²) rather than the entire window unit. Single glazing is a major source of heat loss.

### **Phase 2: Technical Systems (Efficiency Upgrade)**
*Goal: Replace outdated, inefficient heating and ventilation with high-performance systems.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:**
    *   **Action:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System D with heat recovery).
    *   **Justification:** Once the house is insulated and sealed, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery is essential to maintain air quality without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:**
    *   **Action:** Replace the current mixed emission system (Electric Convectors/Radiators) with an Air-Water Heat Pump.
    *   **Justification:** The building has a stated "reason to change emission system" and lacks potential for geothermal or heat grid connections. Since the building already has some floor heating, an Air-Water heat pump is the most efficient and practical choice for a detached building.

### **Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Operational Cost Reduction)**
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*

6.  **Install PV Panels on Roof:**
    *   **Action:** Place PV panels on the gable roof.
    *   **Justification:** The building has confirmed "potential for PV" and the roof is already "Well Insulated," providing a stable base for installation. This reduces the long-term operational cost of the heat pump.
