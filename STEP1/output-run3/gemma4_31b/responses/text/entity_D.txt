Given that your home was built in 2017 and already features high-efficiency systems (Heat Pump, Ventilation D, and PV panels), you are starting from a very strong baseline. An EPC score of 87 is already quite efficient for a Belgian semi-detached house. 

To further lower this score while remaining cost-effective, you should move away from "major overhauls" and focus on **surgical optimizations**. Since the walls, floors, and heating systems are already optimized, the remaining gains are found in the "moderate" areas and energy management.

Here is your tailored renovation path, sequenced by ROI and impact on the EPC.

### Phase 1: High Impact / Low-to-Medium Cost (The "Quick Wins")
**Target: The Thermal Envelope (Roof)**
The main pitched roof is the only part of your insulation described as "moderate." Because heat rises, this is your primary source of energy leakage.

*   **Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the main pitched roof. 
    *   **Method:** Depending on the roof structure, consider adding high-performance mineral wool or PIR boards to reach modern "Passive House" standards (R-value optimization).
    *   **Why:** This is the most cost-effective way to lower your energy demand and directly improve your EPC score.
*   **Action:** Air-tightness audit (Blower Door Test).
    *   **Method:** Check for "invisible" leaks around window frames, electrical outlets, and the attic hatch.
    *   **Why:** Even with System D ventilation, uncontrolled infiltration can lower efficiency.

### Phase 2: Medium Impact / Medium-to-High Cost (The "Precision" Upgrades)
**Target: Glazing and Solar Synergy**
Your windows were updated in 2020 with double glazing. While good, the jump from double to triple glazing is often what separates a "good" EPC from an "excellent" one.

*   **Action:** Selective upgrade to Triple Glazing (HR+++).
    *   **Method:** Rather than replacing all windows, prioritize the **North-facing** rooms. Since your wooden frames are in good condition, check if the glass units can be replaced without replacing the entire frame.
    *   **Why:** This reduces radiative heat loss and eliminates cold spots, allowing the heat pump to operate at a lower, more efficient flow temperature.
*   **Action:** PV System Audit & Expansion.
    *   **Method:** Analyze your annual production vs. consumption. If your heat pump is causing a deficit in winter, consider adding a few additional high-efficiency panels if roof space permits.
    *   **Why:** Increasing self-consumption reduces reliance on the grid and lowers the operational carbon footprint.

### Phase 3: Long-term Operational Optimization (The "Smart" Layer)
**Target: Energy Management**
Since your hardware (Heat Pump, PV, Vent D) is already installed, the goal is now to make these systems "talk" to each other.

*   **Action:** Install a Home Energy Management System (HEMS).
    *   **Method:** Implement a smart controller that triggers the heat pump to run (e.g., heating a buffer tank or floor) specifically during peaks of PV production.
    *   **Why:** This maximizes the use of your own "free" electricity, reducing operational costs without requiring structural changes.
