To improve an EPC of 262 in a 19th-century Belgian home, you must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. Replacing a heating system before insulating the shell is a common and costly mistake; it leads to oversized systems and inefficiency.

Given that your house is not historically protected, you have more flexibility. Here is the optimal, sequenced renovation path to maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness.

---

### Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Stopping Heat Loss)
*Priority: High | Goal: Reduce the energy demand of the building.*

1.  **Flat Roof Insulation:** This is your "low-hanging fruit." Since the flat roof is currently uninsulated, it is likely a major source of heat loss. Install high-performance rigid insulation boards (PIR/PUR) on top of the roof deck before the final waterproofing layer.
2.  **Floor Insulation (Above Basement):** Heat rises, and a cold floor creates a "draft" effect. Since you are in Ghent, check for subsidies for floor insulation. If you are replacing flooring anyway, install insulation boards or inject insulation if there is a void.
3.  **Interior Wall Insulation:** Since it is a solid brick house and likely attached (limiting exterior insulation options), focus on **interior insulation**. 
    *   *Warning:* To avoid condensation and mold between the brick and the insulation, use breathable materials (like wood fiber or calcium silicate) or a strict vapor barrier.
4.  **Pitched Roof Top-up:** While "moderate," check if it meets current Belgian standards (R-value $\geq$ 4.0). Adding a layer of blown-in cellulose or mineral wool is a cost-effective way to reach modern standards.

### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Priority: Medium | Goal: Prevent moisture buildup and ensure health.*

As you insulate the house, it becomes "tighter." Natural ventilation will no longer be sufficient, increasing the risk of humidity in an 1832 structure.
*   **Upgrade to Mechanical Ventilation:** Install a balanced ventilation system with **Heat Recovery (HRV)**. This recovers heat from outgoing stale air to warm incoming fresh air, significantly reducing the load on your heating system.

### Phase 3: Heating Distribution & Generation
*Priority: Medium-High | Goal: Shift to low-temperature, sustainable heating.*

Now that the house requires significantly less heat, you can downsize your heating system.
*   **Heat Emission Upgrade:** Replace the old radiators with **low-temperature radiators** (larger surface area) or install underfloor heating (especially if you are already working on the basement floor). This is a prerequisite for a Heat Pump.
*   **Heat Pump Installation:** Replace the old boiler with an **Air-to-Water Heat Pump**. Given the attached nature of the house and the lack of geothermal options, an air-source pump is the most viable green alternative. 
    *   *Alternative:* If the budget is tight or insulation is partial, a **Hybrid System** (Gas + Heat Pump) is a pragmatic transitional step.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (The Offset)
*Priority: Low-Medium | Goal: Zero out operational energy costs.*

*   **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Now that your energy consumption is minimized, install PV panels on the pitched roof. This will power the Heat Pump and the ventilation system, moving the house toward a "Net Zero" energy balance.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Order | Action | Why? | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Floor $\rightarrow$ Walls** | Stop heat leakage first. | Very High ROI |
| **2** | **Ventilation (HRV)** | Prevent mold/dampness in a sealed house. | High (Long-term) |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Radiators** | Enables the use of efficient Heat Pumps. | Medium |
| **4** | **Air-to-Water Heat Pump** | Eliminates reliance on fossil fuels. | High (with subsidies) |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity used by the pump. | High |

### Expert Pro-Tip for Ghent/Flanders:
Before starting, consult a certified **EPC advisor** to create a "Renovation Obligation" roadmap. In Flanders, there are significant **premiums (subsidies)** available for wall, roof, and floor insulation, as well as for switching to heat pumps. Following this specific sequence often maximizes your eligibility for these grants.
