### Building Analysis
The building is a modern **Attached Building (constructed in 2023)** with an already high level of energy efficiency (**EPC score of 50**, which typically corresponds to an **A or A+ label**). ### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Roof)
**Intervention:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Analysis:** While most of the building is well-insulated, the **Main Roof (Gable Roof)** is currently only **"Moderately Insulated."** In a high-efficiency home, the roof is the most likely area for heat loss via convection.
*   **Action:** Upgrade the gable roof insulation to a "Well Insulated" grade.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This is a high-impact, relatively low-cost upgrade compared to replacing windows or walls.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: Heating System Transition
**Intervention:** **Replace Boiler with an Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Analysis:** The building currently uses a Boiler and has an expressed reason to change the emission system. Since the site has **no potential for geothermal drilling** and **no access to a heat grid**, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient alternative.
*   **Synergy:** The building already possesses **Floor Heating**, which is a low-temperature emission system. This makes the transition to a heat pump exceptionally efficient, as heat pumps perform best when paired with low-temperature emitters.
*   **Action:** Install an **Air-Water Heat Pump** to replace the existing boiler.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** While the initial investment is higher (fixed price ~8,000 + unit price), the long-term operational costs are significantly lower than a traditional boiler.

### Optimal Sequence & Justification

| Sequence | Intervention | Goal | Justification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Reduce Heat Loss | **"Insulate First" Principle:** By increasing the roof's thermal resistance first, you reduce the overall heating load of the building. |
| **2** | **Install Air-Water Heat Pump** | Decarbonize Heating | **Right-Sizing:** By insulating the roof first, the required capacity of the new heat pump may be lower, reducing both the installation cost and future energy consumption. |
