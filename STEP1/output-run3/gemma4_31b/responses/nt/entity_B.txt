Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is an old (1832) attached building in Belgium with an energy score of **262 kWh/m²year (C Label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to **improve the EPC**.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. This sequence ensures that we reduce the energy demand of the building before installing high-efficiency systems, allowing for smaller, cheaper, and more effective heating installations.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Immediate Impact)
The building has several "Not Insulated" elements. Addressing these first prevents heat loss and is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Roof Extension):** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority upgrade to stop significant heat escape from the top of the building.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is not insulated. This reduces "cold feet" and prevents heat loss into the basement.
3.  **Insulate Exterior Walls (Front and Rear):** Both the front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, **Exterior Wall Insulation** is recommended over interior insulation to maximize the thermal bridge reduction and preserve interior living space.

### Phase 2: System Modernization (Operational Performance)
Once the building is airtight and insulated, the heating load is reduced, making a transition to sustainable energy more efficient.

4.  **Upgrade Emission System:** The building currently uses radiators and a boiler, and the owner has expressed a desire to change the emission system. 
    *   **Recommendation:** Replace the boiler with an **Air-Water Heat Pump**. (Note: Geothermal is not possible as vertical and horizontal drilling potentials are "false").
    *   **Optimization:** If budget allows, transition to **Low-Temperature Emission** systems (e.g., underfloor heating) to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the heat pump.
5.  **Ventilation Upgrade:** The building uses Ventilation System A (natural supply). With increased insulation, the risk of humidity and poor air quality increases. Upgrading to a **Ventilation System D** (mechanical supply and exhaust with heat recovery) is recommended to retain heat that would otherwise be lost through vents.

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Cost Reduction)
The final step is to shift the energy source from the grid to on-site production.

6.  **Install PV Panels:** The building has a confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels on the roof will significantly lower operational costs and further improve the EPC label.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Intervention | Logic | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Floor $\rightarrow$ Walls)** | Reduces total energy demand; prevents "over-sizing" the future heat pump. | High (Long-term savings) |
| **2** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces fossil-fuel boiler with a high-efficiency electric alternative. | Medium (High CAPEX, low OPEX) |
| **3** | **Ventilation System D** | Recovers heat from exhaust air, maintaining comfort in a sealed house. | Medium |
| **4** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the new heat pump. | High (Direct ROI) |

**Key Constraint Note:** Because the building is an **Attached Building**, wall insulation should be coordinated with neighbors where possible, and the **Air-Water Heat Pump** installation must ensure the outdoor unit is placed at least 2m from the parcel boundary to avoid the need for complex permits in Flanders.
