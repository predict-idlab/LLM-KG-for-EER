Based on the provided RENO ontology and the specific data for the building, here is the proposed renovation path. The strategy follows the **"Envelope-First"** principle: reducing energy demand through insulation and glazing before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems to ensure the new systems are sized correctly and operate at maximum efficiency.

### Building Analysis Summary
*   **Current State:** A detached building (1862) with a good EPC score (165), but significant efficiency gaps.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** All exterior walls (Front, Rear, Left, Right) are **uninsulated cavity walls**, and all windows utilize **single glazing**, which are the primary sources of heat loss.
*   **Strengths:** The roof is already well-insulated, and the window frames are in good condition.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal energy is not viable (no potential for vertical or horizontal drilling).

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Low-Hanging Fruit)
*Focus: Stop heat leakage using the most cost-effective methods.*

1.  **Insulate Cavity Walls (`InsulateWallCavity`):** 
    *   **Action:** Inject insulation into the cavity walls of all four facades.
    *   **Reasoning:** This is the most cost-effective upgrade (Unit Price: 22/m²) and provides immediate reductions in heating demand. Since there is no historical protection, this is a practical first step.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space (`InsulateFloorAboveBasement`):** 
    *   **Action:** Add insulation to the floor above the crawl space.
    *   **Reasoning:** Prevents cold air from entering the living spaces from below, improving thermal comfort and EPC.

#### Phase 2: Glazing Upgrade
*Focus: Improving the thermal barrier of the windows without replacing the entire structure.*

3.  **Replace Glazing (`ReplaceGlazing`):** 
    *   **Action:** Upgrade single glazing to double or triple glazing.
    *   **Reasoning:** The existing wooden frames are in "Good State," making it more cost-effective to replace only the glass rather than the entire window unit (`ReplaceWindows`). This significantly reduces drafts and heat loss.

#### Phase 3: System Modernization
*Focus: Transitioning to high-efficiency, low-carbon heating and ventilation.*

4.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump (`PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`):** 
    *   **Action:** Replace the existing boiler with an air-to-water heat pump.
    *   **Reasoning:** Since geothermal is not possible, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient alternative. Because the envelope was optimized in Phases 1 and 2, the heat pump can be smaller and more efficient.
5.  **Upgrade Ventilation System (`ReplaceVentilationSystem`):** 
    *   **Action:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural supply) to a more controlled system (e.g., System B or D).
    *   **Reasoning:** As the house becomes more airtight through insulation and new glazing, mechanical ventilation is necessary to ensure indoor air quality and prevent condensation.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
*Focus: Offsetting the electricity demand of the new heat pump.*

6.  **Install Rooftop PV Panels (`PlacePVPanelsOnRoof`):** 
    *   **Action:** Install solar panels on the roof.
    *   **Reasoning:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Generating electricity on-site will lower the operational costs of the heat pump and the overall energy bill, maximizing the long-term cost-effectiveness of the renovation.
