Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a **1951 semi-detached building** with a significant energy performance gap (**EPC score: 388**, Label D). The owner's primary motivation is to **improve the EPC**, and there is a stated need to change the emission system.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. This ensures that the building's energy demand is reduced before installing a new heating system, allowing for a smaller, more efficient, and cheaper heat pump installation.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Energy Demand)
*Priority: High | Goal: Stop heat loss*

1.  **Roof Insulation (Gable Roof):** The main gable roof is currently not insulated. This is the most cost-effective "quick win" as heat rises. 
    *   **Action:** `InsulateGableRoof`.
2.  **Floor Insulation:** The floor above the basement is not insulated.
    *   **Action:** `InsulateFloorAboveBasement`.
3.  **Wall Insulation:** All three main walls (Front, Right, Rear) are full brick and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, exterior insulation is recommended for maximum efficiency.
    *   **Action:** `InsulateWallExterior`. (If budget is constrained, `InsulateWallInterior` is a cheaper alternative, though it reduces living space).

### Phase 2: Air Quality and Ventilation
*Priority: Medium | Goal: Prevent moisture and ensure healthy air*

Since the building is being sealed with insulation, the current **Ventilation System A** (natural supply) will be insufficient and may lead to condensation.
*   **Action:** `ReplaceVentilationSystem` (Upgrade to System C or D). This ensures a controlled air exchange, maintaining the efficiency of the new insulation.

### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition
*Priority: High | Goal: Decarbonization and operational cost reduction*

With the thermal load reduced by Phase 1, the building can now transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency system. Since geothermal drilling is not possible on this site and there is no heat grid, an air-to-water system is the optimal choice.
*   **Action:** `PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`. 
*   **Note:** Because the building already has floor heating and radiators, the heat pump will integrate well, though a check on the emission temperature of the radiators is advised.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
*Priority: Medium | Goal: Offset operational costs*

The building has a confirmed potential for PV panels. This is the final step to move toward a "Net Zero" operational state.
*   **Action:** `PlacePVPanelsOnRoof`.
*   **Synergy:** The electricity generated will directly power the new Air-Water Heat Pump, drastically lowering monthly energy bills.

| Sequence | Intervention | Rationale | Cost Profile |

| **1** | **Insulate Roof & Floor** | Highest ROI; reduces heat pump sizing requirements. | Low/Medium |

**Estimated Long-term Impact:** This path will likely move the building from an **EPC Label D** to **Label B or A**, significantly increasing the market value and reducing operational energy costs.
