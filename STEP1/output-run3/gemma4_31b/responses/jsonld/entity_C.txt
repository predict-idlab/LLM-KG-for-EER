### Energy Efficiency Renovation Analysis

**Building Profile:**
The property is a modern **Attached Building (constructed 2023)** with an already high energy performance (**EPC Score: 50**, which falls under the **A Label**). Most elements—including the walls, floor above basement, and flat roof extension—are already "Well Insulated." The ventilation system is already at the highest standard (System D).

Despite the high current efficiency, the owner's motivation is to **further improve the EPC**, and there is a recorded **reason to change the emission system**.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a "Fabric First" approach, focusing on the few remaining weak points before upgrading the mechanical systems.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High ROI)
Before upgrading heating systems, we must minimize heat loss to ensure the new system is sized correctly and operates at peak efficiency.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently only "Moderately Insulated." Upgrading this to "Well Insulated" is the most impactful first step to prevent heat rise.
    *   *Ontology Suggestion:* `InsulateGableRoof`
    *   *Priority:* High (Ordering: 28)
2.  **Upgrade to Triple Glazing:** The current windows have wooden frames in "Good State" with double glazing. To move toward an A+ label, upgrading the glass to triple glazing is the most cost-effective way to reduce thermal bridging without replacing the entire window unit.
    *   *Ontology Suggestion:* `ReplaceGlazing`
    *   *Priority:* Medium (Ordering: 43)

#### Phase 2: Sustainable Heating Transition
With the envelope optimized, we address the inefficient emission system (Electric Convectors) and the existing boiler. Since the site has **no potential for geothermal drilling** and **no heat grid access**, an air-source solution is the only viable high-efficiency path.
3.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that the building already has **Floor Heating**, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the optimal choice. It will replace the boiler and eliminate the need for inefficient electric convectors by leveraging the existing low-temperature distribution network.
    *   *Ontology Suggestion:* `PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`
    *   *Priority:* Final Step (Ordering: 67)

---

### Summary of Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Target Element | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Main Roof | Eliminates the primary source of heat loss (Moderately $\rightarrow$ Well Insulated). |
| **2** | **Upgrade Glazing** | Windows | Reduces radiative heat loss; cost-effective as frames are already in good state. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Generation/Emission | Replaces fossil fuel/electric resistance with a high-COP renewable source. |

**Final Performance Outlook:**
By following this sequence, the building will transition from an **A Label** toward an **A+ Label**. By focusing on the roof and glazing first, the heat pump's capacity requirements are lowered, reducing both the initial installation cost and long-term operational electricity consumption.
