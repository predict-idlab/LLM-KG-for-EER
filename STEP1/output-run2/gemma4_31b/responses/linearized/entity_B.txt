### **Proposed Renovation Path**
To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reduce energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems.

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The Priority)**
*Goal: Stop heat loss. This ensures that any future heating system is downsized and operates efficiently.*
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Extension):** The extension roof is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority, high-impact win. 
    *   *Note:* Opt for an inverted roof structure to avoid condensation.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. This prevents cold drafts from the basement.
3.  **Insulate Walls (Exterior):** Both the front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, external wall insulation is the most effective choice to eliminate thermal bridges.

#### **Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality**
*Goal: Ensure healthy air exchange now that the building is "sealed" by insulation.*
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A (natural) to System C or D. 
    *   *Reasoning:* Increasing insulation without improving ventilation can lead to moisture and mold issues. System D (mechanical supply and exhaust) offers the highest efficiency.

#### **Phase 3: Energy Generation & Emission**
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to a high-efficiency electrical system.*
5.  **Replace Boiler with Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal is not an option, an Air-Water heat pump is the most cost-effective high-efficiency alternative to the existing boiler.
6.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission:** The owner has indicated a reason to change the emission system. Replacing old radiators with low-temperature emitters (or floor heating) will allow the heat pump to operate at maximum COP (Coefficient of Performance), drastically lowering monthly energy bills.

#### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration**
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*
7.  **Install PV Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Installing these last allows you to size the system based on the new, lower energy demand of the renovated house.

### **Expert Tips for this Property:**
*   **Wall Insulation:** Since you have full brick walls without historical protection, exterior insulation is significantly more effective than interior insulation, as it protects the brickwork from freeze-thaw cycles.
*   **Window Check:** Your windows are already double-glazed and in good state. While triple glazing is an option, the marginal gain is lower than the gain from wall/roof insulation. Keep the current windows to remain cost-effective.
*   **Permit Warning:** Remember that external wall insulation may require a permit if the total thickness exceeds 26cm or overruns the building line.
