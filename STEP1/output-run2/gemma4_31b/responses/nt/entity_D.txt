### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the following sequence is recommended. This follows the "fabric-first" principle: reducing energy demand before optimizing generation.

#### Step 1: Roof Thermal Optimization
*   **Target:** Main Gable Roof.
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof** (Upgrade from "Moderately Insulated" to "Well Insulated").
*   **Justification:** This is the only primary building envelope element not currently rated as "Well Insulated." Heat loss through the roof is a significant factor in energy consumption.
*   **Practical Note:** Since the roof is in a "Good State," this can be achieved via internal insulation or by adding a layer of high-performance material without needing a full roof replacement.

#### Step 2: Glazing Upgrade
*   **Target:** Windows in Living Area and Bedrooms.
*   **Action:** **Replace Glazing** (Upgrade from Double Glazing to **Triple Glazing**).
*   **Justification:** While double glazing is efficient, triple glazing significantly reduces U-values and improves thermal comfort, preventing cold spots near windows.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Since the existing wooden window frames are in a "Good State," the owner should opt for **glazing replacement only** rather than replacing the entire window unit, which is significantly more cost-effective.

#### Step 3: Emission System Fine-Tuning
*   **Target:** Space Heating Emission System.
*   **Action:** Transition to **Low Temperature Emission** systems (e.g., floor heating or oversized radiators).
*   **Justification:** The building already uses a Heat Pump. Heat pumps operate at maximum efficiency (COP) when the temperature difference between the source and the emission system is minimized. Ensuring the system is "Low Temperature" maximizes the operational performance of the existing heat pump.
