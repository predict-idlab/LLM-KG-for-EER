Based on the provided ontology and building data, the property is a historic attached building (1832) in Belgium with a current energy score of 262 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC (Energy Performance Certificate).

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. By improving the building envelope first, we reduce the overall heating load, allowing for a smaller and more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Highest ROI)
*Focus: Stopping heat loss. This must be done before upgrading the heating system.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** The main gable roof is moderately insulated, but the flat roof extension is **not insulated**. This is a priority as heat rises. 
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#InsulateFlatRoof`.
2.  **Wall Insulation:** Both the front and rear walls are full brick and **not insulated**. 
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#InsulateWallExterior`. Since there is no historical protection on these walls, exterior insulation is the most effective way to eliminate thermal bridges.
3.  **Floor Insulation:** The floor above the basement is currently **not insulated**.
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#InsulateFloorAboveBasement`. This prevents cold air from the basement from entering the living spaces.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & System Efficiency
*Focus: Improving ventilation to prevent condensation (common after insulating) and preparing for low-temperature heating.*

4.  **Ventilation Upgrade:** The building currently uses System A (natural supply/mechanical exhaust). 
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#ReplaceVentilationSystem` (upgrade to System B or D). A more efficient system ensures fresh air without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition
*Focus: Replacing the fossil-fuel boiler with a high-efficiency electric alternative.*

5.  **Heat Pump Installation:** The owner has expressed a reason to change the emission system. Geothermal options are not possible for this site.
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`. 
    *   *Strategic Note:* Because the envelope was optimized in Phase 1, the heat pump will operate much more efficiently and will be cheaper to install as a smaller capacity unit will suffice.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
*Focus: Offsetting the operational electricity costs of the new heat pump.*

6.  **Solar Energy:** The building is confirmed to have potential for PV.
    *   *Recommendation:* `reno#PlacePVPanelsOnRoof`. This provides the electricity needed to run the air-water heat pump, significantly lowering long-term operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Intervention | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Roof $\rightarrow$ 2. Walls $\rightarrow$ 3. Floor** | **Insulation** | Reduces energy demand; prevents "over-sizing" the future heating system. |
| **4. Ventilation** | **System Upgrade** | Maintains air quality and prevents mold/dampness in a now-airtight home. |
| **5. Heating** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source with optimized efficiency. |
| **6. Renewables** | **PV Panels** | Lowers the electricity bills generated by the heat pump. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** I have omitted the replacement of windows. The current windows are already double-glazed and in "Good State," meaning the marginal energy gain from triple glazing would not justify the high cost compared to the massive gains from wall and roof insulation.
