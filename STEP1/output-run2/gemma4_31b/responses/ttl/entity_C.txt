### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Fabric First)
Before upgrading heating systems, the building envelope must be fully optimized to minimize heat loss.
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof** (`reno:InsulateGableRoof`).
*   **Reasoning:** While the walls and flat roof are "Well Insulated," the main gable roof is only "**Moderately Insulated**." This is the primary thermal weakness of the building.
*   **Practical Note:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation, as specified in the ontology.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Improving the roof insulation is a low-nuisance, high-impact way to reduce the load on the heating system.

#### Step 2: Heating System Transition
The building currently utilizes **Electric Convectors**, which are energy-intensive and inefficient, and the data explicitly indicates a **reason to change the emission system**.
*   **Action:** **Install an Air-Water Heat Pump** (`reno:PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`).
*   **Reasoning:** 
    *   **Geothermal is not an option** (site potential is `false` for both horizontal and vertical).
    *   The building already has **Floor Heating**, which is the ideal emission system for a low-temperature heat pump.
    *   Replacing electric convectors with a heat pump will significantly reduce operational energy costs and carbon emissions.
*   **Sequence:** This must follow the roof insulation to ensure the heat pump is sized correctly for the reduced heat loss of the building.

#### Step 3: Long-term Monitoring & Maintenance
*   **Action:** Maintain existing high-performance elements.
*   **Reasoning:** 
    *   **Windows:** Already have double glazing and are in "Good State" (Value 8). Replacing them with triple glazing in a 2023 building would not be cost-effective at this time.

    *   **PV Panels:** Already installed; no further action needed unless capacity needs to be increased.
