Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the building is a detached house from 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (B-label). While the roof is already well-insulated, there are significant energy leaks in the walls and windows.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. We will first seal the building envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: The Building Envelope (High Impact, Low-to-Moderate Cost)
*Goal: Reduce heat loss and improve thermal comfort.*

1.  **Insulate Cavity Walls (`reno:InsulateWallCavity`):** 
    *   **Analysis:** All four walls (Front, Left, Rear, Right) are cavity walls and currently **not insulated**. 
    *   **Justification:** This is the most cost-effective insulation method (unit price: 22/m²) with a low nuisance level. It provides an immediate and significant reduction in heat loss.
2.  **Upgrade Glazing (`reno:ReplaceGlazing`):** 
    *   **Analysis:** Both bedrooms and the living area currently have **single glazing**, which is highly inefficient. However, the wooden frames are in **good state**.
    *   **Justification:** Instead of replacing entire windows, replacing only the glazing (Single $\rightarrow$ Double or Triple) is more cost-effective and sustainable, leveraging the existing high-quality frames.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space (`reno:InsulateFloorAboveBasement`):**
    *   **Analysis:** The building has a floor above a crawl space.
    *   **Justification:** This is a low-nuisance, low-cost upgrade (unit price: 30/m²) that eliminates cold drafts from the ground, significantly improving the EPC and resident comfort.

#### Phase 2: Technical Systems (System Optimization)
*Goal: Transition to sustainable energy generation and healthy air circulation.*

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation (`reno:ReplaceVentilationSystem`):**
    *   **Analysis:** The building uses Ventilation System A (natural). 
    *   **Justification:** After insulating walls and upgrading windows, the house becomes more airtight. Upgrading to a more controlled system (System C or D) is necessary to prevent humidity buildup and ensure air quality.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump (`reno:PlaceAirWaterHeatpump`):**
    *   **Analysis:** There is a documented reason to change the emission system. Geothermal is not possible at this site, and there is no heat grid.
    *   **Justification:** Now that the heat demand is lowered (thanks to Phase 1), a heat pump can be sized smaller and operate more efficiently. An Air-Water system is ideal as it can integrate with the existing radiators and floor heating.

#### Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Operational Cost Reduction)
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heating system.*

6.  **Install Solar PV Panels (`reno:PlacePVPanelsOnRoof`):**
    *   **Analysis:** The building has potential for PV, and the roof is already well-insulated (meaning no asbestos removal or roof replacement is needed first).
    *   **Justification:** This reduces long-term operational costs and lowers the carbon footprint, complementing the heat pump installed in Phase 2.
