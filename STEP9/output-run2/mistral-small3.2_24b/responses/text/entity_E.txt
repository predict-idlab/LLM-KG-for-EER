To create a concise and actionable renovation plan for your 1862 detached house in Ghent, Belgium, we'll focus on maximizing energy efficiency while keeping costs in check. Given the current Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) score of 165, the goal is to significantly improve this through practical upgrades. Here's a step-by-step renovation plan:

### 1. **Insulation Upgrades**
   - **Exterior Walls**: Since the cavity walls are not insulated, consider injecting insulation material into the cavities. This is a cost-effective way to improve thermal performance without altering the external appearance of the historic façade.
   - **Floor Above Crawl Space**: Insulate this floor to prevent heat loss. Use materials like rigid foam boards or mineral wool, depending on the crawl space conditions.
   - **Roof**: Although the main roof has good insulation, check for any gaps or areas that might need additional insulation, especially if there are dormers or other roof sections not mentioned.

### 2. **Window Replacement or Upgrade**
   - **Double Glazing**: Replace the single-glazed windows with double-glazed units within the existing wooden frames if possible. This preserves the historic look while improving insulation.
   - **Secondary Glazing**: If replacing the entire window is too costly, consider installing secondary glazing on the interior side. This is a more affordable option that still significantly reduces heat loss.

### 3. **Heating System Upgrade**
   - **Replace Old Systems**: Since the current heating systems (radiators, underfloor heating, air heating, and electric convectors) are in poor condition, consider replacing them with a more efficient system.
   - **Heat Pump**: Given the lack of district heating and unsuitable conditions for geothermal, an air-source heat pump could be a good option. It’s efficient and can work well with underfloor heating or modern radiators.
   - **Smart Thermostats**: Install smart thermostats to optimize heating schedules and reduce energy waste.

### 4. **Ventilation System Upgrade**
   - **Mechanical Ventilation with Heat Recovery (MVHR)**: Replace the basic natural ventilation system with an MVHR system. This recovers heat from exhaust air and uses it to warm incoming fresh air, significantly improving energy efficiency.

### 5. **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels**
   - **Install PV Panels**: Given the potential for PV panels, install them on the roof to generate renewable energy. This will reduce your electricity bills and potentially allow you to sell excess energy back to the grid, depending on local regulations.

### 6. **Air Sealing**
   - **Draft Proofing**: Seal gaps around windows, doors, and other openings to prevent drafts. Use weatherstripping, sealants, or draft excluders where necessary.

### 7. **Monitoring and Maintenance**
   - **Energy Monitoring**: Install an energy monitor to track your home’s energy use and identify areas for further improvement.
   - **Regular Maintenance**: Ensure that all new systems are regularly maintained to keep them running efficiently.

### Optimal Sequence of Renovations
1. **Insulation**: Start with insulating the exterior walls and the floor above the crawl space. This will address the largest sources of heat loss.
2. **Windows**: Upgrade the windows next to further reduce heat loss and improve comfort.
3. **Heating System**: Replace the old heating systems with a more efficient heat pump and modern radiators or underfloor heating.
4. **Ventilation**: Install the MVHR system to ensure good indoor air quality while minimizing heat loss.
5. **PV Panels**: Finally, install the photovoltaic panels to generate renewable energy and reduce electricity costs.

### Cost-Effectiveness Considerations
- Prioritize upgrades that offer the highest return on investment, such as insulation and window upgrades.
- Consider financing options or subsidies available for energy-efficient renovations in Belgium, which can help offset costs.

By following this plan, you should see a significant improvement in your home’s energy performance, leading to a better EPC score, lower energy bills, and increased comfort.
