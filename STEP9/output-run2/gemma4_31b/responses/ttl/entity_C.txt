### Analysis of Building State
The building is a very modern **Attached Building** (constructed in **2023**) with an already excellent energy profile. It currently holds an **A-label EPC** (energy score of **50.0**), meaning it is highly efficient. Most structural elements (walls, flat roof, floor above basement) are already "Well Insulated." The ventilation system is already a **Type D** (mechanical supply and exhaust), which is the most efficient standard.

However, there are two primary opportunities for optimization based on the provided data:
1.  **Thermal Envelope Gap:** The main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated," unlike the rest of the building.
2.  **System Inefficiency:** The building explicitly has a **reason to change the emission system** (`reno:hasReasonToChangeEmissionSystem true`). It currently relies on electric convectors (which are energy-intensive) and a boiler.

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a targeted two-step path. Because the building is already high-performing, we avoid redundant upgrades (like wall or ventilation changes) and focus on the "weakest links."

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization
**Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** The gable roof is the only major building element not rated as "Well Insulated." Improving this will reduce heat loss through the top of the house, ensuring the building maintains its thermal integrity.
*   **Practical Note:** Since the roof is in "Good State," this is a straightforward insulation upgrade rather than a full replacement.
*   **Sequence:** This must be done **before** upgrading the heating system to ensure the new heat pump is sized correctly for the lower heat demand.

#### Step 2: Heating System Transition
**Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Reasoning:** The building is flagged for an emission system change. It currently uses electric convectors, which are inefficient. An air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and energy-efficient choice here because the building already possesses **Floor Heating**, which is a low-temperature emission system perfectly compatible with heat pump technology.
*   **Alternative Considered:** Geothermal is ruled out as the site has no potential for horizontal or vertical geothermal captation.
*   **Benefit:** This will significantly lower the energy score further, moving the building closer to an A+ label and reducing operational costs.
