To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. By improving the building envelope before upgrading the heating systems, we ensure that the new systems are right-sized for a lower-demand home, preventing over-investment and maximizing long-term operational savings.

### Analysis of Current State
*   **EPC Score:** 388.0 (D Label), indicating significant room for improvement.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The main gable roof, all exterior walls (full brick), and the floor above the basement are **not insulated**.
*   **Heating/Ventilation:** The building uses a basic Ventilation System A and has a flagged need to change the emission system.
*   **Constraints:** No access to a heat grid or geothermal potential.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The "Fabric First" Step)
*The goal here is to stop heat loss. This is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.*

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises. 
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Since the walls are full brick (not cavity walls), cavity injection is not possible. Exterior insulation is preferred over interior insulation to prevent mold and maintain interior living space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is not insulated; addressing this eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the lower level.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation
*Once the building is sealed with insulation, natural ventilation (System A) will be insufficient and may lead to condensation/mold.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System C or D) to ensure a healthy indoor climate and maintain the efficiency of the new insulation.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating & Generation
*Now that the heat demand is significantly lowered, we can install a high-efficiency system.*

5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the building already has floor heating and radiators, and there is no geothermal or heat grid availability, an air-water heat pump is the most practical and efficient upgrade to replace the old boiler.
6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and improve the EPC label further, install solar panels (the building is confirmed to have PV potential).

### Final Expert Note on Cost-Effectiveness
By following this sequence, the owner avoids the common mistake of installing a powerful (and expensive) heating system into a "leaky" house. The insulation steps significantly reduce the required capacity of the **Air-Water Heat Pump**, lowering both the installation cost and the monthly energy bills.
