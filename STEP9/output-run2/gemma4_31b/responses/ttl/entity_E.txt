To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: first reducing energy demand through insulation and sealing, then upgrading the technical systems to be more efficient, and finally incorporating renewable energy.

Based on the provided ontology and building data, here is the proposed renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Demand)
The goal here is to stop heat loss. The building is from 1862 and has significant weaknesses in the walls and windows.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities (`insulate wall cavity`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** All four exterior walls are cavity walls and are currently "Not Insulated." Cavity wall insulation is one of the most cost-effective upgrades (lowest unit price at 22/m²) and has a low nuisance level.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement (`insulate floor above basement`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The building has a floor above a crawl space. Insulating this area prevents cold drafts from the ground, improving thermal comfort and reducing the heating load.
3.  **Upgrade Window Glazing (`replace glazing`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The bedrooms and living area currently have "Single Glazing," which is a major source of heat loss. Since the wooden window frames are in "Good State," replacing only the glazing is more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.

### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
Once the building envelope is tightened (insulated and glazed), air circulation must be managed to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation System (`replace ventilation system`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The building currently uses "Ventilation System A" (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient. Upgrading to a more modern system (such as System C or D) will improve the EPC score and ensure a healthy indoor climate.

### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition
With the heat demand lowered by Phase 1, we can now install a smaller, more efficient heating system.

5.  **Optimize Emission System (`place emission system on low temperature`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The building has a mix of radiators and floor heating. To maximize the efficiency of a heat pump, the emission system should be optimized for low-temperature operation first.
6.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump (`place air-water heatpump`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The owner wants to improve the EPC and there is a reason to change the emission system. Since the site has **no potential** for geothermal drilling/captation and **no heat grid** available, an Air-Water heat pump is the most viable sustainable alternative to the existing boiler.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

7.  **Install Solar Panels (`place PV-panels on roof`):**
    *   **Reasoning:** The building has "potential for PV" and a gable roof that is already "Well Insulated" (meaning no roof replacement is needed before installation). This will significantly lower operational costs and further improve the energy performance.
