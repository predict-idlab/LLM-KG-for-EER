Given the specifications of your home, you are starting from a very strong position. A house built in 2017 with a heat pump, Ventilation D, and photovoltaic panels is already significantly more efficient than the average Belgian housing stock. 

With an EPC score of 87, you are likely already in a high energy class, but to push that score lower and maximize operational efficiency, we must address the only "moderate" area of the building envelope: **the roof.**

Since the walls, floors, and heating systems are already optimized, the law of diminishing returns applies here. To remain **cost-effective**, you should avoid replacing components that are already functioning well (like your 2020 windows) and instead focus on "surgical" upgrades.

Here is the proposed renovation path, sequenced by impact and cost-effectiveness.

---

### Step 1: Thermal Upgrade of the Pitched Roof (High Priority)
The main pitched roof is your primary "energy leak" since it is rated as only "moderately insulated." Because heat rises, this is the most impactful area for improvement.

*   **The Action:** Increase the R-value (thermal resistance) of the pitched roof. 
*   **Method:** Depending on the roof construction, add an additional layer of high-performance insulation (such as PIR boards or mineral wool) in the attic space or between rafters.
*   **Why:** This is the most cost-effective way to lower your EPC score. Improving the roof from "moderate" to "well-insulated" will reduce the load on your heat pump, allowing it to operate at a lower, more efficient flow temperature.

### Step 2: Air-Tightness and Thermal Bridge Audit (Medium Priority)
In a house from 2017, the structure is generally sound, but "invisible" energy loss often occurs at junctions (thermal bridges) or through small air leaks.

*   **The Action:** Conduct a professional **Blower Door Test** or a **Thermographic (Infrared) Scan** during winter.
*   **Method:** Identify specific cold spots where the roof meets the walls or around window frames. Seal these gaps using professional-grade expanding foam or airtight tapes.
*   **Why:** This is a low-cost intervention. Fixing small drafts prevents the "chill" factor and ensures your Ventilation D system is working as intended rather than fighting uncontrolled air infiltration.

### Step 3: Optimization of the Heat Pump & Energy Management (Low Cost/High Efficiency)
Since your hardware (heat pump and Vent D) is already in good condition, the focus shifts from *replacement* to *optimization*.

*   **The Action:** Implement a smart energy management system.
*   **Method:** 
    *   Ensure your heat pump is integrated with a **smart thermostat** that utilizes weather compensation (adjusting temperature based on the outside forecast).
    *   Optimize the timing of your heat pump to align with the peak production of your photovoltaic panels (shifting the thermal load to midday).
*   **Why:** This requires almost no capital expenditure but reduces your reliance on the grid and lowers operational costs.

### Step 4: Window Performance "Fine-Tuning" (Long-Term/Low Priority)
Your windows were renewed in 2020 and are in good condition. Replacing double glazing with triple glazing now would **not** be cost-effective, as the ROI would be too long.

*   **The Action:** Enhance passive solar gain and heat retention.
*   **Method:** Install high-quality thermal curtains or cellular blinds in bedrooms and living areas.
*   **Why:** This provides an extra layer of insulation during winter nights for a fraction of the cost of new windows, further protecting the EPC score.
