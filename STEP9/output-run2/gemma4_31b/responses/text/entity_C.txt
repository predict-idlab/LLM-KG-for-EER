### Executive Analysis
Your home already possesses a very strong "shell" (well-insulated walls, floor, and updated glazing) and a high-end ventilation system (Type D). With an EPC of 50, you are already in a high-performance bracket. However, the **heating system is your primary weakness**. The reliance on electric convectors is particularly inefficient, as they are among the most expensive ways to heat a home.

Since you already have photovoltaic (PV) panels, the most cost-effective and energy-efficient path is **full electrification**.

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize the benefit of each Euro spent, the sequence must be: **Insulate $\rightarrow$ System Upgrade $\rightarrow$ Optimization.**

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Roof)
Before replacing the heating system, you must address the "moderate" insulation of the pitched roof. 
*   **Action:** Upgrade the pitched roof insulation to "well insulated" levels (matching the flat roof).
*   **Why:** The heating load of a house is determined by its weakest insulation point. By improving the roof, you reduce the total heat loss. This allows you to install a **smaller, cheaper, and more efficient heat pump** in Step 2.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Roof insulation is generally a low-cost investment with a high impact on the EPC score.

#### Step 2: HVAC System Overhaul (The Core Upgrade)
The combination of an old boiler and electric convectors is the biggest drain on your energy performance.
*   **Action A: Install an Air-to-Water Heat Pump.** Since geothermal and district heating are unavailable, an air-to-water system is the optimal choice. 
*   **Action B: Eliminate Electric Convectors.** Replace these entirely. Since you already have underfloor heating (UFH), extend the UFH loops into the rooms currently served by convectors, or install low-temperature radiators compatible with heat pumps.
*   **Action C: Refurbish/Replace UFH Manifolds.** Since the UFH is in poor condition, ensure the piping and manifolds are flushed and modernized to ensure optimal heat distribution.
*   **Why:** Heat pumps are significantly more efficient than boilers. By pairing them with UFH (low-temperature heating), you maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP), meaning you get more heat per kWh of electricity.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Medium-High. While the upfront cost is higher, the operational cost drops drastically, especially since you produce your own electricity via PV panels.

#### Step 3: System Integration & Smart Control
Now that the hardware is efficient, you must optimize the timing of energy use.
*   **Action:** Install a **Smart Energy Management System (HEMS)** and smart thermostats.
*   **Why:** You have PV panels. The goal is to "shift" your heating demand to the daytime hours when your panels are producing electricity (thermal buffering), rather than drawing from the grid at night.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. This is a low-cost software/sensor upgrade that maximizes the ROI of your existing PV installation.
