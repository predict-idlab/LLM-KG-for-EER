To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1832 attached building, we must follow the "fabric-first" approach: first reducing heat loss through the building envelope, then upgrading the heating and ventilation systems, and finally adding renewable energy generation.

Based on the building's current state (EPC 262) and the owner's motivation to improve the EPC label, here is the proposed renovation path.

### Phase 1: The Building Envelope (Thermal Insulation)
*Prioritizing the "Not Insulated" elements to reduce the heating load before upgrading the boiler.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (RoofExtension):** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority area for heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls (Wall_Front & Wall_Rear):** Both the front and rear full-brick walls are not insulated. Since there is no historical protection and the building is attached (reducing the total exterior wall surface), **exterior insulation** is the most effective way to prevent thermal bridging and significantly lower the EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. This is a cost-effective upgrade that prevents cold drafts from the basement.

*Note: The Gable Roof is already "Moderately Insulated" and in "Good State," so replacing or further insulating it is not a priority compared to the walls and flat roof.*

### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
*Upgrading ventilation before the heating system to ensure the new heat pump operates in a controlled environment.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses **Ventilation System A** (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient. Upgrading to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) will reduce uncontrolled heat loss through air leakage.

### Phase 3: Heating System Transition
*Now that the house is insulated, we can install a smaller, more efficient heating system.*

5.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission:** The current radiators are designed for high-temperature boilers. To maximize the efficiency of a heat pump, the emission system should be transitioned to **low-temperature** (e.g., larger radiators or underfloor heating).
6.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** The owner has a reason to change the generation system, and the site lacks potential for geothermal drilling. An **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
*Offsetting the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the electricity needed for the heat pump and ventilation system, lowering long-term operational costs and further improving the EPC label.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Envelope** | Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Exterior Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor | Stops heat leakage; reduces the required capacity of the new heating system. |
| **2. Air** | Replace Ventilation System | Prevents heat loss from air exchange. |
| **3. Heating** | Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump | Moves away from fossil fuels to a high-efficiency electric system. |
| **4. Energy** | PV-Panels on Roof | Powers the new electric systems with sustainable energy. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** I have excluded the replacement of windows. Since the existing windows already have **double glazing** and the frames are in **good state**, the return on investment for replacing them would be significantly lower than insulating the uninsulated brick walls and flat roof.
