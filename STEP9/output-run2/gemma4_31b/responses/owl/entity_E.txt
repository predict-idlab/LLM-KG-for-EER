To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1862 detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. The goal is to reduce the building's energy demand (insulation and glazing) before upgrading the technical systems (heating and ventilation), ensuring that the new systems are sized correctly and operate at peak efficiency.

### Analysis of Current State
*   **Strengths:** The roof is already well-insulated; window frames are wooden and in good condition (State Value: 8).
*   **Weaknesses:** All four exterior walls are uninsulated cavity walls; all windows have energy-inefficient single glazing; the floor above the crawl space is uninsulated; the heating system is outdated (owner desires a change); and the ventilation is the most basic type (System A).
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal energy and heat grids are not viable for this site.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The "Thermos" Effect)
*Focus: Reducing heat loss using the most cost-effective methods available for this specific building.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** Since the building has cavity walls that are currently not insulated, this is the highest priority. It is significantly cheaper (€22/m²) than interior or exterior insulation and provides an immediate boost to the EPC.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The building currently has single glazing. Because the wooden frames are in good state, we will **replace only the glazing** rather than the entire window. This avoids the high cost of new frames (€800/m²) while achieving similar thermal gains for a fraction of the price (€200/m²).
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** To eliminate cold drafts from the crawl space, we will insulate the floor. This is a low-cost intervention (€30/m²) that significantly improves ground-floor thermal comfort.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & Efficiency
*Focus: Optimizing how air moves through the home to prevent heat loss via uncontrolled ventilation.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) will ensure a healthier indoor climate and reduce the energy wasted by uncontrolled air exchange.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Energy Generation & Distribution
*Focus: Switching to high-efficiency, low-carbon heating and power.*

5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** With the "fabric" now efficient, the building can be heated with a low-temperature system. Since geothermal is not possible, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most effective alternative.
6.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission System:** To maximize the efficiency of the heat pump, the existing radiators/convectors should be transitioned to a low-temperature emission system.
7.  **Install PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump, lowering long-term operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic | Cost Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Wall Cavity** | Highest ROI; lowest cost per m². | Very Low |
| **2** | **Replace Glazing** | Eliminates major heat leaks; leverages existing good frames. | Moderate |
| **3** | **Insulate Floor** | Prevents cold floor effect; low cost. | Low |
| **4** | **Upgrade Ventilation** | Prepares the house for a tighter thermal envelope. | Moderate |
| **5** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Shifts to sustainable heating after demand is lowered. | High |
| **6** | **Low-Temp Emission** | Ensures the heat pump operates at maximum COP. | Moderate |
| **7** | **Roof PV-Panels** | Provides "free" energy to power the new systems. | Moderate |

**Final Result:** This path transforms a 19th-century building into a modern, energy-efficient home by prioritizing the cheapest, most impactful upgrades first, ensuring the owner achieves their goal of improving the EPC label while minimizing unnecessary expenditure.
