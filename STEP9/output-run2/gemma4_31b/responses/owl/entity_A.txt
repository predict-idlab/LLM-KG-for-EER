To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, we must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reduce the energy demand of the building envelope before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems. 

The building currently has an EPC score of **388 (D Label)**. To significantly improve this and meet the owner's motivation, I propose the following phased renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
The building's biggest weakness is the lack of insulation in the walls, main roof, and floor. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system can be smaller and more efficient.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the most cost-effective "quick win" as heat rises.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** All three existing walls (Front, Rear, Right) are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is preferred over interior insulation to prevent mold and maximize usable interior space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is not insulated; adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement.

**Why this sequence?** By insulating the shell first, we lower the "U-value" of the home, which directly reduces the energy required to keep the house warm.

### Phase 2: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is insulated, it becomes more airtight. To prevent condensation and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation system must be upgraded.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current "System A" (natural supply/exhaust) is insufficient for a highly insulated home. Upgrading to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) is essential to avoid moisture buildup.

### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition
With a tightened envelope, the building can now transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency heat pump.

5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal drilling and no access to a heat grid, an air-water heat pump is the most viable and sustainable alternative to the existing boiler.
6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of the new heat pump, the existing radiators/floor heating should be optimized for low-temperature operation.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Installing solar panels will lower operational costs and further improve the EPC label.

### Final Expert Note on Cost-Effectiveness:
By following this sequence, you avoid the common mistake of installing a large, expensive heating system for a "leaky" house. By insulating first, you can install a **smaller, cheaper heat pump** that provides the same level of comfort while using significantly less electricity.
