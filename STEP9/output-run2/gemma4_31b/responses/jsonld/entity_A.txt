To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective for this 1951 semi-detached building (EPC score: 388), the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. This means we prioritize reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading the systems that generate heat.

Based on the provided ontology and building data, here is the optimal renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Demand Reduction)
*The goal here is to stop heat loss. These are the most cost-effective upgrades with the highest immediate impact on the EPC label.*

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the highest priority to prevent thermal leakage.
2.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** The front, rear, and right walls are full brick and not insulated. Cavity wall insulation is significantly more cost-effective ($\approx 22/m^2$) and less disruptive than exterior insulation.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. This prevents "cold feet" and reduces the load on the heating system.
4.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is only moderately insulated; adding further insulation here will bring the entire building envelope up to a consistent standard.

### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Once the house is sealed with insulation, proper ventilation is required to prevent condensation and maintain air quality.*

5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural supply/exhaust). Upgrading to a more modern system (such as System D with heat recovery) will prevent the newly insulated home from losing heat through ventilation.

### Phase 3: Energy Generation & Emission (System Upgrade)
*With a reduced energy demand, we can now install a smaller, more efficient heating system. Geothermal options were ruled out based on the site data.*

6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is a reason to change the emission system and no heat grid is available, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient replacement for the existing boiler, especially as the building already has radiators and floor heating.
7.  **Place Heat Pump Boiler:** To further reduce reliance on the main heating system for domestic hot water, a dedicated heat pump boiler should be installed.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Operational Cost Reduction)
*The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pumps.*

8.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has high potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the electricity needed to run the air-water heat pump and the heat pump boiler, drastically lowering long-term operational costs.

**Why skip window replacement?**
The current windows are double-glazed and the wooden frames are in a "Good State." Replacing them with triple glazing is very expensive ($\approx 800/m^2$) and provides diminishing returns compared to the massive gains found in insulating the non-insulated roof, walls, and floor.
