To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century detached building, the renovation should follow the "fabric-first" principle: reduce energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems and adding renewable energy.

### Analysis of Current State
*   **The Envelope:** The roof is already well-insulated, but all four walls (cavity walls) are not insulated. Windows are in good structural condition (wooden frames) but utilize inefficient single glazing.
*   **Systems:** The heating system is fragmented (electric convectors, radiators, air heating) and the owner has a stated reason to change the emission system. 
*   **Constraints:** There is no access to a heat grid, and the site has no potential for geothermal drilling (vertical or horizontal). However, there is high potential for solar PV.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Demand Reduction)
*Goal: Stop heat loss to allow for a smaller, more efficient heating system.*
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** Since all walls are cavity walls and currently not insulated, **insulate wall cavity** is the highest priority. This is significantly more cost-effective and less disruptive than exterior insulation.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The window frames are in "Good State," so replacing the entire window is unnecessary. **Replace glazing** (upgrading from single to double or triple glazing) will drastically reduce thermal loss and improve comfort.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** To eliminate drafts and heat loss from the crawl space, **insulate floor above basement**.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission Upgrade (Efficiency)
*Goal: Replace the outdated boiler and fragmented emitters with a modern, low-carbon system.*
1.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the building needs emitters (like underfloor heating or oversized radiators) that work at lower temperatures to maximize the heat pump's Coefficient of Performance (COP).
2.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an **air-water heat pump** is the most efficient and practical choice for a detached building.
3.  **Place Heat Pump Boiler:** To optimize domestic hot water production, install a dedicated **heat pump boiler**.

#### Phase 3: Energy Generation & Air Quality (Sustainability)
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ensure a healthy indoor climate.*
1.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** With the building's confirmed PV potential, installing solar panels will lower the operational costs of the heat pump and improve the EPC score.
2.  **Replace Ventilation System:** After sealing the envelope (walls, floor, and glazing), the building will be more airtight. **Replace ventilation system** (upgrading from System A) to ensure proper air exchange and prevent humidity issues.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Insulation $\rightarrow$ Emission Systems $\rightarrow$ Heat Pump $\rightarrow$ Renewables $\rightarrow$ Ventilation**

| Step | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Cavity Wall + Floor Insulation | Lowest cost/highest impact on energy demand. |
| **2** | Glazing Upgrade | Removes major thermal bridges without replacing frames. |
| **3** | Low-Temp Emitters | Necessary prerequisite for heat pump efficiency. |
| **4** | Air-Water Heat Pump | Shifts from fossil/electric heating to high-efficiency electric. |
| **5** | PV Panels | Offsets the increased electricity load from the heat pump. |
| **6** | Ventilation Upgrade | Maintains air quality in a now-insulated building. |
