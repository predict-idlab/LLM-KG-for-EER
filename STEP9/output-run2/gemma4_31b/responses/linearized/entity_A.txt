To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, we must follow the **"Envelope First"** principle. By reducing heat loss first, we can install a smaller, more efficient heating system, thereby reducing both capital expenditure and long-term operational costs.

### **Analysis of Current State**
*   **EPC Score:** 388 (Label D), indicating significant room for improvement.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The main gable roof, all exterior walls (Full Brick), and the floor above the basement are **not insulated**.
*   **Systems:** The building relies on a boiler and a basic Ventilation System A. While the windows are double-glazed, the heating emission systems are outdated and marked for replacement.
*   **Constraints:** There is no potential for geothermal energy or connection to a heat grid. However, there is high potential for PV panels.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact, High Priority)**
Before upgrading the heating system, we must stop the heat leakage.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the most cost-effective way to lower the EPC score quickly.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all uninsulated full brick. Exterior insulation is preferred over interior to prevent mold and maximize usable living space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** To eliminate cold drafts from below, the `FloorAboveBasement` should be insulated.

#### **Phase 2: HVAC System Modernization (Efficiency Upgrade)**
Once the building is airtight and insulated, the heating demand will drop, allowing for a low-temperature system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since there is a stated reason to change the emission system, upgrading to low-temperature emitters (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators) is essential to make a heat pump viable.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With geothermal and heat grid options unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient alternative to the existing boiler.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (e.g., System B or D) will ensure air quality is maintained in the now better-insulated home and reduce heat loss through uncontrolled ventilation.

#### **Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Operational Cost Reduction)**
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing these last allows the owner to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump, bringing the building closer to a net-zero energy balance.

---

### **Summary of Sequence & Logic**

| Step | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Reduces the "heating load," ensuring the subsequent heat pump is not oversized. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission System** | Prerequisite for the efficiency of a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the fossil-fuel boiler with a high-efficiency electric alternative. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents humidity issues and optimizes air exchange. |
| **5** | **PV-Panels on Roof** | Generates free electricity to power the heat pump and home. |

**Estimated Result:** This path will significantly lower the energy score from 388, drastically reduce yearly energy bills, and align with the owner's motivation to improve the EPC label.
