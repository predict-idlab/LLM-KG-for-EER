Given the information about your house in Ghent, Belgium, here’s a step-by-step, cost-effective renovation plan to improve energy efficiency and reduce energy consumption. The plan prioritizes upgrades that offer the best return on investment and long-term savings, while considering the optimal sequence of renovations.

### Step-by-Step Renovation Plan:

1. **Insulate the Flat-Roof Extension**
   - The flat-roof extension is currently uninsulated, which is a significant source of heat loss. Insulating this area first will provide immediate energy savings.
   - Use high-performance insulation materials like rigid foam boards or mineral wool to ensure durability and effectiveness.
   - Cost-effective and relatively straightforward to implement, especially if the roof is already in good condition.

2. **Upgrade the Heating System**
   - Replace the old boiler and radiators with a modern, high-efficiency condensing boiler or consider a heat pump (if feasible) paired with low-temperature underfloor heating or modern radiators.
   - A heat pump might be a good option if the house can accommodate it, but a condensing boiler is a more straightforward replacement.
   - Ensure the new system is properly sized for the house to avoid over- or under-heating.

3. **Insulate the Solid Brick Walls (Front and Rear)**
   - External wall insulation (EWI) is ideal for solid brick walls but can be costly. Internal wall insulation (IWI) is a more cost-effective alternative, though it may reduce floor space slightly.
   - If budget allows, EWI is preferable as it avoids reducing internal space and provides better thermal performance.
   - Consider using high-performance insulation materials like mineral wool or rigid foam boards with a good vapor barrier to prevent moisture issues.

4. **Insulate the Floor Above the Basement**
   - Insulating the floor above the basement will help reduce heat loss through the ground.
   - Use rigid insulation boards or mineral wool insulation, depending on the construction of the floor.
   - This is a relatively simple and cost-effective upgrade that can be done alongside other renovations.

5. **Improve Ventilation System**
   - Upgrade the basic natural ventilation system to a mechanical ventilation system with heat recovery (MVHR). This will improve indoor air quality while reducing heat loss.
   - MVHR systems are particularly effective in older homes where air leakage is a concern.

6. **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels**
   - Given the potential for PV panels, install them on the roof to generate renewable energy.
   - This will reduce electricity costs and contribute to a better energy performance certificate (EPC).
   - Consider battery storage if budget allows to maximize self-consumption of generated electricity.

7. **Review and Upgrade Windows (If Necessary)**
   - The current double-glazed windows with wooden frames are in good condition, so replacing them may not be immediately necessary.
   - However, if drafts or poor insulation are noticed, consider secondary glazing or upgrading to triple glazing for better thermal performance.

### Optimal Sequence:
1. Insulate the flat-roof extension.
2. Upgrade the heating system (boiler or heat pump).
3. Insulate the solid brick walls (EWI or IWI).
4. Insulate the floor above the basement.
5. Improve ventilation system (MVHR).
6. Install photovoltaic panels.
7. Review and upgrade windows if needed.

### Additional Tips:
- **Prioritize Air Sealing**: Ensure that any insulation work includes proper air sealing to prevent drafts and improve overall efficiency.
- **Government Incentives**: Check for available grants or subsidies in Belgium for energy-efficient renovations, as these can significantly reduce costs.
- **Professional Advice**: Consult with a local energy expert or architect to ensure the proposed upgrades are feasible and optimized for your specific home.

By following this plan, you should see a significant improvement in your home’s energy performance certificate and long-term energy savings.
