To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC label for this 19th-century attached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. This approach ensures that we reduce the energy demand of the building before installing new heating technology, preventing the over-sizing of equipment and maximizing long-term cost-effectiveness.

### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Demand Reduction)**
Before upgrading the heating system, we must address the significant heat loss through the uninsulated elements.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority "quick win" to stop heat from escaping through the top of the building.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. Insulating this area provides a significant boost to thermal comfort and prevents cold drafts from the basement.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both the front and rear full-brick walls are not insulated. Given that there is no historical protection, external wall insulation is the most effective way to reduce the overall energy score of the building.
    *   *Note:* We skip the "insulate wall cavity" suggestion because the building consists of full-brick walls (no cavity).

### **Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation**
Increasing insulation increases the airtightness of the home, which can lead to condensation and air quality issues if not managed.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade the current Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor climate and prevents moisture buildup after the walls and roof are sealed.

### **Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition**
With the heat demand now significantly reduced, we can transition from the old boiler to a high-efficiency electric system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the building currently uses radiators, they should be converted to low-temperature emitters. This is a critical prerequisite for the efficiency of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Because the site has no potential for geothermal drilling or a heat grid, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient choice to replace the existing generation system.

### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration**
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has been identified as having potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower operational costs and significantly improve the final EPC label.

---

### **Summary of the Renovation Path**

| Sequence | Action | Target Element | Priority | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `insulate flat roof` | Roof Extension | High | Stops immediate heat loss. |
| **2** | `insulate floor above basement` | Floor | High | Improves thermal comfort. |
| **3** | `insulate wall on exterior` | Front/Rear Walls | High | Major reduction in energy demand. |
| **4** | `replace ventilation system` | Ventilation | Medium | Prevents condensation post-insulation. |
| **5** | `place emission system on low temperature`| Radiators | Medium | Prepares for heat pump efficiency. |
| **6** | `place air-water heatpump` | Generation System | Medium | Sustainable heating source. |
| **7** | `place PV-panels on roof` | Roof | Low | Offsets energy costs. |

**Why this path?**
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** We avoid replacing windows because they are already double-glazed and the frames are in good state.
*   **Energy Efficiency:** By insulating first, the new heat pump can be smaller and run at lower temperatures, drastically reducing electricity bills.
*   **Practicality:** We only suggest the Air-Water heat pump because the ontology explicitly states that geothermal and heat-grid options are unavailable for this site.
