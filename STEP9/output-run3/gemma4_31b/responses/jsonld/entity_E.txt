To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC label while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: first reduce energy demand by improving the building envelope, and then upgrade the technical systems to meet that reduced demand.

Based on the building data and the RENO ontology, here is the proposed renovation path.

### Phase 1: Building Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
*This phase focuses on the most cost-effective upgrades with the highest impact on the EPC score.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** The building has four cavity walls that are currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as "insulate wall cavity" has a very low unit price (€22/m²) and low nuisance level.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is an area of heat loss. Insulating this floor is a low-cost (€30/m²) and low-nuisance intervention.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** The bedrooms and living area currently have single glazing, which is thermally inefficient. Since the wooden window frames are in "Good State," it is more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (e.g., to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.

### Phase 2: Technical System Upgrades (Efficient Energy Use)
*Now that the building is better insulated, the heating system can be downsized and optimized.*

4.  **Install an Air-Water Heat Pump:** The current boiler is outdated, and there is a stated reason to change the emission system. Since geothermal options are not possible for this site and there is no heat grid, an **air-water heat pump** is the optimal choice. This is compatible with the existing radiators and floor heating.
5.  **Low-Temperature Emission Adjustment:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of the new heat pump, the emission system should be adjusted to operate at a **low temperature**.
6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** To prevent moisture buildup in a more airtight, insulated home and to improve air quality, the current Ventilation System A should be replaced with a more efficient system (e.g., System D).

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Reducing Operational Costs)
*The final step is to transition from energy efficiency to energy production.*

7.  **Install PV-Panels on Roof:** The building is a detached house with confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will significantly lower the electricity costs required to run the new heat pump and further improve the EPC rating.
