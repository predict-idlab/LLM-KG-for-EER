To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. By prioritizing the building envelope before upgrading technical systems, we ensure that the new heating systems are sized correctly and operate at peak efficiency.

The building currently has an EPC score of **388 (D label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to improve this EPC value.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The Priority)
The goal here is to stop heat loss. The building has several "not insulated" elements that are the primary cause of the poor EPC score.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is not insulated. This is the most critical step as heat rises.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** All three exterior walls (Front, Rear, Right) are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is more effective than interior insulation and avoids reducing living space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated, leading to significant cold drafts and heat loss to the basement.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
Once the house is "sealed" with insulation, mechanical ventilation becomes necessary to prevent condensation and maintain air quality.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from System A (natural) to a more modern system (like System D) will significantly improve energy recovery and the EPC score.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition
With a well-insulated envelope, the energy demand is lowered, allowing for the installation of a low-temperature heating system.
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing the heat pump, the existing radiators/heating should be optimized for low-temperature operation to maximize the COP (Coefficient of Performance) of the heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal options are not possible at this site and there is no heat grid, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective high-efficiency alternative to the current boiler.
7.  **Place Heat Pump Boiler:** To handle domestic hot water efficiently and separately from space heating.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
8.  **Place PV-panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing these on the gable roof will provide the electricity needed for the heat pump, drastically reducing operational costs and further improving the EPC label.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale | Cost/Complexity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof & Wall Insulation** | Highest impact on EPC; prevents heat loss. | High / High Nuisance |
| **2** | **Floor Insulation** | Eliminates cold floor effect; improves comfort. | Moderate / Low Nuisance |
| **3** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents humidity issues caused by better insulation. | Moderate / Moderate Nuisance |
| **4** | **Low-Temp Emission** | Prepares the house for the heat pump. | Moderate / Moderate Nuisance |
| **5** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches from fossil fuels to electricity. | High / Moderate Nuisance |
| **6** | **PV-Panels** | Generates "free" energy for the new systems. | Moderate / Low Nuisance |

### Final Expert Note on Cost-Effectiveness:
By following this sequence, the owner avoids the common mistake of installing a heat pump in an uninsulated house, which would require a much larger, more expensive unit that would struggle to keep the home warm. This path ensures the **lowest possible operational cost** and the **highest jump in EPC rating**.
