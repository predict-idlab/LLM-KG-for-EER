Based on the provided ontology and the current state of the building, here is the analysis and proposed renovation path.

### 1. Current State Analysis
The building is a modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017) with an exceptionally high energy performance (EPC score of 87). Most critical energy-efficiency components are already in place:
*   **Heating & Ventilation:** Already equipped with a Heat Pump and a Ventilation System D (the most efficient type).
*   **Insulation:** Walls and the floor above the basement are "Well Insulated." The flat roof extension is also "Well Insulated."
*   **Windows:** Currently feature double glazing with wooden frames in good condition.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

Given that the owner's motivation is to **further improve the EPC**, the focus must shift from "major overhauls" to "fine-tuning" the few remaining areas of thermal loss.

---

### 2. Proposed Renovation Path
Since the building is already highly efficient, the goal is to target the only elements not yet rated as "Well Insulated" or "Optimal."

#### **Step 1: Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** While the flat roof is well-insulated, the main gable roof is only **"Moderately Insulated."** This is the most significant remaining thermal gap in the building envelope.
*   **Action:** Execute `insulate gable roof`.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This is a high-impact upgrade with a "low" nuisance level and a relatively low unit price (60/m²).
*   **Ontology Ordering:** 28 (Priority: High).

#### **Step 2: Upgrade Window Glazing**
*   **Reasoning:** The building currently has double glazing. To further lower the EPC score, upgrading to triple glazing is the next logical step.
*   **Action:** Execute `replace glazing`.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Because the existing wooden window frames are in "Good State" (value 8), it is significantly more cost-effective to replace only the glazing rather than replacing the entire window unit.
*   **Ontology Ordering:** 43 (Priority: Medium).

#### **Step 3: Install Sunshades**
*   **Reasoning:** With high levels of insulation and double/triple glazing, the building may be prone to overheating in summer (the "greenhouse effect"). 
*   **Action:** Execute `place sunshade`.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This is a low-cost fixed price (750) intervention that reduces the need for active cooling, thereby lowering long-term operational energy consumption.
*   **Ontology Ordering:** 79 (Priority: Low/Final Touch).
