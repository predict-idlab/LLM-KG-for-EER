To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century detached building, the renovation should follow the "envelope-first" principle: first reducing heat loss, then upgrading the heating systems, and finally adding renewable energy sources.

### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact, Low-to-Medium Cost)**
The building's shell is the primary source of energy loss. Addressing the walls and windows first ensures that any new heating system is sized correctly and operates efficiently.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four walls (Front, Left, Right, Rear) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. 
    *   **Action:** `insulate wall cavity`.
    *   **Reasoning:** This is one of the most cost-effective upgrades with low nuisance levels, significantly reducing heat loss through the building's perimeter.
2.  **Upgrade Window Glazing:** The living area and bedrooms currently have **Single Glazing**, which is thermally inefficient. However, the wooden frames are in **Good State (Value 8)**.
    *   **Action:** `replace glazing`.
    *   **Reasoning:** Since the frames are in good condition, replacing only the glass (e.g., to double or triple glazing) is far more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit (`replace windows`).
3.  **Insulate Floor:** The building has a **Floor Above Basement/Crawl Space**.
    *   **Action:** `insulate floor above basement`.
    *   **Reasoning:** This prevents cold air from entering the living spaces from below, improving thermal comfort and reducing the heating load.

### **Phase 2: System Upgrades (Operational Efficiency)**
Once the heat loss is minimized, the outdated heating and ventilation systems should be replaced to lower the energy score (EPC).

4.  **Upgrade Heating System:** The building has a reason to change its emission system, and there is no access to a heat grid or geothermal potential.
    *   **Action:** `place air-water heatpump`.
    *   **Reasoning:** An air-water heat pump is the most viable high-efficiency alternative to the current boiler, especially given the existing radiator/floor heating infrastructure.
5.  **Upgrade Ventilation:** The building currently uses **Ventilation System A** (natural supply/exhaust), which often leads to significant heat loss.
    *   **Action:** `replace ventilation system`.
    *   **Reasoning:** Moving toward a more controlled system (like System D) will improve air quality and recover heat that would otherwise be lost.

### **Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Long-term Cost Reduction)**
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

6.  **Install Solar Energy:** The building is confirmed to have **PV potential**.
    *   **Action:** `place PV-panels on roof`.
    *   **Reasoning:** Generating electricity on-site will drastically reduce long-term operational costs and further improve the EPC label, aligning with the owner's motivation.

### **Summary of Optimal Sequence**
**Wall Cavity Insulation $\rightarrow$ Glazing Replacement $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump $\rightarrow$ Ventilation Upgrade $\rightarrow$ PV Panels.**
