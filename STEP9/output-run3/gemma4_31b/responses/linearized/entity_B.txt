To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation should follow a **"Fabric First"** approach. By reducing the building's energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems, we can install a smaller, more efficient heat pump and lower long-term operational costs.

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Demand Reduction)**
The building currently has several critical areas with no insulation. Addressing these first prevents heat loss and improves the EPC score most effectively.
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises and flat roofs are significant sources of thermal loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls:** Both the `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` (Full Brick Walls) are not insulated. 
    *   *Recommendation:* Prioritize **insulate wall cavity** (if applicable) or **insulate wall on exterior** to create a continuous thermal wrap.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. This is a cost-effective way to eliminate cold floors and reduce heating demand.

#### **Phase 2: Ventilation and Distribution (System Readiness)**
Before replacing the boiler, the building's internal air and heat distribution must be optimized.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses `VentilationSystemA` (natural). Upgrading to a more controlled system (e.g., System B or C) will reduce uncontrolled air infiltration and improve indoor air quality.
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the building currently uses radiators, these should be transitioned to low-temperature emitters. This is a critical prerequisite for the efficiency of the heat pump proposed in the next phase.

#### **Phase 3: Sustainable Energy Generation (Supply Upgrade)**
With a tightened thermal envelope and low-temperature emitters, the heating source can now be modernized.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Because the site has no potential for geothermal drilling and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and cost-effective sustainable alternative to the current boiler.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump, significantly reducing operational costs.

---

### **Summary of Sequence & Logic**

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Roof & Walls** | Reduces overall heat loss; allows for a smaller heat pump. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor** | Improves thermal comfort and prevents basement heat loss. |
| **3** | **Upgrade Ventilation** | Ensures heat is retained and air quality is maintained. |
| **4** | **Low-Temp Emitters** | Necessary to make the heat pump operate at peak efficiency (COP). |
| **5** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the fossil-fuel boiler with a sustainable source. |
| **6** | **PV-Panels** | Provides "free" energy to power the electrical systems. |

### **Cost-Effectiveness Notes**
*   **Avoided Costs:** We are skipping the replacement of windows and glazing because the existing double glazing and wooden frames are in a "Good State," providing a high return on investment by focusing funds on the uninsulated walls and floors instead.
*   **Operational Performance:** By sequencing the low-temperature emitters before the heat pump, we avoid the common mistake of installing a heat pump with high-temperature radiators, which often leads to inefficiency and higher energy bills.
