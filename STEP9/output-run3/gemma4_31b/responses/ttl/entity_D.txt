### Analysis of Building State
The building is a **semi-detached house** constructed in **2017**, which is relatively modern. It already exhibits high energy performance with an **EPC score of 87.0 (Label A)**. 

**Current Strengths:**
*   **Walls & Floors:** The front, left, and rear walls, as well as the floor above the basement, are already **well insulated**.
*   **Roofing:** The flat roof extension is **well insulated**.
*   **HVAC:** The building is equipped with a **Heat Pump** and a **Ventilation System D** (the most efficient mechanical supply/exhaust system), which significantly reduces the need for HVAC replacements.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

**Identified Weaknesses:**
*   **Main Roof:** The gable roof is only **moderately insulated**, representing the primary thermal bridge in the building envelope.
*   **Glazing:** While the building has double glazing, there is room for improvement to triple glazing to further lower the energy score.

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Since the building is already highly efficient, the goal is to target the remaining "low-hanging fruit" to move the EPC score deeper into the A/A+ range while avoiding redundant costs.

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
**Action: Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** This is the only major building element not currently "well insulated." Heat rises, and improving the gable roof from "moderately" to "well" insulated is the most cost-effective way to reduce energy consumption.
*   **Implementation Detail:** According to the ontology, this has a **low nuisance level** and a unit price of **60/m²**. 
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: Glazing Upgrade (Medium Priority)
**Action: Replace Glazing (Double $\rightarrow$ Triple)**
*   **Reasoning:** To maximize energy efficiency, upgrading from double to triple glazing reduces heat loss through the windows. Since the wooden frames are in "Good State" (Value 8), replacing only the glazing is more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.
*   **Implementation Detail:** This has a higher unit price (**200/m²**) and a **high nuisance level**, but provides a long-term operational performance boost.
*   **Point of Attention:** Verify that the existing wooden frames are thick enough to support the additional weight and width of triple-pane glass.
