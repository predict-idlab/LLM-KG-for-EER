Given that your home was built in 2017 and already features a heat pump, System D ventilation, and photovoltaic panels, you are starting from a very strong baseline. In the Belgian (Flemish) context, a house of this vintage is already relatively efficient. 

The "moderate" insulation of the pitched roof is the most significant thermal weakness in your current profile. To improve your EPC score and reduce operational costs, you should focus on **marginal gains**—targeting the few remaining areas where heat is lost or energy is wasted.

Here is the proposed renovation path, sequenced by cost-effectiveness and impact.

---

### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Priority)
Since the walls, floor, and flat roof are already well-insulated, the **pitched roof** is your primary target. Heat rises, and "moderate" insulation is the most likely cause of energy leakage.

*   **Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the main pitched roof.
*   **Method:** Depending on the construction, add a layer of high-performance mineral wool or cellulose insulation in the attic space or via injection if it is a cavity.
*   **Goal:** Bring the roof insulation level up to the "well-insulated" standard of the rest of the house.
*   **Why first?** This is the most cost-effective way to lower your heat pump's workload and directly improve the EPC score.

### Step 2: Energy Management & Solar Optimization
You already have the hardware (PV panels and a heat pump). The next step is not adding more hardware, but optimizing the **interaction** between them to maximize self-consumption.

*   **Action:** Implement a Smart Energy Management System (HEMS).
*   **Method:** 
    *   Install a smart controller to synchronize your heat pump's operation with your PV production (shifting the heating load to peak sunlight hours).
    *   Evaluate the addition of a home battery system if your current self-consumption rate is low.
*   **Goal:** Reduce reliance on the grid and lower the operational cost of the heat pump.
*   **Why second?** It leverages existing investments to reduce monthly bills without requiring structural work.

### Step 3: Glazing & Solar Gain Control
Your windows were renewed in 2020 with double glazing. Replacing these with triple glazing now would **not** be cost-effective, as the ROI would be too low. Instead, focus on the *performance* of the existing glass.

*   **Action:** Install external solar shading (e.g., adjustable shutters or screens).
*   **Method:** Install high-quality external blinds or "screens" on south- and west-facing windows.
*   **Goal:** Prevent overheating during the summer. Since you have a heat pump, preventing heat gain is significantly cheaper than using energy to cool the house.
*   **Why third?** It improves comfort and prevents "energy spikes" during summer months.

### Step 4: Final "Fine-Tuning" Audit
Because the house is modern (2017), large-scale failures are unlikely, but small "thermal bridges" may exist.

*   **Action:** Perform a targeted thermographic scan (infrared camera).
*   **Method:** A professional scan during winter to identify specific cold spots (e.g., around window frames or roof junctions).
*   **Goal:** Identify and seal any remaining air leaks or insulation gaps.
*   **Why last?** This is a "polishing" step to ensure the house performs as designed.

---

### Summary of the Renovation Path

| Priority | Intervention | Focus Area | Impact on EPC | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof Insulation** | Pitched Roof | High | Very High |
| **2** | **Smart Management** | PV $\rightarrow$ Heat Pump | Low/Medium | High |
| **3** | **External Shading** | Windows | Medium (Summer) | Medium |
| **4** | **Thermal Audit** | Airtightness | Low | Medium |

**Expert Tip for Ghent/Flanders:** Check the current **Flemish Energy Premiums (Energiepremie)**. Even for newer homes, there are often subsidies available for improving insulation or installing smart energy management systems that can significantly lower your upfront costs.
