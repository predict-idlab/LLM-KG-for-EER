Given the specifics of your home in Ghent, you are in a very strong starting position. You already have a high-performing ventilation system (System D), recent glazing (2024), and photovoltaic panels. 

The primary "energy leaks" in your home are the **moderate insulation in the pitched roof** and the **inefficient heating emission systems** (specifically the electric convectors and the aging boiler). 

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, you must follow the "Insulate First, Install Later" principle. This ensures that your new heating system is sized correctly for a lower heat load, reducing both installation and operational costs.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Passive" Phase)
Before touching the heating system, address the weakest point of your building shell.
*   **Pitched Roof Insulation:** You mentioned the pitched roof has "moderate" insulation. Since heat rises, this is your primary area for heat loss. 
    *   **Action:** Increase the insulation thickness in the pitched roof. Depending on the current construction, this can be done via internal insulation (mineral wool/pir boards) or by adding a layer of insulation on top of the ceiling.
    *   **Goal:** Bring the pitched roof up to the "well insulated" standard of your flat roof and walls. This reduces the total kilowatt requirement for your future heating system.

#### Step 2: Heating System Overhaul (The "Active" Phase)
Now that the house is airtight and well-insulated, you can replace the outdated heating generation and emission systems.
*   **Replace Boiler with an Air-to-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and district heating are unavailable, an air-source heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient choice for the Ghent climate.
*   **Eliminate Electric Convectors:** Electric convectors are the most expensive way to heat a home. 
    *   **Action:** Remove all electric convectors.
*   **Overhaul Heat Emission:** Since the underfloor heating is in poor condition, this is the time to repair or replace it.
    *   **Action:** Ensure the underfloor heating is optimized for **low-temperature operation** (which is where heat pumps are most efficient). If certain rooms lack underfloor heating, install low-temperature radiators or extend the underfloor loop rather than returning to electric heating.

#### Step 3: System Integration & Optimization
Maximize the synergy between your existing assets and the new upgrades.
*   **PV Integration:** Your existing photovoltaic panels will now power the heat pump instead of just general electricity. This significantly lowers your operational costs (OPEX) and improves your EPC score.
*   **Ventilation D Synergy:** Your System D ventilation already recovers heat. Ensure that the heat pump is integrated with the ventilation system (if applicable) to maintain a constant, comfortable temperature with minimal energy input.
