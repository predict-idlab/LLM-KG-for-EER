Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached house from 1862 with a current EPC score of 165. While the roof is already well-insulated, the building envelope (walls, windows, and floor) and the heating systems are inefficient. 

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness while pursuing the owner's goal to **Improve EPC**, I propose the following renovation path. This plan follows the "Fabric First" principle: optimizing the building envelope to reduce energy demand before upgrading the technical installations.

### Phase 1: Building Envelope Optimization (Reducing Energy Demand)
The goal here is to stop heat loss. Since the wooden window frames are in good state, we focus on the glazing and the walls.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity**: All four walls are cavity walls and currently not insulated. Cavity wall insulation is one of the most cost-effective upgrades with a high return on investment.
2.  **Replace Glazing**: The windows currently have single glazing, which is a major source of heat loss. Since the frames are in "Good State" (value 8), replacing only the glazing (e.g., to double or triple glazing) is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement**: The building has a floor above a crawl space. Insulating this area prevents cold drafts and heat loss into the ground.

### Phase 2: Heating and Ventilation System Upgrade (Operational Efficiency)
Once the house is airtight and insulated, the heating system can be downsized and modernized for maximum efficiency.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**: To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the existing emission system (currently a mix of radiators and electric convectors) should be transitioned to low-temperature emitters.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**: Since there is no heat grid and geothermal drilling is not possible at this site, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient alternative to the current boiler and electric heaters.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System**: Improving the envelope often requires a better ventilation strategy to maintain air quality and prevent moisture buildup. Upgrading the current System A to a more modern system (B or C) is recommended.

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Reducing Operational Costs)
The final step is to generate clean energy to power the new heat pump and electrical systems.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof**: The building is confirmed to have potential for PV. Installing solar panels on the roof will offset the electricity consumption of the heat pump, significantly lowering long-term operational costs and further improving the EPC score.

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence
**Wall Cavity Insulation $\rightarrow$ Glazing Replacement $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation $\rightarrow$ Low-Temp Emission Upgrade $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump $\rightarrow$ Ventilation Upgrade $\rightarrow$ PV-Panel Installation.**

This sequence ensures that the heat pump is not oversized (because the house is already insulated) and that the electricity generated by the PV panels is used by a highly efficient system.
