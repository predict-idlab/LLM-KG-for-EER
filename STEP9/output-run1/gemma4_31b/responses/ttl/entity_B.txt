To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation should follow the "fabric first" approach. This means we prioritize reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems and adding renewable energy.

Based on the building's current state (EPC score of 262.0) and the constraints provided, here is the proposed renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Reducing Energy Loss)
*The goal is to seal the building to ensure that any new heating system operates at maximum efficiency.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Roof Extension):** The flat roof is currently **Not Insulated**. This is a high-priority area for heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This element is **Not Insulated**. This is typically a cost-effective upgrade with low nuisance that significantly reduces drafts from below.
3.  **Insulate Walls (Exterior):** Both the front and rear walls are **Full Brick and Not Insulated**. Since there is no historical protection, exterior insulation is the most effective way to improve the EPC and thermal comfort, though it is more expensive than interior insulation.
    *   *Note:* If budget is a primary constraint, **Insulate Wall on Interior** is a viable alternative, though it reduces usable floor space.
4.  **Gable Roof:** Since the main roof is already **Moderately Insulated** and in good state, further insulation here is a lower priority than the flat roof and walls.

### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
*Improving the envelope increases airtightness, making a mechanical ventilation upgrade necessary to prevent moisture buildup and ensure air quality.*

5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses **Ventilation System A** (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient. Upgrading to a System C or D will reduce heat loss through uncontrolled air leakage.

### Phase 3: Heating System Modernization
*With the building now insulated, the heating load is significantly reduced, allowing for the installation of a smaller, more efficient system.*

6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The current system uses radiators. To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the emission system should be transitioned to low-temperature (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators).
7.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has **no potential for geothermal captation** and **no heat grid available**, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient sustainable heating choice.
8.  **Place Heat Pump Boiler:** To further decouple domestic hot water from the main space heating and improve efficiency, a dedicated heat pump boiler is recommended.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Offsetting Consumption)
*The final step is to generate clean energy to power the new heat pump and electrical systems.*

9.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building is explicitly marked as having **potential for PV**. Installing solar panels will directly lower the energy score (EPC) and reduce long-term operational costs.

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence
| Order | Action | Target Element | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Flat Roof** | Roof Extension | High heat loss; currently not insulated. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor** | Floor Above Basement | Low cost/nuisance; high impact on comfort. |
| **3** | **Insulate Walls** | Front & Rear Walls | Critical for EPC improvement; no historical restrictions. |
