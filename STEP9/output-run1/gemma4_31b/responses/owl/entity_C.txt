To propose a renovation path for this building, we must first recognize that the property is very modern (constructed in 2023) and already possesses a high energy efficiency rating (EPC score of 50, which corresponds to an **A label**). Most building elements—walls, floors, and the flat roof—are already "Well Insulated."

However, the owner is motivated to further improve the EPC and there is an explicit reason to change the emission system. The current reliance on **Electric Convectors** is the primary inefficiency in an otherwise high-performing home.

Here is the optimized, cost-effective renovation plan.

### 1. Thermal Envelope Optimization (Fabric First)
Before upgrading heating systems, we must ensure the building envelope is as tight as possible to minimize the capacity required for the new heat pump.
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**.
*   **Reasoning:** While the flat roof is well-insulated, the main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated." Upgrading this to "Well Insulated" is the most logical first step to prevent heat loss through the top of the building.
*   **Sequence:** This must be done first to ensure the subsequent heat pump is sized correctly for the actual heat load of the house.

### 2. Emission & Generation System Upgrade
The building currently uses electric convectors, which are expensive to operate and inefficient. Since the building already has **Floor Heating**, it is perfectly primed for a low-temperature heating system.
*   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**.
*   **Reasoning:** The site data explicitly rules out geothermal options (both vertical and horizontal) and the heat grid. An air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient alternative to replace electric convectors, leveraging the existing floor heating for maximum COP (Coefficient of Performance).
*   **Action:** **Place Heat Pump Boiler**.
*   **Reasoning:** To maximize the EPC improvement, the domestic hot water system should be decoupled from the main heating or upgraded to a dedicated heat pump boiler, reducing the energy load on the primary system.

### 3. Operational Efficiency & Comfort
To prevent the "overheating" often associated with highly insulated modern homes (which would otherwise require energy-intensive air conditioning), we implement passive cooling.
*   **Action:** **Place Sunshade**.
*   **Reasoning:** This is a low-cost, low-nuisance intervention. By reducing solar gain during summer, the owner reduces the need for active cooling, further lowering the building's total energy consumption.

---

### Summary of the Renovation Path

| Sequence | Renovation Action | Target Element | Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Main Roof | $\downarrow$ Heat Loss | High (Low cost/High gain) |
| **2** | **Place Air-Water Heat Pump** | Generation System | $\downarrow\downarrow$ Energy Use | High (Replaces electrics) |
| **3** | **Place Heat Pump Boiler** | Water Heating | $\downarrow$ Energy Use | Moderate |
| **4** | **Place Sunshade** | Windows/Facade | $\downarrow$ Summer Cooling | High (Passive) |

**Final Result:** This path transforms the building from a "high-performing but inefficiently heated" home into a near-zero energy dwelling, directly satisfying the owner's motivation to improve the EPC while avoiding unnecessary and costly replacements of already efficient windows and walls.
