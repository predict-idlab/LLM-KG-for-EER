Given the information about your 1832 attached house in Ghent, Belgium, here’s a step-by-step, cost-effective renovation plan to improve energy efficiency while maximizing long-term performance. The plan prioritizes upgrades that offer the best return on investment and complement each other:

---

### **1. Insulate the Flat-Roof Extension**
   - **Why first?** Flat roofs are prone to heat loss, and insulating them is a straightforward, high-impact project.
   - **Action:** Install high-performance insulation (e.g., rigid foam boards or mineral wool) with an airtight vapor barrier. Consider adding a reflective roof coating to reduce heat gain in summer.
   - **Cost:** Moderate (depends on insulation type and thickness).
   - **Benefit:** Immediate reduction in heat loss through the roof.

---

### **2. Replace the Heating System and Radiators**
   - **Why next?** A new, efficient heating system will work better with improved insulation. Opt for a **condensing boiler** (if gas is still used) or a **heat pump** (if feasible).
   - **Action:**
     - Replace old radiators with low-temperature radiators or underfloor heating to work efficiently with a heat pump.
     - If using a boiler, ensure it’s a modern, high-efficiency model.
   - **Cost:** High (but essential for long-term savings).
   - **Benefit:** Reduced energy consumption and improved comfort.

---

### **3. Insulate the Solid Brick Walls (Front and Rear)**
   - **Why now?** Exterior wall insulation (EWI) is more disruptive but will significantly reduce heat loss. Since the house is not historically protected, this is feasible.
   - **Action:** Install **internal wall insulation (IWI)** or **external wall insulation (EWI)**. EWI is preferable if the facade can be altered, but IWI may be more practical for attached houses.
   - **Cost:** High (but necessary for long-term savings).
   - **Benefit:** Dramatic improvement in thermal performance.

---

### **4. Insulate the Floor Above the Basement**
   - **Why now?** Uninsulated floors contribute to heat loss. This is a relatively simple upgrade.
   - **Action:** Use rigid insulation boards or mineral wool between joists, ensuring an airtight seal.
   - **Cost:** Moderate.
   - **Benefit:** Reduces drafts and heat loss through the floor.

---

### **5. Upgrade Ventilation to Heat Recovery Ventilation (HRV)**
   - **Why now?** Natural ventilation is inefficient. HRV recovers heat from exhaust air, improving indoor air quality while reducing heat loss.
   - **Action:** Install a **compact HRV system** (e.g., a single-room unit or ductless system if space is limited).
   - **Cost:** Moderate.
   - **Benefit:** Better air quality and energy savings.

---

### **6. Improve Window Performance (If Needed)**
   - **Current status:** Double-glazed windows in good condition.
   - **Action:** If drafts are an issue, consider **secondary glazing** (cheaper than full replacement) or upgrading to **triple-glazed windows** when the existing ones need replacement.
   - **Cost:** Variable (secondary glazing is cheaper).
   - **Benefit:** Further reduces heat loss through windows.

---

### **7. Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels**
   - **Why last?** PV panels generate electricity but don’t directly reduce heat loss. Prioritize insulation and heating upgrades first.
   - **Action:** Assess roof suitability (orientation, shading) and install **high-efficiency PV panels** with a battery storage system if feasible.
   - **Cost:** High (but long-term energy bill savings and potential feed-in tariffs).
   - **Benefit:** Reduces reliance on grid electricity.

---

### **8. Optimize the Boiler or Heat Pump Performance**
   - **Action:** If using a boiler, install **smart thermostats** and **zone control** to optimize heating. For heat pumps, ensure proper sizing and commissioning.
   - **Cost:** Low to moderate.
   - **Benefit:** Maximizes efficiency of the new heating system.

---

### **Summary of Priorities (Optimal Sequence)**
1. Insulate the flat roof.
2. Replace the heating system and radiators.
3. Insulate the solid brick walls (EWI or IWI).
4. Insulate the floor above the basement.
5. Upgrade to HRV.
6. Improve windows if needed.
7. Install PV panels.
8. Optimize heating system controls.
