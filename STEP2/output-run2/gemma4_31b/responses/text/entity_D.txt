Given that your home was built in 2017 and already possesses high-end features (Heat Pump, Ventilation D, and PV panels), you are starting from a very strong baseline. An EPC score of 87 is already quite efficient for a house of this size in Belgium.

Because the "big wins" (heating system and wall insulation) are already addressed, your path to further efficiency shifts from **major structural overhauls** to **precision tuning and targeted insulation**.

Here is your concise, cost-effective renovation path, ordered by priority.

### Phase 1: High Impact / Low-to-Medium Cost (The "Quick Wins")
**Target: The Pitched Roof**
The pitched roof is the only area identified with "moderate" insulation. In a house where walls and floors are already well-insulated, the roof becomes the primary path for heat loss (convection).
*   **Action:** Upgrade the pitched roof insulation to "high" standards. Depending on the roof structure, this could mean adding a layer of mineral wool or PIR boards in the attic space or improving the existing ceiling insulation.
*   **Why:** This is the most cost-effective way to lower your EPC score further and reduce the load on your heat pump.
*   **Sequence:** Do this first. Improving the envelope always precedes system tuning.

### Phase 2: Operational Efficiency & Comfort (Low Cost)
**Target: Thermal Management & Air Tightness**
Since your windows were updated in 2020, replacing them with triple glazing is **not** cost-effective at this time. Instead, focus on the "edges."
*   **Action 1: Thermal Leak Audit.** Use a thermal camera (available for rent in Ghent/Flanders) to identify "cold bridges" or air leaks around window frames and door seals. Apply high-quality weather-stripping where necessary.
*   **Action 2: Solar Shading.** Since the house is well-insulated and has a heat pump, "overheating" in the summer is a higher risk than "cold" in the winter. Install external solar shading (external blinds or screens) on south- and west-facing windows.
*   **Why:** Reducing summer heat gain prevents the need for energy-intensive cooling, improving the overall annual energy balance.

### Phase 3: Energy Optimization (Medium Cost)
**Target: PV Maximization & System Tuning**
You already have PV panels and a heat pump; the goal now is to synchronize them to minimize grid reliance.
*   **Action 1: Heat Pump Optimization.** Have a certified technician perform a "seasonal tuning" of your heat pump. Ensure the flow temperature is optimized for the current insulation levels of the house to maximize the COP (Coefficient of Performance).
*   **Action 2: Smart Energy Management (EMS).** Install a smart energy manager that directs excess PV production to the heat pump (e.g., pre-heating the house or hot water tank during peak sun hours).
*   **Why:** This maximizes the "free" energy you are already generating, reducing operational costs without requiring structural changes.
