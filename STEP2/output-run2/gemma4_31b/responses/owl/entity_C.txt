### Building Analysis
The building is a modern **attached house (constructed in 2023)** with an already excellent energy performance score (**EPC: 50**, which falls within the **A label** category). Most structural elements—walls, flat roof, and floor above basement—are already "Well Insulated." The ventilation system (System D) is also high-performing.

However, there are two primary areas for improvement to further maximize energy efficiency and operational performance:
1.  **Thermal Envelope Gap:** The gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated," making it the weakest point in the building's thermal shell.
2.  **Heating Inefficiency:** The building currently relies on **electric convectors** (which are energy-intensive) and a boiler. The data explicitly indicates a **reason to change the emission system**.

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovations should follow a **"Fabric First"** approach—optimizing the building shell before upgrading the technical installations.

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** While the rest of the house is well-insulated, the gable roof is the only element rated as "moderately insulated." Improving this prevents heat loss through the top of the building.
*   **Practical Note:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. This is a lower-cost intervention compared to replacing the roof and provides immediate reductions in heating demand.

#### Step 2: Heating System Modernization
*   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Reasoning:** The building has a stated need to change its emission system and currently uses inefficient electric convectors. Since the building already has **floor heating** (a low-temperature emission system), it is perfectly suited for an air-water heat pump.
*   **Constraint Check:** Geothermal options (vertical and horizontal) were ruled out based on the site's potential data. A heat grid is also unavailable.
*   **Benefit:** This replaces the boiler and electric convectors with a sustainable, high-efficiency system, significantly lowering long-term operational costs.

#### Step 3: Domestic Hot Water Efficiency
*   **Action:** **Place Heat Pump Boiler**
*   **Reasoning:** To complement the air-water heat pump, upgrading the water heating to a heat pump boiler ensures that domestic hot water is produced using the most energy-efficient method available.
