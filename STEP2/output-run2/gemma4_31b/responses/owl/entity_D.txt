Based on the provided RENO ontology and the specific building data, here is the proposed renovation plan.

### Building Analysis
The building is a **modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017)** with an already excellent energy profile (**EPC score of 87**). Most critical elements—including the walls, flat roof, and floor above the basement—are already "Well Insulated." The building is also equipped with a heat pump, a Ventilation System D (high efficiency), and PV panels.

Because the building is already highly efficient, the goal is **"fine-tuning"** to further lower the energy score and improve long-term operational performance without incurring unnecessary costs.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

Given the owner's motivation to further improve the EPC, the following sequence is recommended to maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact)
The only remaining "weak point" in the thermal envelope is the main gable roof.
*   **Action: Insulate Gable Roof**
    *   **Reasoning:** While other elements are "Well Insulated," the main gable roof is currently only **"Moderately Insulated."** Upgrading this to a high insulation grade is the most effective way to reduce heat loss and further lower the EPC score.
    *   **Sequence Note:** This should be done first as it addresses the primary thermal deficiency.
    *   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.

#### Phase 2: Glazing Upgrade (Moderate Impact)
The current windows have wooden frames in good condition and double glazing.
*   **Action: Replace Glazing (Upgrade to Triple Glazing)**
    *   **Reasoning:** Since the frames are in "Good State" and made of wood (which is naturally insulating), there is no need to replace the entire window unit. Replacing only the glass panes with **triple glazing** will significantly reduce thermal transmittance (U-value) and improve acoustic performance.
    *   **Sequence Note:** This follows roof insulation as it is a more expensive per-unit upgrade and provides diminishing returns compared to the roof.

#### Phase 3: Passive Solar Control (Low Cost / High Operational Value)
To prevent overheating (which would otherwise force the heat pump to work in cooling mode, increasing electricity consumption).
*   **Action: Place Sunshades**
    *   **Reasoning:** This is a highly cost-effective intervention with a low nuisance level. By reducing solar gain during summer, the building maintains a stable internal temperature passively.
    *   **Sequence Note:** This is a final "finishing" touch that optimizes the operational energy use of the existing HVAC system.

### Summary of the Renovation Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Target Element | Goal | Cost Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Main Roof | Eliminate primary heat loss | Moderate (per $m^2$) |
| **2** | **Replace Glazing** | Windows | Reduce U-value (Double $\rightarrow$ Triple) | Moderate (per $m^2$) |

### Exclusions & Justifications
*   **Wall Insulation:** Not recommended. All walls are already "Well Insulated."
*   **HVAC Replacement:** Not recommended. The building already uses a Heat Pump and Ventilation System D; replacing these would be cost-ineffective as they are already top-tier technologies.
*   **Geothermal/Heat Grid:** Not possible. The site data explicitly states there is no potential for geothermal drilling and no presence of a heat grid.
*   **Additional PV Panels:** Not recommended. The building already has PV panels and the data indicates no further potential for installation.
