To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach: reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating and energy systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact/Priority)
The building has several "Not Insulated" elements. Addressing these first ensures that any subsequent heating system is downsized and more efficient.
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises and flat roofs are significant sources of energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls (Exterior):** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are `FullBrickWall` and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, exterior insulation is the most effective way to reduce the U-value of the building shell.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. This will reduce cold drafts and improve the thermal comfort of the lower living areas.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade
Once the building is sealed with insulation, air quality and moisture control become critical.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current `VentilationSystemA` (natural supply/exhaust) is the least efficient. Upgrading to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) will prevent condensation and improve overall energy performance.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Modernization
With a reduced energy load due to Phase 1, the heating system can be transitioned from a boiler to a sustainable alternative.
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the building uses radiators, these should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters to maximize the efficiency of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Geothermal options are not viable for this site (`potential` is false), and there is no heat grid. An air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective, high-efficiency alternative to the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has `hasPotentialForPV = true`. Installing solar panels will significantly lower operational costs and further improve the EPC label.

### Summary of Sequence & Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Insulation** | Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor | Reduces heat loss; allows for a smaller, cheaper heat pump. |
| **2. Ventilation** | Replace Ventilation System | Maintains indoor air quality after the envelope is sealed. |
| **3. Heating** | Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water HP | Transitions from fossil fuels to electricity with high efficiency. |
| **4. Energy** | PV-Panels on Roof | Offsets the electrical load of the new heating system. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By following this sequence, the owner avoids "over-specifying" the heat pump (which would happen if installed before insulation), thereby reducing both the initial investment and long-term energy bills.
