### **Building Analysis Summary**
The building is a very modern **Attached Building (constructed in 2023)** with an already excellent energy profile (**EPC score of 50**, which typically falls within the A/A+ range). Most elements—including the front and rear walls, the floor above the basement, and the flat roof extension—are already **Well Insulated**. The ventilation system is already at the highest standard (System D).

However, there are two primary opportunities for improvement to further lower the EPC and satisfy the owner's motivation:
1.  **Roof Insulation:** The main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated," making it the weakest link in the thermal envelope.
2.  **Heating System:** There is a documented reason to change the emission system, and the current boiler can be replaced with a more sustainable alternative.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Plan**

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation should follow a **"Fabric First"** approach—optimizing the building envelope before upgrading the technical installations.

#### **Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization**
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** The `RoofMain` is the only major structural element not categorized as "Well Insulated." Improving this will reduce heat loss through the top of the building, reducing the overall heating load.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Roof insulation typically offers one of the fastest returns on investment in terms of energy savings.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.

#### **Step 2: Sustainable Heating Upgrade**
*   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Reasoning:** The building has a documented need to change its emission system (`hasReasonToChangeEmissionSystem, true`). Since the building already possesses **Floor Heating** (a low-temperature emission system) and has no potential for geothermal drilling or access to a heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most efficient and practical choice.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate to High. While the initial investment is higher than a boiler, it leverages the existing floor heating for maximum COP (Coefficient of Performance) and significantly reduces operational energy costs.
