### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the focus should be on completing the "thermal envelope" before considering expensive system upgrades.

#### Step 1: Roof Insulation (High Priority)
*   **Action:** `insulate gable roof`
*   **Rationale:** The Gable Roof is the only significant thermal leak in the building envelope (Moderately Insulated). Bringing this up to a "Well Insulated" state will directly lower the energy score and improve thermal retention.
*   **Practical Note:** According to the ontology, this is a low-nuisance intervention and generally does not require a permit in Flanders. Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: Glazing Upgrade (Medium Priority / Long-term)
*   **Action:** `replace glazing` (Upgrade from Double to Triple Glazing)
*   **Rationale:** The building currently has double glazing. While sufficient, moving to triple glazing is the most effective way to further reduce the EPC score for a house that is already well-insulated.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Since the wooden window frames are in **"Good State,"** you can replace the glazing units without replacing the entire window frame, significantly reducing costs.

#### Step 3: Emission System Optimization (Low Priority)
*   **Action:** `place emission system on low temperature`
*   **Rationale:** Because the building uses a Heat Pump, the system's efficiency is heavily dependent on the emission temperature. If the current space heaters are high-temperature, switching to low-temperature emitters (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators) will maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the existing heat pump.
