Based on the provided RENO ontology and the specific data for the building, here is the proposed renovation plan.

### Building Analysis Summary
The building is a modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017) with an already impressive energy profile (EPC score: 87). Most primary elements—walls, the flat roof extension, and the floor above the basement—are already **well insulated**. The building is also equipped with high-efficiency systems, including a heat pump, a Type D ventilation system, and PV panels.

The primary goal of the owner is to **improve the EPC**, which, given the current high performance, requires targeting the few remaining "moderately" performing elements to reach a peak efficiency level.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the focus is shifted from systemic overhauls (which are unnecessary) to "fine-tuning" the thermal envelope.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact)
**1. Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** The `RoofMain` (Gable Roof) is the only major structural element currently rated as **Moderately Insulated**. Upgrading this to "Well Insulated" will eliminate the primary source of heat loss/gain.
*   **Action:** Apply insulation to the gable roof.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the roof entirely, as the roof is currently in a "Good State" (value 8).
*   **Sequence Note:** This should be done first to seal the building envelope.

**2. Replace Glazing (Upgrade to Triple Glazing)**
*   **Reasoning:** The windows currently have double glazing. While efficient, upgrading to triple glazing is the most effective way to further lower the EPC score.
*   **Action:** Replace existing double glazing with triple glazing.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Because the `WoodenWindowFrames` are in a "Good State," there is no need to replace the entire window unit (`replace windows`). Replacing only the glazing is a high-value, lower-cost intervention.

#### Phase 2: Operational Performance & Comfort (Low Cost)
**3. Place Sunshades**
*   **Reasoning:** With a high-efficiency envelope and a heat pump, preventing solar gain in the summer is critical to reducing the energy load for cooling and maintaining thermal comfort.
*   **Action:** Install outdoor sunshades.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** This is a low-cost, fixed-price upgrade that prevents the "overheating" often associated with highly insulated modern homes.
