Based on the provided RENO ontology and the specific data for the building, here is a professional renovation plan. The strategy follows the **"Envelope-First"** principle: we first reduce the energy demand by improving insulation, then upgrade the ventilation and heating systems, and finally integrate renewable energy to offset operational costs.

### Building Analysis Summary
*   **Building Type:** Attached Building (1832)
*   **Current EPC Score:** 262 (C Label)
*   **Primary Goal:** Improve EPC and overall energy efficiency.
*   **Key Weaknesses:** Uninsulated flat roof, uninsulated exterior walls (full brick), and an uninsulated floor above the basement.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal options are not viable; no heat grid available.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
*The most cost-effective way to improve EPC is to stop heat from escaping. We prioritize areas with the worst insulation.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority target as heat rises and flat roofs often have significant thermal bridges.
2.  **Insulate Wall on Exterior:** Both the front and rear walls are `FullBrickWall` and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, exterior insulation is the most efficient choice to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. This is a low-nuisance, high-impact upgrade that prevents cold air from entering the living spaces from below.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Air Quality & Efficiency)
*Once the house is "sealed" with insulation, controlled ventilation is required to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses **System A** (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient. Upgrading to a more modern system (such as System C or D) will reduce uncontrolled heat loss through drafts.

#### Phase 3: Energy Generation Transition (Decarbonization)
*With a reduced energy load due to Phase 1 & 2, we can now install a smaller, more efficient heating system.*

5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the optimal choice. It replaces the old boiler and works efficiently with the existing radiator system.
6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency of the new heat pump, it is recommended to upgrade the radiators to low-temperature emitters (or increase their surface area) to reduce the required flow temperature.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration (Operational Cost Reduction)
*The final step is to produce the electricity needed to run the new heat pump.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Installing solar panels on the roof will significantly lower the operational cost of the heat pump and improve the final EPC label.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale | Nuisance Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Flat Roof Insulation** | Stops highest heat loss; high EPC impact. | High |
| **2** | **Exterior Wall Insulation** | Addresses the largest surface area of heat loss. | High |
| **3** | **Floor Insulation** | Low-cost, high-comfort improvement. | Low |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents condensation in a now-insulated home. | Moderate |
| **5** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable, high-efficiency source. | Moderate |
| **6** | **PV-Panels** | Offsets the electricity cost of the heat pump. | Low |

### Cost-Effectiveness Note
By insulating the **Flat Roof** and **Exterior Walls** first, the required capacity (and thus the cost) of the **Air-Water Heat Pump** can be reduced, as the building will require significantly less energy to maintain a comfortable temperature. This sequence ensures the highest return on investment (ROI) and the most significant jump in the EPC label.
