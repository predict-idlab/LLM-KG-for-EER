Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is already highly energy-efficient, as evidenced by its excellent **EPC score of 87** (A-label territory) and the fact that most walls and floors are already "Well Insulated." 

Since the owner's motivation is **to improve the EPC**, the focus must shift from major structural overhauls to "fine-tuning" the remaining thermal bridges and optimizing the existing heat pump system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the following sequence is recommended:

#### 1. Thermal Envelope Optimization (Highest Priority)
The only significant gap in the building's insulation is the main gable roof, which is currently only "Moderately Insulated."
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**.
*   **Reasoning:** Improving the roof from "moderate" to "well insulated" is the most impactful way to reduce heat loss in a building where walls and floors are already optimized.
*   **Optimal Sequence:** This should be done first to reduce the overall heating load before adjusting the emission system.

#### 2. Glazing Upgrade (Secondary Priority)
The bedrooms and living areas currently use double glazing. While efficient, this is the next logical step for an A-label target.
*   **Action:** **Replace Glazing** (Upgrade from Double to Triple Glazing).
*   **Reasoning:** Triple glazing further reduces U-values and improves thermal comfort, pushing the EPC score even lower.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness Note:** Since the window frames are wooden and in "Good State," replacing only the glazing (rather than the entire window unit) is the most cost-effective approach.

#### 3. System Efficiency Tuning (Final Step)
The building already utilizes a Heat Pump, which is highly efficient. To maximize its performance, the delivery of heat must be optimized.
*   **Action:** **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**.
*   **Reasoning:** Heat pumps operate with significantly higher Coefficients of Performance (COP) when delivering heat at lower temperatures. Ensuring the emission system is optimized for low-temperature delivery maximizes the operational efficiency of the existing generation system.
