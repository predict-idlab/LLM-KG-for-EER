### **Building Analysis Summary**
*   **Current State:** A semi-detached building from 1951 with an EPC score of **388 kWh/m²year (Label D)**. 
*   **Key Weaknesses:** The thermal envelope is poor; the gable roof, all exterior walls (full brick), and the floor above the basement are **not insulated**.
*   **System Status:** The building uses an outdated boiler (marked for replacement) and a basic Type A ventilation system. It has no access to a heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy.
*   **Strengths:** Windows are already double-glazed with frames in good condition; the flat roof extension is moderately insulated; there is high potential for PV panels.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**
To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the plan follows the **"Fabric First"** principle: reduce energy demand through insulation before upgrading the generation systems.

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Demand Reduction)**
*These steps provide the highest return on investment by drastically lowering the energy required to heat the home.*
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises and escapes rapidly through uninsulated roofs.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The full brick walls are not insulated. Exterior insulation is more effective than interior insulation and is feasible here as there is no historical protection on the facades.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** Addressing the uninsulated floor will eliminate cold drafts and improve thermal comfort in the living areas.

#### **Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation**
*Once the building is "sealed" with insulation, mechanical ventilation is necessary to prevent humidity buildup and ensure health.*
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System D with heat recovery) to ensure fresh air without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### **Phase 3: Heating System Modernization (Efficient Generation)**
*With a reduced energy load, we can now install a smaller, more efficient heating system.*
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, existing radiators should be updated to low-temperature emitters to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration (Offsetting Consumption)**
*The final step is to transition from reducing consumption to generating clean energy.*
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** Utilize the building's high PV potential to offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.
