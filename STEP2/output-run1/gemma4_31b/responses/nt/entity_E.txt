Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, here is the proposed renovation path. The strategy follows the "fabric-first" principle: we first minimize energy loss (insulation), then optimize the heating system, and finally integrate renewable energy.

### **Building Analysis Summary**
*   **Building Type:** Detached, constructed in 1862.
*   **Current EPC Score:** 165 (B Label).
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** Single glazing on all windows and completely uninsulated cavity walls.
*   **Strengths:** Roof is already well-insulated; window frames are in good state.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal drilling is not possible; no heat grid available.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Plan**

#### **Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Priority: High)**
*Goal: Stop heat leakage to reduce the required capacity of the future heating system.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four exterior walls are cavity walls and currently not insulated. Injecting insulation into these cavities is the most cost-effective method to improve the thermal performance of the walls without altering the building's exterior facade.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The building currently has single glazing in both the living area and bedrooms. Since the wooden frames are in a "Good State," replacing only the glazing (upgrading to double or triple glazing) is more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** To eliminate cold drafts from the ground, insulate the floor above the crawl space.

#### **Phase 2: HVAC & Emission Systems (Priority: Medium)**
*Goal: Transition from inefficient electric heating to a sustainable, low-carbon system.*

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new heat pump, the existing emission system (currently a mix of electric convectors and radiators) should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators) to maximize the efficiency of the heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal is not an option and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most efficient choice for a detached building of this size.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural supply) to a more modern system (System B or C) to ensure air quality is maintained after the building envelope has been sealed.

#### **Phase 3: Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)**
*Goal: Offset operational costs with renewable energy.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels is the final step to move toward a net-zero energy balance and significantly lower monthly electricity bills.

### **Optimal Execution Sequence & Rationale**

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Wall & Floor Insulation** | Lowest cost per kWh saved; reduces the size/cost of the heat pump needed. |
| **2** | **Glazing Upgrade** | Eliminates the largest thermal bridges in the house. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emission** | Prepares the "delivery" system for the high-efficiency heat pump. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces inefficient electric heating with a sustainable alternative. |
| **5** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents condensation/mold issues caused by the new airtight envelope. |
| **6** | **PV Panels** | Provides the electricity needed to run the heat pump and ventilation. |

### **Cost-Effectiveness Note**
By choosing **Cavity Wall Insulation** over Exterior Wall Insulation and **Glazing Replacement** over Window Replacement, we leverage the existing structural assets (cavity walls and existing frames) to minimize costs while achieving the same thermal performance goals.
