To move your house from an EPC of 262 to a high-performance rating, you must follow the **"Fabric First"** approach. The most common mistake is replacing the boiler before insulating; this leads to oversized, inefficient heating systems.

Because your home is an attached house in Ghent (likely in a dense urban area), external wall insulation is probably impractical. Therefore, the strategy focuses on internal optimization and high-impact thermal barriers.

### The Optimal Renovation Sequence

#### Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Stopping Heat Loss)
*Before upgrading heating, you must reduce the demand for it.*

1.  **Flat Roof Insulation:** This is your highest priority. Flat roofs are major sources of heat loss. Install high-performance rigid insulation boards (e.g., PIR) on top of the existing structure (inverted roof) or internally.
2.  **Basement Ceiling Insulation:** This is a "low-hanging fruit" with high ROI. Insulating the ceiling of the basement prevents "cold feet" and stops heat from escaping into the ground.
3.  **Internal Wall Insulation (IWI):** Since you have solid brick walls and cannot insulate externally, apply a breathable internal insulation system (such as wood-fiber boards or calcium silicate). **Warning:** Use vapor-permeable materials to avoid interstitial condensation and mold, which is common in 19th-century Belgian brick homes.
4.  **Roof Top-up:** Since the pitched roof has "moderate" insulation, add an extra layer of mineral wool or cellulose in the attic space to reach modern standards (R-value $\geq$ 5.0).

#### Phase 2: Ventilation (Ensuring Air Quality)
*Once you insulate a house, it becomes "airtight," which can lead to humidity and mold if natural ventilation is insufficient.*

5.  **Balanced Ventilation with Heat Recovery (MVHR):** Replace the basic natural ventilation with a mechanical system. An MVHR system recovers heat from outgoing stale air to warm incoming fresh air, drastically reducing the load on your heating system.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Transition (The Engine)
*Now that the house is insulated, you can install a smaller, more efficient heating source.*

6.  **Low-Temperature Emitters:** Since your radiators are in poor condition, replace them with **low-temperature radiators** or **underfloor heating** (if you are already renovating floors). This is critical because modern heat pumps are inefficient when paired with old, high-temperature radiators.
7.  **Air-to-Water Heat Pump:** Replace the old boiler with an electric air-to-water heat pump. Given that geothermal is not an option, this is the most sustainable choice for Ghent’s climate.

#### Phase 4: Energy Generation (Offsetting Costs)
*Now that you have shifted your heating from gas/oil to electricity, you need to produce your own power.*

8.  **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Install PV panels on the pitched roof. This will offset the electricity consumption of the heat pump and the ventilation system, bringing your operational costs close to zero.
