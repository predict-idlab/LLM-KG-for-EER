Given the specific characteristics of your home in Ghent, you are in a very strong starting position. Your building envelope (walls, floor, and ventilation) is already high-performing. The primary "energy leaks" and inefficiencies are located in the **heating generation and distribution systems**, as well as a moderate gap in **roof insulation**.

To maximize your EPC score and long-term cost-effectiveness, I propose the following sequenced renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Quick Win")
Before upgrading the heating system, you must ensure the house retains as much heat as possible. This allows you to install a smaller, less expensive heating unit.

*   **Target: Pitched Roof Insulation.** While the flat roof is well-insulated, the pitched roof is only "moderate." 
    *   **Action:** Increase the insulation thickness of the pitched roof (e.g., adding high-performance mineral wool or PIR boards). 
    *   **Why:** Heat rises. Improving the roof is the most cost-effective way to lower the total heat load of the house, directly improving the EPC score.

### Phase 2: Heating System Overhaul (The "Core Upgrade")
Your current reliance on a boiler and electric convectors is the biggest inefficiency. Electric convectors are among the most expensive ways to heat a home in Belgium.

*   **Action A: Transition to an Air-to-Water Heat Pump (ASHP).** 
    *   Since geothermal is not viable and district heating is unavailable, an ASHP is the optimal choice. 
    *   **Integration:** The ASHP should be connected to your existing underfloor heating. Underfloor systems operate at low temperatures, which is where heat pumps achieve their maximum efficiency (COP).
*   **Action B: Eliminate Electric Convectors.** 
    *   **Action:** Remove all electric convectors. 
    *   **Replacement:** Depending on the room, either extend the underfloor heating loop or install low-temperature radiators connected to the heat pump. 
    *   **Why:** This removes the reliance on expensive direct electricity for heating and consolidates all heating into one high-efficiency system.

### Phase 3: Energy Synergy & Control (The "Optimization")
Since you already have photovoltaic (PV) panels and a Ventilation D system, the goal now is to make these systems work together.

*   **Action A: Smart Energy Management System (HEMS).** 
    *   Install a smart controller that synchronizes your PV production with your Heat Pump. 
    *   **Goal:** "Thermal Buffering." Use excess solar energy during the day to slightly overheat the floor slabs or a buffer tank, effectively using your house as a thermal battery.
*   **Action B: Zoning Controls.** 
    *   Implement smart thermostats for the underfloor heating to ensure you aren't heating unused rooms, further reducing the load on the heat pump.
