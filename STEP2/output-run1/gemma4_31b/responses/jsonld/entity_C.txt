Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, here is the proposed renovation plan. 

### **Executive Summary**
The building is already highly efficient, with an **EPC score of 50 (A Label)** and most structural elements (walls, floor, flat roof) being "Well Insulated." However, there is a clear opportunity to maximize long-term operational performance by addressing the one moderately insulated structural element and upgrading the heating generation system, for which the owner has already indicated a need for change.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovations should follow the "fabric-first" approach: reducing energy demand before upgrading the systems that meet that demand.

#### **Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Demand Reduction)**
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** While the flat roof and walls are well-insulated, the main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated." Improving this to a "Well Insulated" state is the most cost-effective way to prevent heat loss through the top of the building.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.

#### **Step 2: Emission System Optimization (Efficiency Prep)**
*   **Action:** **Place emission system on low temperature**
*   **Reasoning:** The building currently utilizes electric convectors and floor heating. To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump (planned in Step 3), the emission system should be optimized for low-temperature operation. This reduces the workload on the heat pump and significantly lowers electricity consumption.

#### **Step 3: Generation System Upgrade (Sustainable Heating)**
*   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Reasoning:** The building has a "reason to change emission system" and lacks access to a heat grid or geothermal potential (both horizontal and vertical). An air-water heat pump is the most efficient alternative to a traditional boiler, especially when paired with the existing floor heating and the low-temperature optimizations from Step 2.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure the unit is placed at least 2m away from the parcel boundary to avoid the need for a permit (per Flanders regulations).

---

### **Summary of Strategy & Cost-Effectiveness**

| Sequence | Intervention | Priority | Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Insulate Gable Roof | High | Reduces heat loss | High (Low nuisance, high ROI) |
| **2** | Low-Temp Emission | Medium | Increases system COP | High (Pre-requisite for HP) |
| **3** | Air-Water Heat Pump | High | Eliminates fossil fuels | Medium (High fixed cost, low op-cost) |

**Why other suggestions were excluded:**
*   **Windows/Glazing:** Existing windows are in "Good State" (Value 8) and already feature double glazing; replacement would provide diminishing returns.
*   **PV Panels:** The building already possesses PV panels, and the site is marked as having no further potential for additional installations.
*   **Ventilation:** The building already uses Ventilation System D, which is the most advanced type in the ontology.
*   **Wall/Floor Insulation:** These are already categorized as "Well Insulated."
