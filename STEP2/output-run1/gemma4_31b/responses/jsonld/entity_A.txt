### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Reducing Energy Demand)
*The most cost-effective way to lower an EPC score is to stop heat from escaping.*

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. Since it is in "Good State," there is no need for a full replacement. Adding insulation here is the highest-priority intervention due to the volume of heat lost through the top of the building.
2.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** The front, rear, and right walls are full brick and not insulated. I recommend starting with **cavity wall insulation** as it is significantly more cost-effective than exterior or interior insulation.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is not insulated. This is a practical upgrade to prevent cold drafts and improve the thermal comfort of the ground floor.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*As the building becomes more airtight through insulation, ventilation must be upgraded to prevent moisture buildup and ensure air quality.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System C or D) to maintain a healthy indoor climate in the now-insulated home.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating (Generation & Emission)
*With a reduced energy load, the building can now transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency heat pump.*

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The building has a "reason to change emission system." To make a heat pump viable and efficient, the radiators/heating elements should be converted to low-temperature emissions.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal drilling and no heat grid available, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most effective and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Offsetting Consumption)
*The final step is to generate the electricity needed to run the new heat pump.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will significantly lower operational costs and further improve the EPC score by reducing the building's reliance on the grid.

| Sequence | Action | Rationale | Cost/Efficiency Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
