Based on the provided RENO ontology and the specific data for the building, here is the proposed renovation path. 

This plan follows the **"Fabric First"** principle: we first minimize energy loss through the building envelope before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems. This ensures that any new heating system is correctly sized for a more efficient home, preventing over-investment and maximizing long-term cost-effectiveness.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Shell)
The building is from 1951 and currently has an EPC score of **388.0 (Label D)**. The primary goal is to stop heat leakage.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises. 
    *   *Action:* **Insulate gable roof**.
2.  **Insulate External Walls:** All three external walls (Front, Rear, Right) are full brick and not insulated. Since they are full brick (not cavity walls), cavity injection is not possible.
    *   *Action:* **Insulate wall on exterior**. (While more expensive, this is the most energy-efficient method and prevents interior space loss).
3.  **Insulate Floor:** The floor above the basement is not insulated.
    *   *Action:* **Insulate floor above basement**. (This is a low-nuisance, cost-effective upgrade to prevent cold feet and heat loss to the basement).

### Phase 2: Air Quality and Ventilation
Increasing the airtightness of the home through insulation can lead to moisture issues if ventilation is not addressed.

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient.
    *   *Action:* **Replace ventilation system** (upgrade to System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality and maintain thermal comfort.

### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition
The building has a recorded reason to change the emission system, but lacks access to a heat grid and has no potential for geothermal energy.

5.  **Install Heat Pump:** With the thermal shell now optimized, the energy demand is lower.
    *   *Action:* **Place air-water heatpump**. This is the most logical choice given the existing radiator and floor heating systems, replacing the old boiler with a sustainable electric alternative.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6.  **Solar Energy:** The building is flagged as having potential for PV.
    *   *Action:* **Place PV-panels on roof**. This maximizes the use of the roof surface to generate "free" energy for the heat pump and household appliances.
