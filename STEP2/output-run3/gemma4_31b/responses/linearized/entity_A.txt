To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building (EPC score: 388), I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. By prioritizing the building envelope before upgrading technical systems, we reduce the overall energy demand, allowing for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact)
The building currently has several "Not Insulated" elements. Addressing these first prevents heat loss and is the most cost-effective way to lower the EPC score.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises.
2.  **Insulate Walls (Exterior):** `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all not insulated. Given they are `FullBrickWall` (likely solid), exterior insulation is recommended over interior to maximize thermal performance and avoid reducing internal living space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated; addressing this removes a significant source of cold drafts from below.

*Note: The `RoofExtension` is already moderately insulated, so it is a lower priority than the main roof.*

### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation (Necessary Balance)
Once the building is sealed with insulation, natural ventilation (currently `VentilationSystemA`) will be insufficient and may lead to humidity issues.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A to a more controlled system (e.g., System B or C) to ensure a healthy indoor climate and maintain energy efficiency by reducing uncontrolled air leakage.

### Phase 3: Heating System Transition (System Efficiency)
With the heat demand significantly reduced by Phase 1, we can now replace the outdated boiler system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the owner has a reason to change the emission system, upgrading to low-temperature emitters is essential to maximize the efficiency of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal options are not viable for this site (`potentialForVerticalGeothermalDrilling` and `potentialForHorizontalGeothermalCaptation` are both false) and there is no heat grid, an air-water heat pump is the most sustainable and cost-effective generation upgrade.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration (Operational Cost Reduction)
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has `hasPotentialForPV: true`. Installing solar panels will significantly reduce long-term operational costs and further improve the EPC label.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Insulation** | Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor | Reduces energy demand; determines the size of the future heat pump. |
| **2. Ventilation** | Upgrade Ventilation System | Prevents condensation/mold after the envelope is sealed. |
| **3. Heating** | Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Heat Pump | Transitions from fossil fuels to electricity with max efficiency. |
| **4. Renewables** | PV-Panels on Roof | Offsets the electricity cost of the heat pump. |

**Why skip Windows?** The current windows already have double glazing and the wooden frames are in "Good State" (Value: 8). Replacing them now would offer diminishing returns compared to the massive gains from insulating the uninsulated walls and roof.
