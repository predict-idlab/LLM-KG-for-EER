To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. This means reducing the energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
*Prioritize elements that are currently "Not Insulated" to achieve the highest immediate impact on the EPC score.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Extension):** The roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority area for heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This element is not insulated and provides a significant opportunity to reduce cold drafts from below.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both the front and rear full-brick walls are not insulated and have no historical protection. Exterior insulation is more effective than interior insulation for thermal bridging and preserving internal living space.
4.  **Roof Main (Gable Roof):** While moderately insulated, a review is recommended to see if it can be brought up to "Well Insulated" standards during the wall insulation phase to ensure a continuous thermal wrap.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Once the building is airtight due to insulation, mechanical ventilation is necessary to prevent condensation and maintain air quality.*

5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade the current Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (such as System C or D) to ensure that the heat retained by the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Upgrade
*With a reduced energy load, the heating system can be downsized and switched to a sustainable source.*

6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency of a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emitters (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators).
7.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal drilling/captation and no heat grid is present, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
*The final step is to offset the remaining electricity consumption of the heat pump.*

8.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower operational costs and significantly boost the final EPC label.

### Summary of Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Insulation** | Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Floor $\rightarrow$ Walls | Reduces total energy demand; prevents "over-sizing" the new heater. |
| **2. Ventilation** | Upgrade System A | Prevents moisture build-up in a now-insulated envelope. |
| **3. Heating** | Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water HP | Shifts from fossil fuels to electricity; optimized for the new envelope. |
| **4. Renewables** | PV Panels | Offsets the electricity cost of the heat pump. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** I have excluded the replacement of windows. Since the existing windows already have double glazing and the wooden frames are in "Good State" (Value: 8), the ROI on replacing them is significantly lower than insulating the uninsulated walls and roof.
