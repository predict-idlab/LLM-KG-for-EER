Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building from 1862 with a current energy score of 165 (B Label). While the building is already performing relatively well, the owner's motivation is to further improve the EPC.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. We will first reduce the energy demand of the building through insulation and glazing before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
*The goal here is to stop energy leaks using the most cost-effective methods available.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** 
    *   **Action:** `insulate wall cavity`
    *   **Justification:** All four exterior walls are cavity walls and currently "Not Insulated." This is the most cost-effective insulation upgrade (Unit Price: 22/m²) and has a low nuisance level.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** 
    *   **Action:** `insulate floor above basement`
    *   **Justification:** The building has a floor above a crawl space. Insulating this area (Unit Price: 30/m²) prevents cold air from entering the living spaces from below.
3.  **Upgrade Window Glazing:** 
    *   **Action:** `replace glazing`
    *   **Justification:** The bedrooms and living area currently have "Single Glazing," which is a major source of thermal loss. Since the wooden frames are in "Good State" (Value: 8), replacing only the glass is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency System Upgrade (Reducing Energy Consumption)
*Once the building is "tight," we can install a smaller, more efficient heating system.*

4.  **Transition to Heat Pump:** 
    *   **Action:** `place air-water heatpump`
    *   **Justification:** There is a stated reason to change the emission system, and a heat grid is unavailable. Since the site has no potential for geothermal drilling/captation, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient alternative to the existing boiler. This aligns with the goal of improving the EPC score.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Energy Generation (Lowering Operational Costs)
*The final step is to offset the electricity used by the new heat pump.*

5.  **Install Solar Energy:** 
    *   **Action:** `place PV-panels on roof`
    *   **Justification:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing panels on the roof will reduce reliance on the grid and lower the overall energy score of the dwelling.
