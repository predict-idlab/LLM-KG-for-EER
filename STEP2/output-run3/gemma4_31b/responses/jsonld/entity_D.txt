### Energy Efficiency Renovation Analysis

**Current Building Status:**
The property is a modern semi-detached building (constructed in 2017) with an exceptionally high energy performance (EPC score of 87, placing it in the **A Label** category). The thermal envelope is largely well-insulated (walls and floors), and the building already utilizes high-efficiency systems, including a heat pump and a Type D ventilation system.

**Primary Opportunity:**
The only significant thermal weakness identified is the **Gable Roof**, which is currently only **"Moderately Insulated."** Given the owner's motivation to further improve the EPC, the focus should be on completing the thermal envelope and optimizing operational comfort.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the following sequence is recommended. This follows the "Fabric First" principle—optimizing the building shell before adding technical equipment.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact)
*   **Action: Insulate Gable Roof**
    *   **Reasoning:** The roof is the only element not yet "Well Insulated." Increasing the insulation grade here will reduce heat loss during winter and heat gain during summer.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Roof insulation typically offers one of the best returns on investment for energy reduction.
    *   **Note:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.

#### Phase 2: Glazing Upgrade (Incremental Gain)
*   **Action: Replace Glazing (Double $\rightarrow$ Triple Glazing)**
    *   **Reasoning:** While the current double glazing is sufficient, upgrading to triple glazing will further lower the U-value of the windows, reducing the load on the heat pump.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate. Since the wooden frames are in "Good State," replacing only the glazing (rather than the entire window) is the most cost-effective approach.

#### Phase 3: Operational Comfort & Cooling (Preventative)
*   **Action: Place Sunshades**
    *   **Reasoning:** In highly insulated modern homes (EPC A), overheating in summer is a common risk. External sunshades are the most energy-efficient way to manage solar gain without relying on energy-intensive air conditioning.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Low installation cost with a significant impact on summer energy consumption.

#### Phase 4: System Optimization (Efficiency Refinement)
*   **Action: Place Heat Pump Boiler**
    *   **Reasoning:** The building already has a heat pump for space heating. Adding a dedicated heat pump boiler for domestic hot water ensures that water heating is performed with the same high efficiency as space heating.
    *   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate. This optimizes the "Generation System" to ensure no fossil fuels or inefficient electric resistance heaters are used for water.

**Final Expert Note:** Because the building is already an "A" rated property, the marginal gains of each step will be smaller than in a traditional renovation. The focus here is on **precision tuning** to reach a near-zero energy standard.
