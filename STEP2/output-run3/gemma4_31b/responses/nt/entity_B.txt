Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is an older attached building (1832) with a current energy score of **262 kWh/m²year (C Label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC. 

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach. This ensures that the building's energy demand is reduced before investing in high-cost heating systems, allowing for a smaller, more efficient heat pump.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Immediate Impact)
Focus on eliminating the largest sources of heat loss. The building has several "Not Insulated" elements that are high priorities.
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (RoofExtension):** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises and flat roofs are often significant energy leaks.
2.  **Insulate Walls (Exterior):** Both the front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, **exterior wall insulation** is the most effective choice to eliminate thermal bridges.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and improves the comfort of the ground floor.
4.  **Insulate Gable Roof (RoofMain):** While currently "moderately insulated," upgrading this to "well insulated" should be considered to fully seal the envelope.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
Once the building is airtight and insulated, the ventilation system must be upgraded to prevent moisture buildup and ensure a healthy indoor climate.
5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A (natural supply) to a more efficient system (e.g., System D with heat recovery) to ensure that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air exchange.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition
With the reduced heat demand from Phase 1, we can now transition from the old boiler to a sustainable system.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal drilling is not possible at this site, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient alternative for a whole-house solution.
7.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of the heat pump, replace existing radiators with low-temperature emitters (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized radiators).

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the remaining energy consumption with on-site production.
8.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. This will lower operational costs and significantly improve the final EPC label.

### Summary of Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Insulation** | Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor | Reduces total energy demand; prevents "over-sizing" the heat pump. |
| **2. Ventilation** | System Upgrade | Maintains air quality in a now-sealed, insulated building. |
| **3. Heating** | Air-Water Heat Pump | Shifts from fossil fuels to electricity; optimized for a low-demand shell. |
| **4. Generation** | PV Panels | Reduces electricity bills for the new heat pump. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By insulating the walls and roof first, the required capacity of the heat pump is reduced, lowering both the initial installation cost and the long-term monthly energy bills. Because there is no historical protection on the walls, exterior insulation provides the best return on investment.
