To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. The goal is to first reduce the building's energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating and generation systems. This ensures that the new heating system is correctly sized for a lower-load building, preventing over-investment and maximizing operational performance.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Demand)
The building currently has an EPC score of 388 (Label D), indicating significant heat loss. The priority is to seal the envelope.

1.  **Roof Insulation:** The **RoofMain (Gable Roof)** is currently not insulated. This is the most critical step as heat rises. 
    *   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**.
2.  **Wall Insulation:** The **Wall_Front, Wall_Rear, and Wall_Right** are all full brick and not insulated. 
    *   **Action:** **Insulate Wall on Exterior**. (Note: Since they are full brick, cavity insulation is likely not an option; exterior insulation provides the highest thermal benefit).
3.  **Floor Insulation:** The **FloorAboveBasement** is not insulated.
    *   **Action:** **Insulate Floor Above Basement**.

### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
Once the building is airtight and insulated, the existing Ventilation System A (natural supply) will be insufficient and may lead to moisture issues.

4.  **Ventilation Upgrade:** 
    *   **Action:** **Replace Ventilation System**. Upgrading to a system with heat recovery (such as System D) will prevent the heat gained from the new insulation from being lost through ventilation.

### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition
With the energy demand significantly lowered, the current boiler system can be replaced with a sustainable alternative.

5.  **Emission System Upgrade:** Heat pumps operate most efficiently at lower temperatures.
    *   **Action:** **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**. This prepares the building for the heat pump.
6.  **Heat Pump Installation:** Since geothermal drilling is not possible on this site and no heat grid is available:
    *   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**. This is the most cost-effective and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

7.  **Solar Energy:** The building has confirmed potential for PV.
    *   **Action:** **Place PV-Panels on Roof**. This reduces operational costs and improves the EPC label toward the owner's goal.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor** | **Fabric First:** Reduces the total kWh needed to heat the home. |
| **2** | **Replace Ventilation System** | Ensures air quality and prevents heat loss from the newly insulated shell. |
| **3** | **Low Temp Emission $\rightarrow$ Air-Water HP** | **Right-Sizing:** The HP is sized based on the *new* lower demand, reducing installation costs. |
| **4** | **PV-Panels on Roof** | **Operational Cost:** Powers the HP with clean energy, maximizing long-term ROI. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By following this sequence, you avoid the common mistake of installing a large, expensive heating system that is only necessary because the house is poorly insulated. This path ensures the lowest possible operational cost and the highest jump in EPC rating.
