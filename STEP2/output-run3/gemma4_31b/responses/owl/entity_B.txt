To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. This approach ensures that the building's energy demand is reduced through insulation before upgrading the heating and generation systems, preventing the installation of oversized and expensive equipment.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Impact, Cost-Effective)
The goal here is to eliminate the most significant heat losses. Several elements are currently "Not Insulated."
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (RoofExtension):** Since the flat roof is not insulated, this is a priority to prevent heat from escaping through the top of the extension.
2.  **Insulate Wall Cavity (Wall_Front & Wall_Rear):** The walls are full brick and not insulated. Cavity insulation is the most cost-effective method for brick walls compared to exterior insulation, offering a high return on investment.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This element is currently not insulated; adding insulation here will significantly reduce cold drafts from the basement.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building envelope is sealed through insulation, the natural ventilation (System A) may become insufficient.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System C or D) to ensure a healthy indoor climate and prevent condensation in the now better-insulated home.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition
With the heat demand lowered by Phase 1, we can now replace the outdated generation system.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal options are not possible for this site and no heat grid is available, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient electric alternative to replace the existing boiler.
6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of the new heat pump, the existing radiators should be transitioned to a low-temperature system.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing panels on the roof will lower operational costs and further improve the EPC label.
