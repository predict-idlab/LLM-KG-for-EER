Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the building is a detached house from 1862 with an EPC score of 165. While the roof is already well-insulated, the building envelope (walls, floors, and windows) and the technical installations are outdated. 

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** approach: sealing the building envelope to reduce heat loss before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Quick Wins")
The priority is to stop energy leakage using the most cost-effective methods available for this specific building.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four exterior walls are cavity walls and currently not insulated. This is the most cost-effective insulation upgrade (low nuisance, lowest unit price).
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated; addressing this will eliminate cold drafts from the ground.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** The bedrooms and living area use single glazing. Since the wooden frames are in "Good State," replacing only the glazing (rather than the entire window) is the most cost-effective way to drastically reduce thermal loss.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
Once the building is better insulated and sealed, air exchange must be managed to prevent moisture buildup and improve efficiency.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality and reduce heat loss through uncontrolled ventilation.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Modernization
With the heat demand lowered by Phase 1, we can now install a right-sized, high-efficiency heating system.
5.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission:** The current system is a mix of electric convectors and radiators. To maximize the efficiency of a heat pump, the emission system should first be converted to low-temperature emitters.
6.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy on-site, an air-water heat pump is the optimal high-efficiency alternative to the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower operational costs and further improve the EPC label.
