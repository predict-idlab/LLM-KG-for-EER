Most of the thermal envelope is already "WellInsulated," and it features a high-end Ventilation System D and existing PV panels.

Since the owner's motivation is to further improve the EPC, the strategy shifts from "major overhaul" to "fine-tuning." The goal is to eliminate the remaining thermal weaknesses and transition away from inefficient heating elements.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Finalizing the Thermal Envelope (Priority: Low/Medium)
While most elements are well-insulated, the main gable roof is the only remaining point of moderate thermal loss.
1. **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently "ModeratelyInsulated." Upgrading this to "WellInsulated" will minimize heat loss through the top of the building, ensuring the envelope is fully optimized.

#### Phase 2: Transitioning to Sustainable Generation (Priority: Medium)
The building currently utilizes a Boiler and Electric Convectors. Electric convectors are among the least efficient ways to heat a space, and a traditional boiler relies on fossil fuels or high energy consumption.
2. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and efficient upgrade. This will leverage the existing high-quality insulation to operate at maximum efficiency.

#### Phase 3: Emission System Optimization (Priority: Medium)
To maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new heat pump, the system must operate at lower flow temperatures.
3. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Transitioning the current emission system to low-temperature settings is essential. This ensures that the heat pump does not have to work harder (and consume more electricity) to reach the desired indoor temperature.
