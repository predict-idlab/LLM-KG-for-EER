To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388, D Label) for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation must follow a **"Fabric First"** strategy. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The building has several critical points of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment and the most significant drop in energy consumption.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Because heat rises, insulating the gable roof is the most effective single measure to reduce energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective way to eliminate thermal bridges and prevent dampness.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves the thermal comfort of the living spaces.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated, and the windows are already double-glazed with frames in a good state; therefore, these are not priorities for this phase to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the heat demand will be significantly lower, allowing the house to be heated with lower water temperatures.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before upgrading the boiler, the current radiators/heating elements should be transitioned to a low-temperature system. This is crucial for maximizing the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site lacks geothermal potential and access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Medium Priority)
Increasing the insulation and sealing the envelope reduces natural air infiltration. To prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (such as System C or D) to ensure controlled air exchange without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity used by the new heat pump and ventilation system, the building should generate its own power.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will further lower the energy score and reduce long-term operational costs.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the building. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the building for high-efficiency heating. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces fossil fuel heating with a sustainable, low-energy alternative. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures air quality in a now-sealed, energy-efficient envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Provides renewable energy to power the new technical installations. |
