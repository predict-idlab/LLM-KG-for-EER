Given the age of the building and the current energy performance, the most cost-effective and efficient strategy is to follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the building envelope before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The primary goal is to reduce the overall heat demand. Because the house is from 1832 with solid brick walls, it likely suffers from significant thermal bridging and heat loss.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The flat-roof extension is currently uninsulated. Since heat rises, this is one of the most effective and immediate ways to lower energy consumption.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** Both the front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. Since the house is not historically protected, **exterior wall insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the structure from moisture.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and stops heat from leaking into the basement.

*Note: The windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition; therefore, replacing them is not recommended at this stage to keep the renovation cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the thermal envelope is sealed, the house will require significantly less energy to stay warm, allowing for a transition to a low-temperature heating system.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The existing radiators are in poor condition. They should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (such as underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators) to maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the old boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Insulating an old building increases its airtightness. To prevent humidity buildup, mold, and poor indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a more controlled system (such as **System C or D**). This ensures a constant flow of fresh air without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system, the house should move toward energy autonomy.

7.  **Install PV Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for photovoltaic panels, installing them on the roof will provide renewable energy to power the home's electrical systems and further improve the EPC score.
