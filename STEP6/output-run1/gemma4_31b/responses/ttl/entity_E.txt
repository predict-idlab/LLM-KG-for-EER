To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to eliminate heat leakage through the most vulnerable points of the building shell.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into the cavities is the most cost-effective and least disruptive way to significantly reduce thermal loss.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The building currently uses **Single Glazing** in both the bedrooms and living area. Since the wooden window frames are in a **"Good State" (Value 8)**, it is more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space should be insulated to prevent cold drafts and heat loss to the ground.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the heat demand is lowered via insulation and glazing, the heating system can be downsized and modernized.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the existing radiators and emission systems should be transitioned to low-temperature operation.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Renewable Energy (Long-term Priority)
The final phase ensures a healthy indoor climate and offsets the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). Once the envelope is sealed with cavity insulation and new glazing, the air exchange rate will drop. Upgrading to a more controlled system (System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the heat pump and ventilation system, further improving the EPC score.
