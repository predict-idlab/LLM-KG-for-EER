Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building from 1862. While it already possesses a relatively good EPC score of 165 (B Label), there are significant opportunities to further reduce energy consumption and transition to sustainable systems.

To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC rating in a cost-effective manner, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to eliminate heat loss through the building's skin. Because the walls are cavity walls, we can utilize a highly cost-effective insulation method.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, Right) are cavity walls and currently not insulated. Injecting insulation into the cavities is the most cost-effective way to improve the U-value of the walls without altering the building's exterior or interior.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The windows have wooden frames in a "Good State," but they currently use single glazing. Instead of replacing the entire window, **replacing the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) is a cost-effective way to drastically reduce thermal loss.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawlspace/basement should be insulated to prevent heat loss to the ground and eliminate cold drafts.

#### Phase 2: Transition to Low-Temperature Heating (Medium Priority)
Once the building is better insulated, the overall heat demand is lowered, allowing for a more efficient heating system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To prepare for a heat pump, the current emission system (radiators/convectors) should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions. This ensures the future heat pump operates at maximum efficiency (higher COP).
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal drilling, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the optimal sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Medium Priority)
Upgrading the envelope and sealing the building can reduce natural air exchange, making a modern ventilation system necessary.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient mechanical system (such as System C or D) to maintain indoor air quality and prevent moisture buildup in the now-sealed envelope.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and further lower the EPC score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable energy needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.
