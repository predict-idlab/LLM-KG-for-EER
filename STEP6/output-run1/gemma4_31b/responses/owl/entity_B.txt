Upgrading the heating system before the thermal envelope is sealed would lead to an oversized system and higher operational costs.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several significant thermal leaks. Addressing these first reduces the total energy demand, allowing for a smaller, cheaper heating system in later phases.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is a critical priority to prevent energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Because they are full brick (not cavity walls) and have no historical protection, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and drastically improve the EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the basement.
*Note: The gable roof is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state; therefore, these are not recommended for replacement at this time to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the heat demand is significantly lowered. We can now transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency sustainable system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The current radiators should be upgraded or replaced with low-temperature emitters. This is a prerequisite to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid is available, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Increasing insulation and sealing the envelope reduces natural air exchange, which can lead to moisture issues if ventilation is not upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (such as System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor climate without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the energy score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new heat pump and ventilation system, moving the building toward energy independence.
