### Analysis of the Building's Current State

1. **Building Type and Age**:
   - The building is an attached structure constructed in 1832, making it a historical property. This implies potential constraints on modifications due to preservation regulations.
   - The current EPC score is 262, placing it in the C label range.

2. **Thermal Envelope**:
   - **Walls**: Both the front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Insulating these walls is critical to reducing heat loss.
   - **Roofs**: The flat roof (`RoofExtension`) is not insulated, while the gable roof (`RoofMain`) is moderately insulated. Insulating the flat roof is a priority.
   - **Floor**: The floor above the basement is not insulated, contributing to heat loss.

3. **Windows**:
   - All windows have double glazing, which is a positive aspect. The window frames are in good condition (state value of 8), so replacement is not immediately necessary.

4. **Heating and Ventilation**:
   - The current heating system uses radiators, which are efficient but may not be optimized for a well-insulated building.
   - The ventilation system is of type A (natural), which is less efficient compared to mechanical systems.

5. **Potential for Renewable Energy**:
   - The building has potential for PV panels, which can be leveraged to offset energy consumption.

### Proposed Renovation Plan

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Fabric First Approach)
1. **Insulate Walls on Interior**:
   - **Action**: Insulate the interior of the front and rear walls.
   - **Rationale**: Interior insulation is less disruptive and more cost-effective for historical buildings. It helps reduce heat loss significantly.

2. **Insulate Flat Roof**:
   - **Action**: Insulate the flat roof.
   - **Rationale**: Flat roofs are prone to heat loss, and insulating them is crucial for improving thermal performance.

3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement**:
   - **Action**: Insulate the floor above the basement.
   - **Rationale**: Prevents cold drafts from the basement and improves overall comfort.

#### Phase 2: Heating System Upgrade
4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**:
   - **Action**: Install an air-water heat pump.
   - **Rationale**: Replaces the existing boiler with a more efficient and sustainable heating system. It is suitable for buildings with a well-insulated thermal envelope.

5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**:
   - **Action**: Upgrade the emission system to operate at lower temperatures.
   - **Rationale**: Maximizes the efficiency of the heat pump by ensuring it operates within its optimal temperature range.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation System Upgrade
6. **Replace Ventilation System**:
   - **Action**: Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (e.g., System C or D).
   - **Rationale**: Ensures proper air exchange and maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**:
   - **Action**: Install PV panels on the roof.
   - **Rationale**: Leverages the building's potential for PV to generate renewable energy, offsetting the electricity consumption of the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
1. **Insulate Walls on Interior**
2. **Insulate Flat Roof**
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement**
4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**
6. **Replace Ventilation System**
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**

This sequence ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized first, followed by upgrades to the heating and ventilation systems, and finally the integration of renewable energy sources. This approach maximizes energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness while respecting the historical constraints of the building.
