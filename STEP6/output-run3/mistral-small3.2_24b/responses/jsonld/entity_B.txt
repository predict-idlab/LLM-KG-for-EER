### Renovation Plan for the Attached Building (1832)

#### **Context**
The property is an **attached building** constructed in **1832** with a **C label (262)**. The owner's primary motivation is to **improve the EPC rating**. The building has:
- **Uninsulated walls** (front and rear).
- **Moderately insulated gable roof**.
- **Uninsulated floor above the basement**.
- **Double-glazed windows** in good condition.
- **Ventilation system A** (natural air supply and exhaust).
- **Radiator** as the emission system.
- **Potential for PV panels** but no geothermal or heat grid availability.

#### **Renovation Strategy**
The strategy follows the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before sealing the envelope leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

#### **Proposed Renovation Path**

**Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)**
The building has significant heat loss through uninsulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1. **Insulate Walls on Interior**
   - **Action:** Insulate `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` on the interior.
   - **Rationale:** Since the walls are full brick and not cavity walls, interior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Basement**
   - **Action:** Insulate the `FloorAboveBasement`.
   - **Rationale:** Prevents cold drafts from the basement and improves thermal comfort.

3. **Insulate Gable Roof**
   - **Action:** Insulate the `RoofMain` (currently moderately insulated).
   - **Rationale:** Improves thermal performance and reduces heat loss through the roof.

*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and frames are in a "Good State," so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

**Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)**
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
   - **Action:** Replace the existing boiler with an **Air-Water Heat Pump**.
   - **Rationale:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement.

5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**
   - **Action:** Transition the current radiators to low-temperature emissions.
   - **Rationale:** Maximizes the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new heat pump.

**Phase 3: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)**
Modern ventilation systems are necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality in a now-sealed envelope.

6. **Replace Ventilation System**
   - **Action:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (such as System C or D).
   - **Rationale:** Ensures proper air exchange and maintains indoor air quality.

**Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**
   - **Action:** Install PV panels on the roof.
   - **Rationale:** Provides renewable electricity to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

#### **Summary of Optimal Sequence**
1. **Interior Wall Insulation**
2. **Floor Insulation**
3. **Gable Roof Insulation**
4. **Air-Water Heat Pump Installation**
5. **Low-Temperature Emission Upgrade**
6. **Ventilation System Upgrade**
7. **PV Panels Installation**

This sequence ensures that the building is optimized for energy efficiency, thermal comfort, and cost-effectiveness. The renovation plan is tailored to the specific needs and context of the property, ensuring a balanced approach to improving the EPC rating.
