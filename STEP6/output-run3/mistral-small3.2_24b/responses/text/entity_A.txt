### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is a 1951 semi-detached house with several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Pitched Roof:** The pitched roof is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises; insulating the pitched roof is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
2. **Insulate Exterior Walls:** All exterior walls are solid brick and not insulated. **Insulating the walls on the interior** is significantly more cost-effective than wall cavity insulation and provides a substantial increase in thermal retention.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the basement.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition
With a tightened thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.
5. **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission Systems:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Replace the natural ventilation system with a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Pitched Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Exterior Wall Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temperature Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **Ventilation Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

This sequence ensures that each renovation step builds on the previous one, maximizing energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness.
