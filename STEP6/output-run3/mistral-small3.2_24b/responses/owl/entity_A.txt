### Renovation Plan:
Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached building from 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (E Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)
The building has significant heat loss through non-insulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1. **Insulate Flat Roof:**
   - The `RoofExtension` is currently moderately insulated. Since heat rises through flat sections, this should be addressed first to reduce thermal bridging.
   - **Action:** Insulate the flat roof.

2. **Insulate Gable Roof:**
   - The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here is crucial for thermal retention.
   - **Action:** Insulate the gable roof.

3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:**
   - The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement.
   - **Action:** Insulate the floor above the basement.

4. **Insulate Wall On Exterior:**
   - Both `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.
   - **Action:** Insulate the walls on the exterior.

*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and frames are in a "Good State," so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
   - **Action:** Install an air-water heat pump.

6. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:**
   - Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.
   - **Action:** Upgrade the emission system to low temperature.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

7. **Replace Ventilation System:**
   - Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.
   - **Action:** Replace the ventilation system.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

8. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:**
   - The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.
   - **Action:** Install PV panels on the roof.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Flat Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Gable Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Exterior Wall Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **Ventilation System Replacement** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

### Expected Outcome
By following this sequence, the property should see a significant improvement in its EPC rating, reducing energy consumption and costs while increasing comfort and sustainability.
