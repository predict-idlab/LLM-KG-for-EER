Based on the provided building data, this is an attached house from 1832 with an EPC score of 262. Given the age of the property and the current state of the installations, the most cost-effective and energy-efficient strategy is to follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimize the building envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate the primary sources of heat loss. Because the house is not historically protected, we have more flexibility in how we insulate the walls.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Heat rises, and the current flat roof extension is uninsulated. This is the most immediate "quick win" for reducing energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. To maximize efficiency and eliminate thermal bridges, **exterior wall insulation** is recommended. If the facade cannot be altered, interior insulation is a viable, though slightly less efficient, alternative.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The uninsulated floor above the basement allows significant heat to escape downward and creates cold drafts. Adding insulation here will improve thermal comfort and lower the heating load.
*Note: The windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition; therefore, replacing them is not recommended at this stage to keep the renovation cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the thermal envelope is sealed, the house will require significantly less energy to stay warm. This allows for the installation of a low-temperature heating system.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The current radiators are in poor condition. They should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (such as oversized radiators or underfloor heating) to ensure the new heat pump can operate at maximum efficiency.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
By insulating the walls, roof, and floor, the building becomes more airtight. To prevent moisture buildup, mold, and poor indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). A balanced system (System D) with heat recovery would further maximize energy efficiency by recovering heat from exhausted air.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install PV-Panels:** Since the house has the potential for photovoltaic panels, installing them on the roof will provide renewable electricity, further lowering the operational costs and improving the final EPC score.
