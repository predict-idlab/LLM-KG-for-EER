### Proposed Renovation Path

Since the building is already highly efficient, the focus shifts from "stopping heat loss" (which is largely achieved) to **"optimizing energy generation and distribution."**

#### Phase 1: Final Envelope Optimization (Priority: Low)
While the building is mostly well-insulated, there is one remaining gap in the thermal envelope.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` (Gable Roof) is currently only "Moderately Insulated," unlike the walls and flat roof which are "Well Insulated." Upgrading this to a high insulation grade will eliminate the final significant source of thermal leakage.

#### Phase 2: Emission System Upgrade (Priority: High)
The current use of `ElectricConvector` heating is the primary weakness in an otherwise A+ building. Electric resistance heating is inefficient compared to heat pump technology.
2.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To prepare for a high-efficiency heat pump, any remaining high-temperature emitters (like the electric convectors) should be replaced or integrated into a low-temperature system. Since the building already has `FloorHeating`, the goal is to ensure the entire house operates on a low-temperature regime to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the future generator.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heat Generation (Priority: High)
With the envelope optimized and the emission system ready for low-temperature operation, the energy source can be modernized.
3.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** The building has no potential for geothermal drilling or horizontal captation, and no access to a heat grid. Therefore, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and energy-efficient replacement for the existing boiler and electric convectors. This will drastically reduce the electricity consumption required for heating.
