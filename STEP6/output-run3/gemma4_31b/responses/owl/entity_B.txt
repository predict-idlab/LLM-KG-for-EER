**Renovation Plan: Energy Efficiency & EPC Optimization**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a large attached building dating back to 1832 with a current EPC score of 262 (C Label). The owner's primary goal is to improve the EPC rating.

To achieve the maximum energy efficiency gain while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing the energy demand of the building envelope before upgrading the systems that generate and distribute heat.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first prevents the installation of an oversized heating system and provides the most immediate impact on the EPC score.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. Because heat rises, this is a primary point of energy loss and should be addressed first.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the basement.
*Note: The main gable roof is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in good state; therefore, replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is better insulated, the total heat demand is lowered, allowing for a transition to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the current radiator system should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no available heat grid and no potential for geothermal drilling, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality & Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Improving the insulation and sealing the building envelope reduces natural air exchange, which can lead to moisture issues if not managed.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further push the EPC label toward a higher grade.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.
