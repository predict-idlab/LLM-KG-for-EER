To maximize energy efficiency and further improve the EPC score (currently 165, B Label) for this 19th-century detached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the total energy demand, allowing for a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The building is an older structure (1862) with significant heat loss through the walls, floor, and windows. Addressing these elements first is the most effective way to lower energy consumption.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **not insulated**. Injecting insulation into the cavities is the most cost-effective and least disruptive method to drastically reduce wall heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the ground and improves the thermal comfort of the lower level.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** While the wooden window frames are in a "Good State" (value 8), all windows currently use **Single Glazing**. Replacing the glass with double or triple glazing is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit and will eliminate a major source of thermal leakage.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the building is better insulated, the energy required to maintain a comfortable temperature drops, enabling a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before upgrading the boiler, the current emission system (which includes radiators and electric convectors) should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions. This is critical to maximize the efficiency (COP) of the subsequent heat pump installation.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the optimal replacement for the existing boiler to eliminate reliance on fossil fuels.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Medium Priority)
Increasing the airtightness of the home through better glazing and insulation can lead to moisture issues if ventilation is not addressed.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current Ventilation System A (natural) should be upgraded to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality and prevent heat loss through uncontrolled drafts.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the increased electricity demand from the new heat pump and further drive the EPC score toward an A label.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for solar energy. Installing PV panels on the roof will provide renewable electricity to power the heat pump and ventilation system, reducing operational costs.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Cavity Wall $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Most cost-effective "quick wins" to reduce total heat demand. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Eliminates the highest point of thermal loss without replacing good frames. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower demand. |
| **5** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains air quality in a now-sealed, energy-efficient envelope. |
| **6** | **PV Panels** | Generates renewable energy to power the new electrical systems. |
