Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached house from 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (D Label). The owner's primary goal is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall energy demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient, and less expensive heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: Critical)
The building is currently suffering from significant heat loss through its primary structures. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment (ROI) for the EPC score.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the most urgent priority to stop thermal leakage.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** All three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. Because they are full brick (not cavity walls), **exterior insulation** is the most effective way to eliminate thermal bridges and drastically improve the U-value.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the ground and improves the comfort of the lower floors.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in good state, so these will be bypassed to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: High)
Once the building is insulated and sealed, the natural air exchange rate will drop. To prevent moisture buildup (condensation) and maintain indoor air quality, the ventilation must be modernized.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient mechanical system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the energy gains from the new insulation are not offset by uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 3: Transition to High-Efficiency Heating (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened thermal envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower. We can now replace the outdated boiler with a sustainable system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the current radiators should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters. This is crucial to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid available and the site has no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient sustainable alternative.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, and to push the EPC label toward A or B.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the home's new energy-efficient infrastructure.
