### Analysis of Current Building State
The building is a modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017) that is already performing at a very high level of energy efficiency. With an **EPC score of 87**, it already falls within the **A Label** category. 

**Current Strengths:**
*   **Thermal Envelope:** Most elements (walls, flat roof, and floor above basement) are already "Well Insulated."
*   **Systems:** It is equipped with a high-efficiency **Heat Pump** and a **Ventilation System D** (the most advanced mechanical supply and exhaust system), which minimizes heat loss through ventilation.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

**Identified Weakness:**
*   **Roof Main (Gable Roof):** This is the only primary structural element that is only **"Moderately Insulated"** rather than "Well Insulated."

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan
Given that the building is already highly optimized, the goal is no longer a total overhaul, but rather "fine-tuning" to move toward an **A+ label** (energy score < 100, though already achieved, the owner wishes to improve it further) while ensuring cost-effectiveness. 

Because the building is already sealed and efficient, the focus shifts from major heat-loss prevention to marginal gains and summer energy management.

#### Phase 1: Finalizing the Thermal Envelope (Priority: High)
The most significant opportunity for improvement in the building's fabric is the gable roof.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** Upgrading the `RoofMain` from "Moderately Insulated" to "Well Insulated" will reduce the remaining thermal leakage at the top of the house. This is the most cost-effective way to lower the EPC score further.

#### Phase 2: Summer Energy Efficiency (Priority: Medium)
Modern, well-insulated homes with high-performance windows often face the risk of overheating in the summer, which can lead to increased energy consumption for cooling.
2.  **Place Sunshade:** Installing outdoor sunshades is a practical, low-cost addition. By preventing solar gain before it enters the building, the owner reduces the need for active cooling, thereby improving the overall annual energy performance.

#### Phase 3: Marginal Gains (Priority: Low/Optional)
Since the current windows are double-glazed and the frames are in a "Good State," replacing them is generally not cost-effective. However, for a homeowner obsessed with the absolute lowest energy score:
3.  **Replace Glazing (Triple Glazing):** Upgrading from double to triple glazing would provide the final marginal gain in thermal resistance. *Note: This is only recommended if the budget allows, as the return on investment is low given the already high EPC.*

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Addresses the only "moderately" insulated part of the envelope to maximize heat retention. |
| **2** | **Place Sunshade** | Prevents summer overheating, reducing the energy load for cooling in a highly sealed house. |
| **3** | **Upgrade to Triple Glazing** | Optional final step for absolute maximum thermal performance (marginal gain). |

**Final Verdict:** The building is in excellent condition. The most actionable and cost-effective path to further improving the EPC is to focus exclusively on the **Gable Roof insulation** and **Summer shading**.
