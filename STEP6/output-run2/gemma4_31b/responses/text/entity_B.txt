**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided data, this is a large, 19th-century attached house with a current EPC score of 262. To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By prioritizing the building envelope, we reduce the total energy demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building's 1832 solid brick construction and uninsulated sections are the primary sources of heat loss. Addressing these first is the most effective way to lower the EPC score.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Heat rises rapidly; since the flat roof is currently uninsulated, this is a high-priority, high-impact intervention.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. Since the house is not historically protected, **exterior wall insulation** is recommended to eliminate thermal bridges and provide the best thermal performance.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and significantly reduces heat loss to the basement.
*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition; therefore, replacing them is not recommended at this time to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the house is sealed and insulated, the energy required to maintain a comfortable temperature drops significantly. This allows for a transition to low-temperature heating.

4.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission Systems:** The existing radiators are in poor condition. They should be replaced with low-temperature emitters (such as underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators) to ensure the new heat pump can operate at maximum efficiency (higher COP).
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that geothermal energy is not viable and there is no district heating, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the old boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Priority: Medium)
As the building envelope becomes more airtight through insulation, the existing natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from natural ventilation to a mechanical system (such as System C or D). This ensures a controlled flow of fresh air without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the property has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof is the final step to move the home toward energy neutrality.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the building's total energy demand. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to utilize a heat pump efficiently. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the inefficient boiler with a sustainable energy source. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents mold and ensures air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Generates renewable energy to power the new systems. |
