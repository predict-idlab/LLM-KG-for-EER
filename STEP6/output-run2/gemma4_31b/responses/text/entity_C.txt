### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Final Envelope Fine-Tuning (Priority: Low)
The house is already well-insulated, but there is one remaining area for improvement to ensure the thermal shell is fully optimized before upgrading the heating system.

1.  **Upgrade Pitched Roof Insulation:** While the flat roof and walls are "well insulated," the main pitched roof is only "moderately" insulated. Increasing the insulation level here will further reduce heat loss through the top of the house, allowing the new heating system to operate at the lowest possible temperature.

#### Phase 2: Transition to Sustainable Heating (Priority: High)
The current heating system is the weakest link in this home's energy profile. Replacing the boiler and electric convectors is the most impactful step.

2.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the house is already well-insulated and has PV panels to provide renewable electricity, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most logical and cost-effective choice. It will replace the boiler and leverage the existing PV system to minimize operational costs.
3.  **Replace Electric Convectors:** Electric convectors are highly inefficient. These should be removed and replaced. Depending on the layout, the best options are:
    *   **Extending Underfloor Heating:** The most efficient method for a heat pump.
    *   **Low-Temperature Radiators:** If underfloor heating is not feasible in all rooms, installing oversized low-temperature radiators will ensure the heat pump operates at maximum efficiency (higher COP).

#### Phase 3: System Integration and Monitoring (Priority: Medium)
With the hardware in place, the final step is to ensure the systems work in harmony.

4.  **Smart Energy Management:** Since the house already has PV panels and a heat pump, installing a smart energy management system (HEMS) can help synchronize the heat pump's operation with peak solar production hours, effectively storing "thermal energy" in the floor/house during the day.
