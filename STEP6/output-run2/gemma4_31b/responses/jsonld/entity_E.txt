Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building from 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (B Label). While the current EPC is relatively good, the owner's motivation is to further improve this rating.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. By optimizing the building envelope first, we reduce the overall heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into existing cavities is one of the most cost-effective ways to reduce heat loss without altering the building's exterior.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the living area and bedrooms currently have **Single Glazing**. Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State," replacing only the glazing (upgrading to double or triple glazing) is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space should be insulated to eliminate cold drafts and reduce heat loss to the ground.

#### Phase 2: Emission System Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
Before upgrading the boiler, the system that distributes heat must be optimized to work with modern, low-temperature generators.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Transitioning the current radiators/convectors to low-temperature emitters is essential. This increases the efficiency (COP) of the heat pump planned for the next step.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a sealed envelope and low-temperature emitters, the building can now move away from the traditional boiler.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no heat grid available and the site has no potential for geothermal drilling or captation, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable choice for the generation system.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Renewable Energy (Priority: Long-term)
The final steps ensure the home remains healthy and moves toward energy independence.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality once the building is better insulated and sealed.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, further driving down the EPC score.
