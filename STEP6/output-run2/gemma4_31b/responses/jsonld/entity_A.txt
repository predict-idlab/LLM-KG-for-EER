To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388, D Label) for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading technical systems, we ensure that the heating demand is minimized, allowing for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heat pump.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment and is essential before changing the heating system.
1. **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the most critical intervention to reduce energy consumption.
2. **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is recommended to eliminate thermal bridges and provide the best thermal performance.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves ground-floor comfort.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in good state, so these are not prioritized to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the building is sealed, the energy required to maintain temperature drops significantly, allowing for a transition to sustainable technology.
4. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the current emission system (radiators/air heating) should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters.
5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal drilling/captation, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and viable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Medium Priority)
Increasing the insulation and sealing the envelope reduces natural air exchange, which can lead to moisture issues if not addressed.
6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more controlled system (e.g., System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels allows the home to generate its own renewable electricity.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the building. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the building to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents condensation and maintains air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV-Panels Installation** | Offsets operational energy costs with renewable electricity. |
