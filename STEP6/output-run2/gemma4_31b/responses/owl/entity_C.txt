### Analysis of Building State
The property is a modern attached building (constructed in 2023) with an already excellent energy performance profile, indicated by an **EPC score of 50 (A+ Label)**. The thermal envelope is largely optimized: the walls, flat roof, and floor above the basement are all "Well Insulated," and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state.

However, there are two primary areas for improvement to further lower the energy score and satisfy the owner's motivation to improve the EPC:
1.  **Roof Insulation:** The main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated," representing the only significant gap in the building's thermal shell.
2.  **Heating Efficiency:** Despite the new construction, the building utilizes **Electric Convectors**, which are inefficient, and the owner has explicitly indicated a reason to change the emission system.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

To move from an A+ to an even higher efficiency tier, the renovation should follow the optimal sequence of **Envelope $\rightarrow$ Generation $\rightarrow$ Emission**.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Finalization (Priority: Low-Medium)
Before upgrading the heating system, the building's heat retention should be maximized to ensure the new system is as small and efficient as possible.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently only moderately insulated. Upgrading this to a "well-insulated" state will eliminate the remaining primary source of heat loss through the roof.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Generation Transition (Priority: Medium)
The current reliance on a boiler and electric convectors is the least efficient part of the home's operational performance.
2.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the building has no potential for geothermal drilling/captation and there is no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and sustainable replacement for the current generation system.

#### Phase 3: Emission System Optimization (Priority: Medium)
A heat pump's efficiency (COP) is heavily dependent on the temperature of the emission system.
3.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** While the building already has floor heating (which is low-temperature), the existing **Electric Convectors** must be replaced or transitioned to a low-temperature system. This ensures the heat pump operates at peak efficiency, further reducing the energy score.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Closes the final gap in the thermal envelope, reducing the total heat load. |
| **2** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces inefficient electric/boiler heating with a sustainable, high-efficiency generator. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Optimizes the heat pump's performance by removing inefficient electric convectors. |

**Final Note on Cost-Effectiveness:** Because the building is already highly efficient (EPC 50), the marginal gains of further insulation are smaller than in an older home. Therefore, the focus is shifted toward **electrification and system efficiency** (Heat Pump) to achieve the highest possible operational performance.
