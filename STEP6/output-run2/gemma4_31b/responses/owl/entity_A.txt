To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388, D Label) for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation must follow a **"Fabric First"** strategy. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating system, we ensure the new HVAC system is correctly sized and operates at peak efficiency.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant thermal leakage through the roof, walls, and floor. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment in terms of energy reduction.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. As heat rises, this is the most critical point of energy loss. Insulating the gable roof is the first priority.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves the thermal comfort of the living spaces.
*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and the wooden frames are in a "Good State," so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the heat demand is lowered, allowing for a transition to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the current radiators/emission systems should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is essential to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no heat grid available and no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
Sealing the building envelope with insulation reduces natural air exchange, which can lead to moisture issues if not addressed.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (such as System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor air quality while minimizing the loss of the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further improve the EPC label and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels is the final step to transition the home toward energy independence.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Reduces total energy demand and prevents heat leakage. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the building to utilize a heat pump efficiently. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the fossil-fuel boiler with a sustainable energy source. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains air quality in a now-sealed, energy-efficient envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Generates renewable energy to power the new electrical systems. |
