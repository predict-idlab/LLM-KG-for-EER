To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 165) for this 1862 detached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. Given the age of the property, the priority is to seal the thermal envelope to reduce the overall energy demand before upgrading the heating generation system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building's envelope has significant gaps. Since the roof is already well-insulated, the focus shifts to the walls and floors.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Right`, and `Wall_Rear`) are identified as cavity walls and are currently not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is the most cost-effective and least disruptive method to drastically reduce heat loss through the walls.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space (`FloorAboveCrawlSpace`) is a major source of cold drafts in a building of this era. Adding insulation here will improve thermal comfort and reduce the heating load.

#### Phase 2: Glazing Upgrade (Priority: High)
The current windows are a critical weak point in the thermal envelope.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the living area and bedroom windows currently utilize **single glazing**, which allows significant heat transfer. Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State," it is more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.

#### Phase 3: Transition to Sustainable Heating (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened envelope, the building can now operate with a lower-temperature heating system, making a heat pump viable.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a new heat pump, the existing emission system should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
The final phase ensures the building remains healthy and offsets the electricity used by the new HVAC system.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (System C or D) is necessary once the house is better insulated to prevent condensation and ensure indoor air quality.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for solar energy. Installing **PV panels on the roof** will provide renewable electricity to power the heat pump and ventilation system, further lowering the EPC score and operational costs.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Wall Cavity $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Stops the largest volume of heat loss via the building shell. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Eliminates the "cold spots" and high heat loss of single glazing. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emission $\rightarrow$ Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to a lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures air quality in a now-sealed, energy-efficient envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets electrical consumption with renewable energy. |
