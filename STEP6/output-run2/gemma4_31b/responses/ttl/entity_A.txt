### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the most immediate impact on the EPC score and thermal comfort.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Because heat rises, this is the highest priority for reducing energy consumption.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, **insulating the walls on the exterior** is the most effective way to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the energy score. (If budget or building lines are an issue, *insulating on the interior* is a cost-effective alternative).
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and removes the "cold floor" effect.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the total heat demand is lowered, allowing for a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature heating system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the current radiators should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This ensures that the subsequent heat pump can operate at its maximum efficiency (COP).

The final stage ensures the home remains healthy and offsets the electricity costs of the new heating system.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). After the heavy insulation in Phase 1, the house will be more airtight. Upgrading to a mechanical system (System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the new heat pump and ventilation system, further driving down the EPC score.
