The building is a detached house from 2023 with a net floor area of 159.0m² and an A-label EPC score of 50. The owner is motivated to improve the EPC rating. The building is well-insulated and has an existing PV panel installation. The current heating system consists of electric convection and floor heating.

Given the high insulation quality of the building and the presence of PV panels, the focus should be on optimizing the heating system and further improving the EPC score.

### Proposed Renovation Path

1. **Replace Glazing**: Although the windows are already double-glazed, upgrading to triple glazing would further improve thermal performance. This is a medium-priority intervention.

2. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump with Cooling**: The current emission system (electric convection and floor heating) can be complemented with an air-water heat pump with cooling functionality. This would significantly improve the EPC score and reduce energy consumption. This is a high-priority intervention.

3. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**: To maximize the efficiency of the new heat pump, the emission system should be adapted to work at lower temperatures. This is a medium-priority intervention.

4. **Place Sunshade**: Installing external sunshades can further reduce the cooling demand in summer, improving the overall energy efficiency. This is a low-priority intervention.

### Summary of Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Upgrade Glazing** | Improves thermal performance and reduces heat loss. |
| **2** | **Install Air-Water Heat Pump with Cooling** | Replaces inefficient electric heating with a more sustainable and efficient system. |
| **3** | **Adapt Emission System for Low-Temperature Operation** | Maximizes the efficiency of the new heat pump. |
| **4** | **Install Sunshades** | Reduces cooling demand and improves comfort. |

This plan prioritizes interventions that provide the most significant improvements in energy efficiency and EPC score while maintaining cost-effectiveness. The sequence ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before upgrading heating systems, preventing the need for oversized equipment.
