### Renovation Plan for the Detached Building (1862)

**Current State:**
- **Building Type:** Detached (single-family) from 1862
- **EPC Score:** 165 (D Label)
- **Envelope Condition:**
  - **Walls:** All four walls (Front, Rear, Left, Right) are cavity walls and **not insulated**.
  - **Roof:** Gable roof is **well insulated**.
  - **Windows:** All glazing is **single glazing**, which is inefficient.
  - **Floor:** Floor above crawl space is **not insulated**.
- **Heating System:** Current boiler system (type not specified) with radiators.
- **Ventilation:** System A (natural ventilation).
- **PV Potential:** Confirmed ("true"), but no panels currently installed.

**Owner’s Goal:** Improve EPC rating.

---

### Proposed Renovation Sequence

**Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)**
The primary focus is to **minimize heat loss** through the building fabric before upgrading the heating system. This reduces the overall energy demand and allows for a smaller, more efficient system.

1. **Insulate Wall Cavity**
   - **Action:** Insulate all four cavity walls (Front, Rear, Left, Right) from the inside.
   - **Why First?**
     - Cavity wall insulation is **non-disruptive** and **cost-effective**.
     - Reduces heat loss through the largest surface area of the building.
     - Required before installing a new heating system to prevent oversizing.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space**
   - **Action:** Add insulation to the floor above the crawl space.
   - **Why?**
     - Prevents heat loss to the unconditioned crawl space.
     - Eliminates "cold feet" and improves comfort.

3. **Replace Single Glazing**
   - **Action:** Replace all single-glazed windows with double or triple glazing.
   - **Why?**
     - Single glazing is a major source of heat loss.
     - Upgrading to double/triple glazing significantly improves thermal performance.

---

**Phase 2: Heating System Transition (Medium Priority)**
With a tighter envelope, the heating system can be upgraded to a more efficient, low-temperature option.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
   - **Action:** Replace the existing boiler with an **Air-Water Heat Pump**.
   - **Why?**
     - Geothermal potential is "false," and there is no heat grid.
     - Heat pumps are more energy-efficient than boilers and work well with insulated buildings.

5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**
   - **Action:** Upgrade the radiators to a low-temperature emission system.
   - **Why?**
     - Heat pumps operate most efficiently at lower flow temperatures.
     - Ensures compatibility with the new heat pump.

---

**Phase 3: Ventilation Upgrade (Low Priority)**
Improving air quality is essential in a now-sealed building.

6. **Replace Ventilation System**
   - **Action:** Upgrade from **Ventilation System A** (natural) to **Ventilation System C** (mechanical supply + natural exhaust).
   - **Why?**
     - System A is inefficient and does not ensure proper air exchange.
     - System C provides better control over humidity and air quality.

---

**Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Optional)**
To offset electricity consumption from the heat pump and further improve the EPC.

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**
   - **Action:** Install solar panels on the gable roof.
   - **Why?**
     - The building has confirmed PV potential.
     - Solar energy can power the heat pump and reduce reliance on the grid.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Walls** (Cavity) | Reduces the largest source of heat loss. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor** | Eliminates heat loss to the crawl space. |
| **3** | **Replace Glazing** | Improves thermal performance of windows. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the boiler with a more efficient system. |
| **5** | **Low-Temperature Emission** | Optimizes heat pump performance. |
| **6** | **Upgrade Ventilation** | Ensures air quality in a sealed building. |
| **7** | **PV Panels** (Optional) | Generates renewable electricity to power the heat pump. |

---

### Expected Outcome
By following this sequence, the building’s EPC score should improve from **D (165)** to **B or better**, significantly reducing energy costs and carbon footprint. The renovations prioritize **cost-effectiveness** and **long-term efficiency**, ensuring the owner achieves their goal of an improved EPC rating.
