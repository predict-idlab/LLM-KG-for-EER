### Analysis of the Building

The building in question is a detached structure constructed in 1862, with a net floor area of 136.0 m². It has an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) score of 165.0, placing it in the B label category. The primary motivation for renovation is to improve the EPC score.

Key characteristics:
1. **Structure**: Detached building.
2. **Walls**: All four walls (Front, Left, Rear, Right) are cavity walls that are not insulated.
3. **Roof**: Gable roof that is well insulated.
4. **Windows**: All windows have single glazing and wooden frames in good condition.
5. **Floors**: Floor above crawl space (basement) is not insulated.
6. **Ventilation**: Currently uses Ventilation System A (natural).
7. **Heating**: Uses a combination of air heating, electric convectors, floor heating, and radiators.
8. **Potential for PV**: True.
9. **Heat Grid**: Not present.

### Renovation Plan

Given the building's characteristics and the goal of improving the EPC score, the renovation should follow a "fabric-first" approach, focusing on improving the thermal envelope before upgrading heating systems. This ensures that the building's energy demand is minimized, making subsequent heating and ventilation upgrades more effective and cost-efficient.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization

1. **Insulate Wall Cavity**
   - **Action**: Insulate the cavity walls (Front, Left, Rear, Right).
   - **Rationale**: Cavity wall insulation is a cost-effective way to significantly improve the thermal performance of the building. Since the walls are not historically protected, this intervention is feasible.
   - **Note**: Ensure that the cavity is wide enough and free of contamination before proceeding.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space**
   - **Action**: Add insulation to the floor above the crawl space.
   - **Rationale**: Insulating this floor will reduce heat loss to the unconditioned crawl space, improving overall comfort and energy efficiency.

3. **Replace Glazing**
   - **Action**: Replace single glazing with double or triple glazing.
   - **Rationale**: Single glazing leads to significant heat loss. Upgrading to double or triple glazing will improve thermal performance and reduce drafts.

#### Phase 2: Heating System Upgrade

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
   - **Action**: Install an air-water heat pump to replace the existing heating system.
   - **Rationale**: Air-water heat pumps are efficient and suitable for buildings with low heat demand, which this building will have after insulation upgrades. They also reduce reliance on fossil fuels.

5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**
   - **Action**: Upgrade the emission system to operate at lower temperatures.
   - **Rationale**: This ensures compatibility with the heat pump and maximizes its efficiency.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation System Upgrade

6. **Replace Ventilation System**
   - **Action**: Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system, such as System C or D.
   - **Rationale**: A more efficient ventilation system will ensure good indoor air quality while minimizing energy losses.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**
   - **Action**: Install photovoltaic (PV) panels on the roof.
   - **Rationale**: Given the potential for PV, installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the heat pump and other electrical loads, further improving the EPC score.
