Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building constructed in 1862 with a current EPC score of 165 (B Label). Despite the relatively good EPC score, the building possesses several critical thermal weaknesses—most notably single glazing and uninsulated cavity walls.

To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC rating in a cost-effective manner, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. This strategy ensures the building's thermal envelope is optimized to reduce overall energy demand before upgrading the heating systems, which prevents the installation of oversized and inefficient equipment.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to eliminate the primary sources of heat loss. Since the building is an older structure with specific wall types, we focus on the most non-invasive and cost-effective insulation methods.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls are identified as `CavityWall` and are currently `NotInsulated`. Injecting insulation into these cavities is the most cost-effective way to significantly reduce heat loss through the walls without altering the building's exterior.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The building has a `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` (FloorAboveBasement). Insulating this area is essential to prevent cold air infiltration from the crawl space and to eliminate "cold feet" for the residents.
*Note: The roof is already `WellInsulated`, so no roof interventions are required.*

#### Phase 2: Glazing Upgrade (Medium Priority)
The current windows are a major source of thermal inefficiency.

3.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the bedrooms and living area currently have `SingleGlazing`. Because the `WoodenWindowFrame` is in a `GoodState`, it is far more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window units.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Medium Priority)
Once the walls, floor, and windows are sealed, the natural air exchange rate will drop, increasing the risk of moisture buildup.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current `VentilationSystemA` (natural ventilation) should be upgraded to a more modern system (such as System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor climate and prevents the loss of heat through uncontrolled drafts.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Heating Transition (Strategic Priority)
With a tightened envelope, the building's heat demand is now low enough to support high-efficiency, low-temperature heating.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the existing radiators/emitters should be upgraded to low-temperature versions. This is crucial to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and the site has no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 5: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable energy needed to power the new electrical systems, further driving down the EPC score.
