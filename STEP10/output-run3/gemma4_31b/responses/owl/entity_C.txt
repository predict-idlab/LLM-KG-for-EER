### Analysis of Building State
The building is a very modern **attached house (constructed in 2023)** with an exceptional energy profile. It already possesses an **EPC score of 50 (A+ Label)**, indicating that the thermal envelope is nearly optimized. 

**Current Strengths:**
*   **Thermal Envelope:** The walls, floor above the basement, and flat roof extension are already "Well Insulated."
*   **Windows:** All windows feature double glazing with wooden frames in a "Good State."
*   **Ventilation:** The building is equipped with a **Ventilation System D**, the most efficient type available.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

**Identified Weaknesses:**
*   **Gable Roof:** This is the only envelope element not fully optimized, currently rated as "Moderately Insulated."
*   **Heating System:** Despite the high EPC, the building uses a standard boiler and **electric convectors**, which are inefficient compared to modern heat pump systems. The data explicitly indicates a **reason to change the emission system**.

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Given that the building is already high-performing, the goal is not a "deep renovation" but rather a **technological upgrade** to transition from fossil-fuel/electric resistance heating to a sustainable, low-carbon system.

#### Phase 1: Final Envelope Polish (Priority: Low)
Before upgrading the heating generator, we ensure the building is as airtight and insulated as possible to minimize the required capacity of the new system.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** While the rest of the house is well-insulated, the gable roof is only "moderately" insulated. Upgrading this will eliminate the final significant point of heat loss.

#### Phase 2: Emission System Modernization (Priority: Medium)
The current use of electric convectors is inefficient. To prepare for a heat pump, the delivery of heat must be optimized.
2.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Transitioning from convectors to low-temperature emitters (such as underfloor heating or oversized radiators) is essential. This allows the subsequent heat pump to operate at a much higher Coefficient of Performance (COP).

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Generation Upgrade (Priority: High)
With a highly efficient envelope and low-temperature emitters, the building is now perfectly primed for a sustainable heat source.
3.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site lacks potential for geothermal drilling/captation and there is no heat grid available, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Completes the thermal envelope, ensuring maximum heat retention. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Necessary prerequisite to ensure the new heat pump operates efficiently. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches the energy source to a sustainable one, leveraging the existing PV panels for power. |

**Final Note on Cost-Effectiveness:** Because the building is nearly new and already an A+ label, these upgrades are "finishing touches." The focus is on removing the reliance on the boiler and electric convectors to reach a near-zero carbon footprint.
