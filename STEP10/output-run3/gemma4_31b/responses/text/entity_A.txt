To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388) for this 1951 semi-detached house, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the total heat demand of the building, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The priority is to eliminate heat leakage. Given the age of the home (1951) and the lack of insulation in key areas, this phase provides the most significant return on investment.

1.  **Insulate Pitched Roof:** The main roof currently has no insulation. Because heat rises, this is the highest priority. Insulating the roof will drastically reduce heat loss and immediately improve thermal comfort.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The solid brick walls are currently uninsulated. For a house of this era, **exterior wall insulation** is highly recommended to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the structure from moisture, though interior insulation is a more budget-friendly alternative.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents the "cold floor" effect and reduces energy loss to the lower level.
*Note: Since the windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition, replacing them is not necessary at this time, allowing budget to be allocated to insulation.*

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition
Now that the building is "tight" and retains heat effectively, the outdated heating system can be replaced with a low-temperature alternative.

4.  **Upgrade Heat Emission Systems:** Since the current radiators and air heating systems are in poor condition and need replacement, they should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (such as oversized radiators or expanded underfloor heating). This is crucial for the efficiency of the next step.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no district heating or geothermal potential, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable choice. Because the envelope is now insulated and emitters are low-temperature, the heat pump will operate at a much higher Coefficient of Performance (COP), lowering monthly electricity bills.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Increasing the airtightness of a home through insulation can lead to moisture buildup and poor indoor air quality if ventilation is not addressed.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (System C or D). This ensures a constant flow of fresh air without causing the massive heat loss associated with opening windows in winter.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the operational costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV panels, installing them will allow the homeowner to generate their own renewable electricity, further lowering the EPC score and reducing dependence on the grid.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the building's total energy demand. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the home to utilize a heat pump efficiently. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the old boiler with a sustainable, high-efficiency generator. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents humidity and mold in the newly sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the new systems. |
