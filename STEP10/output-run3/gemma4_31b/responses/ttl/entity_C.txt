### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Finalizing the Thermal Envelope (The Last Gap)
While most of the house is well-insulated, there is one remaining discrepancy in the building envelope.
1. **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` (Gable Roof) is currently only "Moderately Insulated," whereas the flat roof and walls are "Well Insulated." Upgrading the gable roof insulation will eliminate the primary remaining point of thermal leakage, ensuring a consistent thermal envelope across the entire structure.

#### Phase 2: Emission System Optimization
The building currently uses a mix of `FloorHeating` and `ElectricConvectors`. Electric convectors are notoriously inefficient and can negatively impact the EPC score due to high energy consumption per unit of heat.
2. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To move toward a more sustainable heating model, the reliance on electric convectors should be reduced. Transitioning all spaces to low-temperature emission systems (expanding the floor heating or adding low-temp radiators) is a prerequisite for a high-efficiency heat pump.

#### Phase 3: Generation System Upgrade
The building currently utilizes a standard `Boiler`. Given that the building is already highly insulated, it is the perfect candidate for a low-temperature heat source.
3. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient choice. Because the envelope is already tight (2023 build), the heat pump will operate at a very high Coefficient of Performance (COP), drastically reducing the energy required for heating.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy & Passive Cooling
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity used by the heat pump and ventilation system:
4. **Place PV-Panels in Garden:** The data indicates that `hasPotentialForPV` for the roof is `false` (or already maximized, as `PVPanels` are listed as an existing element). If the owner wishes to increase renewable production, installing panels in the garden is the only remaining viable option.
5. **Place Sunshade:** To prevent overheating in a highly insulated modern home (which can act like a thermos in summer), installing external sunshades is a cost-effective way to reduce the need for active cooling (air conditioning), thereby improving overall energy efficiency.
