To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating systems. This prevents the installation of oversized equipment and ensures maximum efficiency.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **not insulated**. Injecting insulation into the cavity is one of the most cost-effective ways to drastically reduce thermal leakage without altering the building's exterior appearance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is a significant source of cold drafts. Adding insulation here will improve ground-floor thermal comfort and reduce heat loss.
*Note: The gable roof is already "Well Insulated," so no further roof insulation is recommended at this time.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: High)
The existing windows are a major weakness in the thermal envelope.

3.  **Replace Glazing:** All windows currently utilize **single glazing**, which offers very poor thermal resistance. Since the wooden window frames are in a **"Good State" (Value: 8)**, it is more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window units.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the total heat demand will drop, allowing for a transition to a more sustainable generation system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the current emission system (radiators/convectors) should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions. This is crucial to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy (horizontal or vertical), an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Upgrading the envelope and glazing reduces natural air infiltration, which can lead to moisture issues if ventilation is not addressed.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses **Ventilation System A** (natural). This should be upgraded to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to maintain indoor air quality without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1 and 2.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity used by the new heat pump.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels on the roof will provide renewable energy to power the new HVAC and ventilation systems.
