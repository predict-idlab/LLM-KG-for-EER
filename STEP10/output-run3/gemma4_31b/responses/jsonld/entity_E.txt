To maximize energy efficiency and further improve the EPC score (currently 165) for this 1862 detached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating systems, we ensure that the new equipment is correctly sized and operates at maximum efficiency.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has a strong foundation (well-insulated roof), but significant heat is lost through the walls and windows. Addressing these is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are cavity walls and are currently not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is a high-impact, low-nuisance intervention that significantly reduces heat loss through the building's skin.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The property currently has single glazing in both the bedrooms and living area. Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State" (value 8), it is more cost-effective to **replace the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` should be insulated to prevent cold air from the crawl space from entering the living areas, eliminating thermal discomfort at the floor level.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
As the building becomes more airtight through wall and window upgrades, the natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to maintain air quality and prevent moisture buildup.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures a controlled flow of fresh air without losing the thermal gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 3: Transition to Sustainable Heating (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened envelope, the overall heat demand is reduced, allowing for a transition from the existing boiler to a high-efficiency electric system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump, the current emitters (radiators/convectors) should be transitioned to a low-temperature system.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no potential for geothermal drilling or access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, the building should generate its own power.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels is the final step to move the property toward a near-zero energy balance and drastically improve the EPC label.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Cavity Wall $\rightarrow$ Glazing $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Minimizes heat loss and reduces the required capacity of the heating system. |
| **2** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents humidity and ensures air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emission $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source optimized for the new lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **PV Panels** | Provides renewable energy to power the new electrical installations. |
