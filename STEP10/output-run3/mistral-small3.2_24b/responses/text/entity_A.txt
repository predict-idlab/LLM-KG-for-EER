### Renovation Plan for Semi-Detached House in Ghent, Belgium

Given your house's current condition (built in 1951, 149 m², EPC score of 388, uninsulated walls and floor, poorly insulated pitched roof, single-glazed windows, outdated heating systems, and potential for photovoltaic panels), the renovation should follow a **"Fabric-First"** approach. This ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before installing high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for an oversized and more expensive HVAC system.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)**
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building has several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Pitched Roof:**
   The pitched roof is currently uninsulated. This is a priority as heat rises; insulating the pitched roof is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
   - **Action:** Add insulation to the pitched roof (e.g., mineral wool or rigid foam insulation).

2. **Insulate Exterior Walls (Interior or Exterior Insulation):**
   The exterior walls are solid brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, interior or exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.
   - **Action:** Insulate the walls on the interior (more cost-effective) or exterior (better for thermal bridging but more expensive).

3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:**
   The floor above the basement is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the basement.
   - **Action:** Add insulation to the floor above the basement (e.g., rigid foam insulation).

#### **Phase 2: Window Performance**
The windows already have double glazing, which is good. However, if the frames are old or drafty, consider upgrading them.

4. **Replace Window Frames (if necessary):**
   If the wooden frames are in good condition, replacing them is not necessary. However, if they are drafty or poorly sealed, consider upgrading to modern, thermally broken frames.
   - **Action:** Replace window frames if they are not in good condition.

#### **Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition**
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.
   - **Action:** Install an air-water heat pump and replace the existing radiators with low-temperature emission systems (e.g., underfloor heating or low-temperature radiators) to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### **Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Upgrade Ventilation System:**
   Replace the natural ventilation system with a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.
   - **Action:** Install a mechanical ventilation system with heat recovery (MVHR).

#### **Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Install Photovoltaic Panels:**
   The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.
   - **Action:** Install photovoltaic panels on the roof.

---

### **Summary of Optimal Sequence**

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Window Frame Replacement (if necessary)** | Reduce the remaining thermal heat losses. |
| **3** | **Heat Pump Installation** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **Photovoltaic Panels** | Generates renewable energy to power the heat pump and ventilation system. |

This plan ensures that your home becomes more energy-efficient, cost-effective, and comfortable while significantly improving its EPC score.
