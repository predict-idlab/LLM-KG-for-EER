### Analysis of the Building's Current State

1. **Building Type and Construction Year**: The building is a semi-detached house constructed in 2017. It has a net floor area of 173.0 square meters.
2. **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)**: The current EPC score is 87.0, which falls under the A label (A+ label indicates scores less than 100 kWh/m²year). This is already a good rating, but there is room for improvement.
3. **Insulation**: Most of the building elements are well-insulated (walls, floor above basement, flat roof). The gable roof is moderately insulated.
4. **Windows**: The windows have double glazing, which is a good standard for energy efficiency.
5. **Heating and Emission System**: The building uses a heat pump for space heating, which is an efficient system.
6. **Ventilation**: The building has a ventilation system type D, which is a mechanical system for both supply and return air.
7. **PV Panels**: The building has solar panels installed, indicating a focus on renewable energy.
8. **Owner's Motivation**: The owner wants to improve the EPC further, suggesting a desire for higher energy efficiency.

### Potential Areas for Improvement

1. **Gable Roof Insulation**: The gable roof is currently moderately insulated. Improving its insulation could further enhance the building's thermal performance.
2. **Window Frames**: While the glazing is double, the frames are wooden. Depending on their condition and thermal properties, they might benefit from an upgrade to more thermally efficient materials like PVC or aluminum with thermal breaks.
3. **PV Panels**: Since the building already has PV panels, ensuring they are well-maintained and optimized for maximum efficiency could be beneficial.

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Given the building's already good EPC score and the owner's motivation to improve it further, the following steps are recommended:

1. **Insulate Gable Roof**: Since the gable roof is currently moderately insulated, improving its insulation could significantly enhance the building's thermal performance. This is a high-priority action as it addresses a major potential heat loss area.
2. **Upgrade Window Frames**: Although the windows have double glazing, the wooden frames might not be as thermally efficient as modern materials. Upgrading to PVC or aluminum frames with thermal breaks could improve overall energy efficiency.
3. **Optimize PV Panels**: Ensure the PV panels are functioning at peak efficiency. This might involve cleaning, checking for shading issues, or upgrading to more efficient panels if necessary.

### Detailed Renovation Path

1. **Insulate Gable Roof**
   - **Action**: Insulate the gable roof to improve its insulation grade from moderately insulated to well-insulated.
   - **Rationale**: The gable roof is a significant area for potential heat loss. Improving its insulation will enhance the overall thermal performance of the building.
   - **Expected Outcome**: Improved thermal comfort and reduced energy consumption for heating.

2. **Upgrade Window Frames**
   - **Action**: Replace the wooden window frames with more thermally efficient materials such as PVC or aluminum with thermal breaks.
   - **Rationale**: Modern window frames with thermal breaks can significantly reduce heat loss through the window frames, complementing the double glazing.
   - **Expected Outcome**: Enhanced thermal performance of the windows, leading to lower energy consumption for heating and cooling.

3. **Optimize PV Panels**
   - **Action**: Conduct a thorough inspection and maintenance of the existing PV panels to ensure they are operating at peak efficiency.
   - **Rationale**: Maximizing the efficiency of the PV panels will increase the renewable energy generation, reducing the building's reliance on grid electricity.
   - **Expected Outcome**: Increased renewable energy production, contributing to a better EPC score and lower energy costs.
