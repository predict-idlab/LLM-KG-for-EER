To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388, D Label) for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall energy demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment regarding energy reduction.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the most urgent priority to prevent thermal leakage.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** All three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. Because they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is recommended over interior insulation to eliminate thermal bridges and maximize the EPC improvement, especially since there is no historical protection.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the basement.
*Note: The `RoofExtension` is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state; therefore, these elements do not require intervention at this stage.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the energy required to maintain a comfortable temperature drops significantly. This allows us to move away from the traditional boiler.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the existing radiators and heating system should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions first.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable generation system to replace the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Increasing the insulation levels reduces the natural air exchange rate of the house. To prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D).

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the energy score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Stops the majority of heat loss; lowers the required capacity of the heating system. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house for high-efficiency heat generation. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures healthy indoor air quality in a now-airtight thermal envelope. |
| **5** | **PV-Panels Installation** | Reduces operational costs and carbon footprint via renewable energy. |
