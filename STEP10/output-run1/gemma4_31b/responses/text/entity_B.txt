**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided data, this is an attached house from 1832 with an EPC score of 262. Given the age of the building and the current energy performance, the most cost-effective strategy is a **"Fabric-First" approach**. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall heating demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient, and less expensive heating system.

The proposed renovation path follows the sequence: **Insulate $\rightarrow$ Upgrade Emission $\rightarrow$ Transition Heat Source $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate.**

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate heat leakage. In a house from 1832, the solid brick walls and uninsulated floors are the primary sources of thermal loss.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Heat rises rapidly; since the flat roof is currently uninsulated, this is the most immediate priority to prevent significant energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. Since the house is not historically protected, **exterior wall insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the brickwork from moisture.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and stops heat from escaping into the basement.
*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition, so replacing them is not recommended at this time to remain cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is insulated, the internal temperature will remain more stable, and the heat demand will drop significantly.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems (Low-Temperature):** The current radiators are in poor condition. They should be replaced with **low-temperature emission systems** (such as underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators). This is a critical prerequisite for the efficiency of a heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** With the heat demand lowered and low-temp emitters in place, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and sustainable replacement for the old boiler, as geothermal and district heating are unavailable.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Insulating a 19th-century house effectively "seals" the building. To prevent humidity buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). This ensures controlled air exchange without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further improve the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof is the final step to move the home toward energy independence.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents moisture and maintains air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Generates renewable energy to power the new electrical systems. |
