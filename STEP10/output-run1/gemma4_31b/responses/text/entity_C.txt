Based on the provided building data, this is a very modern home (built in 2023) with an already excellent energy performance certificate (EPC score of 50). The thermal envelope is largely optimized, and high-efficiency systems like Ventilation System D and PV panels are already in place.

Since the "Fabric First" approach is already mostly realized, the focus of this renovation is not on sealing the building, but on **optimizing the heating generation and emission systems** to eliminate the inefficiency of electric convectors and the aging boiler.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Final Envelope Optimization (Priority: Low/Medium)
While the house is well-insulated, there is one remaining area for improvement to ensure the heating system can operate at the lowest possible temperature.
1. **Enhance Pitched Roof Insulation:** The pitched roof currently has "moderate" insulation, whereas the rest of the house is "well insulated." Upgrading this to a high level of insulation will eliminate the primary remaining point of heat loss, further reducing the load on the new heating system.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: High)
The current heating setup is the primary weakness of the home. The combination of a boiler and electric convectors is inefficient and costly to operate.
2. **Replace Boiler with Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and district heating are not options, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most logical upgrade. Because the house is already well-insulated and has PV panels to offset electricity costs, a heat pump will operate with a very high Coefficient of Performance (COP).
3. **Eliminate Electric Convectors:** Electric convectors are among the most inefficient ways to heat a home. These should be removed entirely.
4. **Optimize Underfloor Heating:** Since underfloor heating is already installed, it is perfectly suited for the low-temperature output of a heat pump. The system should be balanced and integrated with the new heat pump to ensure uniform heat distribution throughout the 159 m² area.

#### Phase 3: System Integration and Monitoring (Priority: Long-term)
Given the modern nature of the house, the final step is ensuring the existing systems work in harmony.
5. **Smart Thermostat Integration:** To maximize the efficiency of the new heat pump and the existing Ventilation System D, a smart zoning system should be implemented to ensure energy is only used where and when needed.
