To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388) in a cost-effective manner, the renovation should follow a **"Fabric-First"** approach. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the total heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal is to eliminate heat leakage. Given the 1951 construction and lack of insulation in key areas, this phase provides the most significant return on investment.

1.  **Insulate Main Roof:** The pitched roof currently has no insulation. Since heat rises, this is the highest priority. Insulating the roof is the most effective way to immediately lower the energy demand.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The solid brick walls are currently uninsulated. To prevent thermal bridging and heat loss, **interior wall insulation** is often the most cost-effective for semi-detached homes (to avoid altering the shared facade), though exterior insulation is thermally superior if budget permits.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and stops heat from escaping into the basement.
*Note: The flat-roof extension already has moderate insulation, so it is a lower priority than the main roof.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition
Once the house is sealed and insulated, the energy required to keep it warm drops significantly. This allows us to move away from the inefficient boiler.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The current radiators and air heating systems are in poor condition. These should be replaced with **low-temperature emission systems** (such as oversized radiators or expanded underfloor heating) to ensure the new heat pump can operate at maximum efficiency (COP).
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Because geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient choice to replace the old boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
As the building envelope is tightened through insulation, the natural air exchange rate will decrease. To prevent humidity buildup and ensure a healthy living environment, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). This ensures controlled air circulation without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, the final step is to generate clean energy on-site.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will further lower the EPC score and reduce long-term operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate with a low-temperature heat source. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable generator tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents moisture and maintains air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets electricity costs and maximizes the EPC improvement. |

**Note on Windows:** Since the windows already have double glazing and the frames are in good condition, replacing them is **not recommended** at this stage, as it would not be cost-effective compared to the other upgrades.
