### Analysis of the Building's Current State

The building is a **detached house** constructed in **1862** with an **EPC score of 165** (B Label). The key characteristics and issues are:

1. **Insulation Issues**:
   - **External Walls**: All four walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are either **cavity walls** or **full brick walls** with **no insulation**.
   - **Roof**: The main gable roof (`RoofMain`) is well insulated, but there is no information about the flat roof section.
   - **Floor**: The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is uninsulated.

2. **Windows**:
   - **Living Area**: Single glazing with wooden frames.
   - **Bedrooms**: Single glazing with wooden frames.

3. **Heating System**:
   - Current system: **Electric Convector** (floor heating).
   - **Ventilation System**: Type A (natural ventilation).

4. **Other**:
   - The building is **historically unprotected**.
   - There is **no heat grid** nearby.
   - The site has **no potential for geothermal energy**.
   - **Potential for PV installation** is confirmed.

### Prioritization of Renovation Steps

To maximize energy efficiency while keeping costs manageable, the renovation must follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before sealing the envelope leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

### Proposed Renovation Plan

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is old and has several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Cavity Walls**:
   - **Insulate Wall on Exterior**: Since the walls are cavity walls, **injecting insulation into the cavity** is the most cost-effective method. This prevents thermal bridges and significantly reduces heat loss.
   - **Insulate Wall on Interior**: If cavity injection is not feasible (e.g., due to contamination), **insulating the interior** is the next best option.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space**:
   - The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here eliminates cold drafts from the basement and improves overall thermal comfort.

3. **Insulate Flat Roof** (if present):
   - If there is a flat roof section, it should be insulated to prevent heat loss through the top of the building.

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: Medium)
The window performance should be enhanced, as single glazing leads to significant heat losses.

4. **Replace Glazing**:
   - Replace all single-glazed windows with **double or triple glazing** to drastically improve thermal performance and reduce heat loss.

#### Phase 3: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**:
   - Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing electric convector.

6. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature**:
   - Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

7. **Replace Ventilation System**:
   - Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

8. **Place PV-Panels on Roof**:
   - The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Cavity Walls** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house by eliminating thermal bridges. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space** | Prevents cold drafts from the basement and improves thermal comfort. |
| **3** | **Replace Glazing** | Reduces the remaining thermal heat losses through windows. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **5** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Maximizes the efficiency of the new heat pump. |
| **6** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **7** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the heat pump and ventilation system. |

This plan ensures that the building is renovated in a cost-effective and energy-efficient manner, improving its EPC label and reducing long-term energy costs.
