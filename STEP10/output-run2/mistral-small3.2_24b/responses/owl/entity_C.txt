### Renovation Plan for Attached Building (2023)

**Current Situation:**
- **Building Type:** Attached (row house)
- **Construction Year:** 2023 (newly constructed)
- **EPC Score:** 50 (A label)
- **Current Heating System:** Electric convectors + floor heating
- **Ventilation System:** System D (mechanical supply and exhaust)
- **Windows:** Double-glazed, wooden frames in good condition
- **Insulation:** Walls and roof are well-insulated (grade: WellInsulated)
- **PV Potential:** False (no potential for PV panels)
- **Heat Grid:** False (no connection to district heating)

**Owner's Motivation:**
- **Primary Goal:** Improve EPC (already excellent at 50)

**Key Observations:**
1. The building is already well-insulated and has efficient double-glazed windows.
2. The heating system is electric, which is not sustainable or cost-effective.
3. The ventilation system is modern (System D).
4. There is no potential for PV panels or connection to a heat grid.

**Proposed Renovation Path:**

**Phase 1: Heating System Upgrade (Priority: High)**
Since the building is already well-insulated and has efficient windows, the primary focus should be on upgrading the heating system to a more sustainable and cost-effective solution.

1. **Replace Electric Heating with Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - **Rationale:** Electric heating is expensive and not sustainable. An air-water heat pump can provide both heating and cooling, reducing energy consumption and costs.
   - **Action:** Install an air-water heat pump to replace the existing electric convectors and floor heating.
   - **Expected Impact:** Significant reduction in energy consumption and improvement in comfort.

**Phase 2: Emission System Optimization (Priority: Medium)**
With a new heat pump, the emission system should be optimized to ensure efficient heat distribution.

2. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:**
   - **Rationale:** Heat pumps work most efficiently at lower temperatures. Optimizing the emission system ensures that the heat pump operates at its highest Coefficient of Performance (COP).
   - **Action:** Adjust the radiators or underfloor heating system to operate at lower temperatures.
   - **Expected Impact:** Improved heat pump efficiency and lower energy consumption.

**Phase 3: Ventilation System Check (Priority: Low)**
The ventilation system is already modern (System D), so no immediate action is required. However, regular maintenance is recommended to ensure optimal performance.

**Phase 4: Future-Proofing (Optional)**
If the owner is interested in future upgrades, they could consider:
- **Solar Thermal Panels:** If space allows, solar thermal panels could be installed to pre-heat water, further reducing energy consumption.
- **Battery Storage:** If the owner decides to install PV panels in the future, a battery storage system could be added to store excess energy for use during peak times.

**Summary of Optimal Sequence:**
1. **Air-Water Heat Pump Installation**
2. **Low-Temperature Emission System Adjustment**
3. **Optional Future Upgrades (Solar Thermal, Battery Storage)**

This plan focuses on maximizing energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness while aligning with the owner's motivation to improve the already excellent EPC score. The proposed upgrades prioritize sustainability and long-term energy savings.
