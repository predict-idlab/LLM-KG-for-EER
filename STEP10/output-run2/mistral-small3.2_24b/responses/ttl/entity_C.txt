### Renovation Plan for the Provided Entity

Given the current state of the building (an attached structure from 2023 with an EPC score of 50.0), the renovation plan should focus on maintaining and enhancing the already high energy efficiency while ensuring cost-effectiveness. The building is already well-insulated and equipped with double glazing, a ventilation system (Type D), and solar panels, indicating a strong foundation for energy efficiency.

#### **Current Strengths:**
1. **Well-Insulated Envelope:** All walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`), the `FloorAboveBasement`, and the `RoofExtension` are well-insulated.
2. **Double Glazing:** Windows have double glazing, reducing heat loss.
3. **Modern Ventilation System (Type D):** Ensures good air quality and moisture control.
4. **Solar Panels:** Contribute to renewable energy generation.
5. **Low-Energy Emission System:** The use of electric convectors and floor heating suggests a modern, efficient setup.

#### **Areas for Potential Improvement:**
1. **Moderately Insulated Gable Roof (`RoofMain`):** This could be a weak point for heat loss.
2. **Heating System Transition:** The building currently uses electric convectors and floor heating, which may not be the most efficient for space heating.
3. **Solar Panel Optimization:** Ensuring the solar panels are functioning optimally and considering additional renewable energy sources if feasible.

#### **Proposed Renovation Path:**
1. **Insulate Gable Roof (`RoofMain`):**
   - **Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the gable roof to match the well-insulated status of other elements.
   - **Rationale:** Eliminates potential thermal bridges and improves overall thermal performance.
   - **Cost:** €60 per m² (low nuisance level).

2. **Replace Glazing (if necessary):**
   - **Action:** Assess the condition of the double glazing. If there is significant wear or damage, consider upgrading to triple glazing for even better thermal performance.
   - **Rationale:** Enhances insulation further and reduces heat loss through windows.
   - **Cost:** €200 per m² (high nuisance level, but justified if significant improvements are needed).

3. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump with Cooling:**
   - **Action:** Replace the current electric convectors and floor heating with an air-water heat pump that includes cooling functionality.
   - **Rationale:** More energy-efficient and sustainable than electric heating, especially with the existing solar panels offsetting electricity consumption.
   - **Cost:** €9,000 fixed price + €35 per m² (moderate nuisance level).

4. **Optimize Solar Panel Performance:**
   - **Action:** Review the performance of the existing solar panels and consider adding more panels or optimizing their placement if the potential for PV is still underutilized.
   - **Rationale:** Maximizes renewable energy generation to support the heat pump and other electrical needs.
   - **Cost:** €275 per m² (low nuisance level).

5. **Place Sunshade:**
   - **Action:** Install external sunshades to reduce solar gain in summer and improve cooling efficiency.
   - **Rationale:** Complements the cooling functionality of the heat pump and reduces reliance on active cooling.
   - **Cost:** €750 (low nuisance level).

#### **Summary of Sequence and Rationale:**
| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Eliminates thermal bridges and improves overall insulation. |
| **2** | **Replace Glazing (if necessary)** | Enhances thermal performance of windows. |
| **3** | **Place Air-Water Heat Pump with Cooling** | Transition to a more efficient and sustainable heating/cooling system. |
| **4** | **Optimize Solar Panel Performance** | Maximizes renewable energy generation to support the heat pump. |
| **5** | **Place Sunshade** | Reduces solar gain and improves cooling efficiency. |

#### **Additional Considerations:**
- **Permits and Regulations:** Check local regulations for any required permits, especially for structural changes like roof insulation.
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Prioritize actions based on cost-effectiveness and potential energy savings. For example, insulating the gable roof may offer a higher return on investment than replacing glazing if the existing double glazing is in good condition.
- **Future-Proofing:** Consider the long-term benefits of each upgrade, such as the ability to adapt to future energy regulations or climate changes.

This plan ensures that the building maintains its high energy efficiency while addressing any remaining weaknesses in the thermal envelope and heating system. The focus is on practical, cost-effective upgrades that maximize long-term operational performance.
