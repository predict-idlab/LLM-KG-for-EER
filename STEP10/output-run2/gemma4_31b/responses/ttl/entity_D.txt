### Analysis of Current Building State
The building is a modern semi-detached house (constructed in 2017) that already exhibits an excellent energy profile. With an **EPC score of 87**, it currently falls within the **A Label** category. Most of the high-impact energy efficiency measures have already been implemented:
*   **Heating:** Already utilizes a high-efficiency Heat Pump.
*   **Ventilation:** Already equipped with Ventilation System D (the most advanced mechanical system).
*   **Envelope:** Walls, the flat roof extension, and the floor above the basement are all "Well Insulated."
*   **Windows:** Recently updated to double glazing (2020) with frames in good condition.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

Because the building is already highly optimized, the focus shifts from "major renovation" to "fine-tuning" to push the EPC score even lower (potentially toward an A+ label).

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Since the building is already in the top tier of energy performance, a "fabric-first" approach remains the priority, but the scope is limited to the few remaining areas of moderate performance.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Refinement
The only building element not classified as "Well Insulated" is the main gable roof.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently "Moderately Insulated." Improving this to a "Well Insulated" state is the most effective way to reduce the remaining heat loss, as heat continues to rise through the highest point of the structure. This is the most cost-effective remaining upgrade to improve the EPC score.

#### Phase 2: High-Performance Glazing (Optional/Long-term)
While the windows were updated in 2020 to double glazing, there is still a marginal gain to be made.
2.  **Replace Glazing (Upgrade to Triple Glazing):** To move the EPC score from an A toward an A+ (below 100 kWh/m²year), upgrading from double to triple glazing would further minimize thermal bridging and improve interior thermal comfort. Given the frames are in a "Good State," only the glazing units would need replacement, making this more cost-effective than replacing entire windows.

#### Phase 3: Energy Generation Optimization
The building already has PV panels and the site is listed as having no further potential for new large-scale PV installations.
3.  **Maintain and Monitor:** No new installation of heat pumps or ventilation is required. The focus should be on the operational performance of the existing Heat Pump and Ventilation System D to ensure they are calibrated for the new, tighter envelope.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | Addresses the only "Moderately Insulated" element in the envelope. |
| **2** | **Upgrade to Triple Glazing** | The final step to maximize the EPC score and push the building toward A+ status. |
| **3** | **System Calibration** | Ensures the existing high-efficiency systems operate optimally with the improved insulation. |

**Final Verdict:** The building is already an energy-efficient model. The proposed path is minimal and targeted, ensuring that the owner does not over-invest in systems they already possess, but rather closes the small remaining gaps in the thermal envelope.
