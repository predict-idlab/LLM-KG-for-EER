**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is an attached building from 1832 with an EPC score of 262 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating. 

To maximize energy efficiency and ensure cost-effectiveness, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first ensures that any subsequent heating system is not oversized and operates at maximum efficiency.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, insulating this flat section is a high-priority, high-impact intervention.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Given they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and drastically reduce the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the basement.
*Note: The main gable roof is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state; therefore, no actions are proposed for these elements to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building envelope is sealed, the total heat demand is significantly lower, allowing for a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The current radiators are designed for high-temperature boilers. To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump, the emission system should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions first.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Insulating an old building increases its airtightness. To prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality, the ventilation must be modernized.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to ensure healthy air exchange without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, and to further lower the EPC score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new electrical installations.
