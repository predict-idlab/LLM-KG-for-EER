### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system is appropriately sized and operates at maximum efficiency.

Injecting insulation into the cavities is the most cost-effective method for this wall type, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is a significant source of heat loss in older homes. Adding insulation here will eliminate drafts and improve the overall thermal comfort of the ground floor.
*Note: The Gable Roof is already "Well Insulated," so no further roof insulation is required.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: High)
The current windows are a major weak point in the thermal envelope.

Since the wooden window frames are in a **"Good State" (value 8)**, it is more cost-effective to replace only the glazing (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.

With the envelope sealed and heat loss minimized, the building can now transition from an old boiler to a sustainable system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the existing emission system (radiators/convectors) should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is crucial for maximizing the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Upgrading the envelope and glazing increases the airtightness of the home, which can lead to moisture issues if ventilation is not addressed.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current **Ventilation System A** (natural ventilation) should be replaced with a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1 and 2.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump.
