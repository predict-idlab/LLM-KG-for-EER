To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 165) for this 1862 detached house, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating system, we reduce the overall energy demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heat pump.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to minimize heat loss through the building's skin. This is the most cost-effective way to lower energy consumption.

1.  **Cavity Wall Insulation:** Since all four exterior walls are cavity walls and currently uninsulated, this is the "low-hanging fruit." Injecting insulation into the cavities is significantly cheaper and less disruptive than interior or exterior cladding, providing a massive boost in thermal retention.
2.  **Floor Insulation (Crawl Space):** The floor above the crawl space is likely a major source of heat loss and cold drafts. Since the space is accessible, insulating the ceiling of the crawl space (the underside of the floor) is a highly effective way to improve comfort and efficiency.
3.  **Window Glazing Upgrade:** Single glazing acts as a "thermal hole" in the house. Since the wooden frames are in good condition, the priority is to replace the single glazing with high-performance double or triple glazing to eliminate drafts and reduce radiative heat loss.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the house is sealed and insulated, the total heat load is significantly reduced, making low-temperature heating viable.

4.  **Replace Heat Emission Systems:** The existing radiators, air heating, and electric convectors are in poor condition. These should be replaced with low-temperature emitters (such as updated underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators). This is a prerequisite for the heat pump to operate at maximum efficiency.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable replacement for the old boiler. Because the envelope was optimized in Phase 1, the heat pump can be sized smaller, reducing both installation and operational costs.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Essential Integration)
A well-insulated house is a sealed house. To prevent moisture buildup, mold, and poor indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (such as System C or D). A System D (balanced ventilation with heat recovery) would be the gold standard, as it recovers heat from outgoing air to warm the incoming fresh air.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Sustainability)
The final step is to offset the electricity required to run the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the property has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will lower monthly utility bills and further improve the EPC rating by generating carbon-free electricity on-site.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Cavity Wall & Floor Insulation** | Most cost-effective way to drastically reduce heat demand. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Eliminates the primary remaining points of thermal leakage. |
| **3** | **Emission System Upgrade** | Prepares the house for low-temperature heating. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new low demand. |
| **5** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures air quality and prevents dampness in a sealed envelope. |
| **6** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets the electricity cost of the new HVAC systems. |
