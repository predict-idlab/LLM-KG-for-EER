Based on the provided building data, the property is a semi-detached house from 1951 with an EPC score of 388. To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. This means we optimize the building's shell to minimize heat loss before upgrading the heating systems, ensuring that the new HVAC system is correctly sized and operates at maximum efficiency.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: Critical)
The building has significant thermal bridges and heat leakage points. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment by drastically reducing the total energy demand.

1.  **Insulate Main Pitched Roof:** The main roof currently has no insulation. Since heat rises, this is the most critical priority to prevent massive heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The solid brick walls are currently uninsulated. Given the age of the house (1951), **interior wall insulation** is often a cost-effective choice, though exterior insulation is more thermally efficient if the facade allows.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here eliminates the "cold floor" effect and prevents heat from escaping into the basement.
*Note: The flat-roof extension already has moderate insulation, so it is a lower priority than the main roof and walls.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Upgrade (Priority: High)
Once the envelope is sealed, the house will require significantly less energy to stay warm. This allows for a transition from a high-temperature boiler to a low-temperature sustainable system.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The current radiators and air heating systems are in poor condition. These should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (e.g., oversized radiators or expanded underfloor heating) to maximize the efficiency of a heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since district heating and geothermal energy are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable alternative to the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
As the building becomes more airtight through insulation, the existing natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from natural ventilation to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). A System D (balanced ventilation with heat recovery) would be the most energy-efficient choice as it recovers heat from outgoing air.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, the property should move toward energy autonomy.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the home's new energy-efficient infrastructure.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
