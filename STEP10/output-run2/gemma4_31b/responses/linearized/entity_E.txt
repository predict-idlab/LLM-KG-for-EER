### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The building has significant heat loss through its walls and floor. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system is sized correctly and operates at maximum efficiency.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Rear, Left, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into these cavities is the most cost-effective way to drastically reduce thermal transmission without altering the building's facade.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is currently not insulated. Insulating this area prevents cold air from the crawl space from entering the living zones, eliminating drafts and improving thermal comfort.

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Medium Priority)
The current windows in both the bedrooms and the living area use **Single Glazing**, which acts as a major thermal bridge.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Since the wooden window frames are noted to be in a **"Good State,"** it is more cost-effective to replace only the glass (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.

#### Phase 3: Transition to High-Efficiency Heating (Medium Priority)
With the building envelope sealed, the total heat demand is lowered, allowing for a transition from the current boiler to a sustainable system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the emission system should be upgraded to low-temperature emitters. This is critical to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Renewable Energy (Long-term Priority)
Once the home is airtight and energy-efficient, the focus shifts to maintaining air quality and offsetting electricity costs.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (such as System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup and ensure a healthy indoor climate in a well-insulated home.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the new heat pump and ventilation system, further lowering the EPC score.
