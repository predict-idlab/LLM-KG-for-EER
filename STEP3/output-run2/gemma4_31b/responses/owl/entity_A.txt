To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, we will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading heating and ventilation systems. 

The building currently has an EPC score of **388 (D label)**. The owner's primary motivation is to improve this EPC rating.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Reducing Heat Loss)
The building's current state shows significant gaps in insulation. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system is sized correctly and operates at peak efficiency.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises. 
    *   *Action:* **Insulate gable roof**.
    *   *Rationale:* Low nuisance level and high impact on energy retention.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front, rear, and right walls are full brick and not insulated. 
    *   *Action:* **Insulate wall on exterior**.
    *   *Rationale:* While it has a higher nuisance level, exterior insulation is more effective than interior insulation for full brick walls and prevents thermal bridging.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated.
    *   *Action:* **Insulate floor above basement**.
    *   *Rationale:* Prevents cold drafts and heat loss into the basement, improving ground-floor thermal comfort.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is "sealed" with insulation, the existing natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient and could lead to moisture issues.

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation:** 
    *   *Action:* **Replace ventilation system**.
    *   *Rationale:* Moving to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) ensures healthy indoor air quality and prevents condensation in the now-insulated envelope.

#### Phase 3: Energy Generation and Emission
With the heat demand significantly lowered by Phase 1, we can now transition from the old boiler to a sustainable energy source.

5.  **Upgrade Heating System:** The building has a reason to change its emission system, but geothermal and heat grids are not viable options for this site.
    *   *Action:* **Place air-water heat pump**.
    *   *Rationale:* Since the building already possesses radiators and floor heating, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient replacement for a traditional boiler.
6.  **Optimize Emission:** 
    *   *Action:* **Place emission system on low temperature**.
    *   *Rationale:* Heat pumps operate most efficiently at lower flow temperatures. Adjusting the radiators/floor heating to low-temperature settings maximizes the COP (Coefficient of Performance).

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

7.  **Install Solar Energy:** The building has confirmed potential for PV.
    *   *Action:* **Place PV-panels on roof**.
    *   *Rationale:* This reduces operational costs and provides the final push to move the EPC label from **D** toward **A or B**.

### Summary of Sequence & Logic

| Step | Action | Priority | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Roof & Walls** | Critical | Stop heat leakage first to lower energy demand. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor** | High | Complete the thermal envelope. |
| **3** | **Upgrade Ventilation** | Medium | Prevent moisture/mold after sealing the building. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Medium | Replace inefficient boiler with a sustainable alternative. |
| **5** | **Low-Temp Emission** | Medium | Optimize the heat pump's efficiency. |
| **6** | **PV Panels** | Long-term | Offset energy costs with renewable generation. |

**Estimated Result:** This sequence minimizes the risk of over-investing in a heating system that is too large for a well-insulated home and provides the most direct path to a significantly improved EPC label.
