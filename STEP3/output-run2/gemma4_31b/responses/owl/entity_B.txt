To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation should follow the "Fabric First" principle: reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading technical systems and adding renewable energy.

Based on the building's current state (EPC C, uninsulated walls and floor) and the provided ontology, here is the optimal renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Reducing Heat Loss)
The building is an "Attached Building" from 1832. While the main gable roof is moderately insulated, there are significant gaps in the thermal shell.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Ordering: 25):** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority, high-impact upgrade to prevent heat from escaping through the top of the extension.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls (Ordering: 40):** Both the `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Since there is no historical protection, **exterior insulation** is the most effective choice to eliminate thermal bridging and significantly lower the EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement (Ordering: 49):** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement, improving ground-floor thermal comfort.

*Note: Window replacement is excluded from this plan as the existing wooden frames and double glazing are in "Good State," making replacement cost-ineffective at this stage.*

### Phase 2: System Modernization (Improving Efficiency)
Once the building is airtight and insulated, the heating and ventilation systems can be downsized and optimized.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System (Ordering: 52):** The building currently uses a Type A system (natural). Upgrading to a more modern system (e.g., Type C or D) is essential after insulating the walls and roof to ensure healthy indoor air quality and prevent condensation.
5.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission (Ordering: 70):** Before installing a new heat pump, the existing radiators should be transitioned to a low-temperature system. This ensures the heat pump operates at maximum efficiency (COP).
6.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump (Ordering: 67):** Since the building has a reason to change its emission system and geothermal options are not possible at this site, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective replacement for the current boiler.

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy Integration (Reducing Operational Costs)
The final step is to offset the remaining energy consumption with on-site generation.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof (Ordering: 85):** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels on the roof will provide the electricity needed to run the new heat pump, drastically reducing long-term operational costs and further improving the EPC label.
