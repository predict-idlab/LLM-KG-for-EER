Given that your home was built in 2017 and already features a heat pump, Ventilation System D, and photovoltaic panels, you are starting from a very strong baseline. An EPC score of 87 is already quite efficient for a house of this size in Belgium.

To further improve the energy performance while remaining cost-effective, we must move from "major corrections" to "marginal gains." The goal is to eliminate the few remaining thermal bridges and optimize the synergy between your energy production and consumption.

Here is the proposed renovation path, ordered by the optimal sequence for maximum ROI.

### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Priority)
The only "moderate" point in your building envelope is the pitched roof. Since heat rises, this is your primary area for energy loss.

*   **Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the pitched roof.
*   **Detail:** Depending on the current construction, this can be done via **blown-in cellulose or mineral wool** (if there is a cavity) or by adding a layer of high-performance insulation boards under the eaves.
*   **Why:** Moving from "moderate" to "well-insulated" on the roof will have the most immediate impact on your EPC score and reduce the workload on your heat pump during winter.

### Step 2: Air-Tightness & Thermal Bridge Audit
Even in 2017 builds, small leaks can occur around window frames or utility penetrations, which can undermine the efficiency of a Ventilation System D.

*   **Action:** Conduct a professional **Blower Door Test** or a thermographic scan.
*   **Detail:** Identify specific "cold spots" or air leaks. Use professional-grade sealing tapes or expanding foam to close gaps around pipes and window junctions.
*   **Why:** This is a low-cost intervention. Ensuring the house is airtight allows your Ventilation System D to work exactly as designed, recovering maximum heat from the exhaust air.

### Step 3: Intelligent Energy Management (The "Smart" Layer)
Since you already have PV panels and a heat pump, the focus should shift from *saving* energy to *optimizing* when you use it.

*   **Action:** Install a **Smart Energy Management System (HEMS)** and optimize heat pump settings.
*   **Detail:** 
    *   Implement "load shifting": program the heat pump to pre-heat the house or the hot water tank during peak PV production hours.
    *   If your budget allows, consider a **home battery system** to store excess summer energy for winter use (though the ROI on batteries is longer, it improves operational autonomy).
*   **Why:** This maximizes the "self-consumption" rate of your PV panels, reducing your reliance on the grid and lowering operational costs.

### Step 4: Glazing Refinement (Low-Cost/High-Impact)
Your windows are from 2020 and in good condition, so replacing them with triple glazing would be **cost-ineffective** (the ROI would be too low). Instead, focus on the "external skin."

*   **Action:** Install high-quality **external solar shading** (e.g., adjustable shutters or screens).
*   **Detail:** Focus on south- and west-facing windows to prevent overheating in the summer.
*   **Why:** Reducing solar gain in summer prevents the need for active cooling (which is energy-intensive), maintaining a stable internal temperature and improving overall energy performance.
