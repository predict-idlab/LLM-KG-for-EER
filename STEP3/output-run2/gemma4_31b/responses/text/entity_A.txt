To improve your energy performance certificate (EPC) from a score of 388 and ensure long-term cost-effectiveness, you must follow a **"Fabric First"** approach. This means we prioritize the building envelope (insulation) before upgrading the heating system. Installing a modern heat pump in an uninsulated 1951 house would lead to an oversized, expensive system that performs poorly.

Since the property is in Ghent (Flanders), I highly recommend checking the **"Mijn VerbouwPremie"** (My Renovation Premium) to subsidize these specific steps.

Here is the optimal renovation sequence:

### Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Stopping the Heat Loss)
*Goal: Reduce the energy demand of the house so that a smaller, cheaper heating system can be used.*

1.  **Roof Insulation (Highest Priority):** Heat rises. Since your pitched roof is uninsulated, this is your biggest source of heat loss. 
    *   **Action:** Insulate the pitched roof (either via the rafters or by creating a suspended ceiling). Ensure the flat-roof extension is topped up to modern standards if possible.
2.  **Wall Insulation:** Solid brick walls from 1951 have virtually no thermal resistance.
    *   **Action:** Since the house is not historically protected, **External Wall Insulation (EWI)** is the gold standard. It eliminates thermal bridges and doesn't reduce interior living space. If budget is a constraint, consider cavity wall insulation (if a cavity exists) or internal insulation (though this risks condensation).
3*  **Floor Insulation:** The uninsulated floor above the basement creates a "cold feet" effect and significant heat loss.
    *   **Action:** Insulate the ceiling of the basement. This is far more cost-effective than ripping up the floors of the ground level.

### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Goal: Prevent mold and humidity issues that arise once a house is made airtight through insulation.*

4.  **Ventilation Upgrade:** Your current natural ventilation is insufficient for a highly insulated home.
    *   **Action:** Install a **Balanced Ventilation system with Heat Recovery (HRV)**. This recovers heat from outgoing stale air to warm incoming fresh air, drastically reducing the load on your heating system.

### Phase 3: Heating System Modernization
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.*

5.  **Emission System Replacement:** Your current mix of radiators and air heating is inefficient and likely incompatible with modern low-temperature heat sources.
    *   **Action:** Replace old radiators with **low-temperature radiators** or expand the **underfloor heating** to more rooms. This allows the heating system to run at a lower flow temperature, which is critical for the efficiency of a heat pump.
6.  **Heat Source Transition:** 
    *   **Action:** Replace the old boiler with an **Air-to-Water Heat Pump**. Because you have already insulated the roof, walls, and floor, the heat pump can be smaller (lower kW), cheaper to install, and significantly cheaper to operate.

### Phase 4: Energy Generation (The Final Touch)
*Goal: Offset the remaining electricity costs for the heat pump and household appliances.*

7.  **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** 
    *   **Action:** Install PV panels on the pitched roof. Since you are moving toward an electric heating system (Heat Pump), your electricity demand will increase; on-site generation is the most cost-effective way to neutralize this cost.

---

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence

| Step | Intervention | Impact | Why now? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor** | Massive $\downarrow$ Energy Demand | Prevents heat loss before adding heat. |
| **2** | **HRV Ventilation** | $\uparrow$ Air Quality / $\downarrow$ Heat Loss | Prevents condensation in an insulated shell. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emitters** | $\uparrow$ System Efficiency | Prepares the house for a heat pump. |
| **4** | **Air-to-Water Heat Pump** | $\downarrow$ Carbon / $\downarrow$ Fuel Cost | Replaces inefficient boiler with clean energy. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | $\downarrow$ Electricity Bills | Offsets the power used by the heat pump. |

**Expert Tip:** Do not be tempted to replace the boiler first. If you install a heat pump before insulating the walls and roof, you will need a much larger unit that will struggle to keep the house warm during Ghent's winters, leading to high electricity bills and discomfort.
