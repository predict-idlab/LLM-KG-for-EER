### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the following sequence is recommended. We follow the "Fabric First" principle: improving the building envelope before upgrading the technical installations to ensure the new systems are sized correctly and operate at peak efficiency.

#### Step 1: Enhance the Thermal Envelope
*   **Action:** `insulate gable roof`
*   **Reasoning:** While most of the house is well-insulated, the main gable roof is the "weak link" (Moderately Insulated). Improving this first reduces the overall heat loss of the building, which minimizes the capacity (and cost) required for the heating system in the next step.
*   **Constraint Check:** No asbestos is mentioned for the roof, and the state is "Good," making this a straightforward upgrade.

#### Step 2: Upgrade the Heat Generation System

*   **Reasoning:** The current use of electric convectors is inefficient. An air-water heat pump is the most logical upgrade because the building already possesses **Floor Heating** (a low-temperature emission system), which is the ideal partner for a heat pump to achieve maximum COP (Coefficient of Performance).
*   **Constraint Check:** 
    *   *Geothermal:* Ruled out (Site potential for horizontal and vertical drilling is `false`).
    *   *Heat Grid:* Ruled out (Presence of heat grid is `false`).

#### Step 3: Optimize Domestic Hot Water
*   **Action:** `place heatpumpboiler`
*   **Reasoning:** To further decouple the building from inefficient electric heating and improve the EPC, a dedicated heat pump boiler for domestic hot water should be installed. This complements the air-water heat pump for space heating.
