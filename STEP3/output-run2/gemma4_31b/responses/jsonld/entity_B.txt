To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation must follow the **"Envelope First"** principle. By reducing the energy demand of the building before upgrading the heating systems, we can install a smaller, more efficient heat pump and lower long-term operational costs.

### Analysis of Current State
*   **Energy Performance:** Current EPC score is 262 (C label). The goal is to improve this label.
*   **Thermal Weaknesses:** The flat roof extension, the floor above the basement, and the exterior walls are all **not insulated**.
*   **Systems:** The building uses an outdated boiler and a basic Ventilation System A. There is a stated reason to change the emission system.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal and heat grid options are unavailable. However, there is high potential for PV panels.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Demand Reduction)
*Goal: Stop heat loss to minimize the capacity needed for the future heating system.*
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority, high-impact upgrade.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This area is currently not insulated; adding insulation here prevents significant heat loss to the basement.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is the most effective way to improve the EPC label and thermal comfort without losing interior living space.
    *   *Note: Windows are already double-glazed and frames are in good state, so replacing them is not currently cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Goal: Ensure healthy indoor air and prevent moisture buildup caused by the new airtight envelope.*
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System D with heat recovery). This prevents the "cold draft" effect and recovers heat from exhausted air.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating (Generation & Emission)
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to high-efficiency electric heating.*
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing the heat pump, upgrade radiators to low-temperature emitters. This significantly increases the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most effective replacement for the existing boiler, especially now that the building's heat demand has been lowered by Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Offsetting Consumption)
*Goal: Lower electricity bills and reach a higher EPC label.*
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** With the heat pump now providing the primary heating, electricity consumption will increase. Installing PV panels will offset this energy cost and maximize the building's sustainability.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Insulation** | Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Floor $\rightarrow$ Walls | Reduces total energy demand; allows for a smaller, cheaper heat pump. |
| **2. Ventilation** | Replace System A | Maintains air quality in a now-insulated "tight" building. |
| **3. Heating** | Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water HP | Maximizes the efficiency of the heat pump generation. |
| **4. Renewables** | PV-Panels on Roof | Offsets the electrical load of the heat pump. |

**Estimated Outcome:** This path transforms the building from a C-label to a significantly higher energy class (likely A or B), drastically reduces monthly energy bills, and ensures the investment is cost-effective by avoiding the over-sizing of technical installations.
