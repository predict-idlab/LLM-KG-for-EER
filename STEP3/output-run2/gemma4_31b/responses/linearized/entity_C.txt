Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the building is a very modern (constructed in 2023) attached house with an already excellent energy performance (EPC score of 50). Most structural elements—walls, floors, and the flat roof extension—are already "Well Insulated."

However, there is a clear opportunity to maximize efficiency by addressing the **heating system** and the **main roof**, as the building owner is motivated to improve the EPC further and has a recorded reason to change the emission system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

The optimal sequence follows the "envelope-first" principle: reduce energy demand before upgrading the energy generation/distribution systems.

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization
Although the building is new, the **Main Gable Roof** is currently only "Moderately Insulated." Improving this is the most cost-effective way to lower the overall heating load.
*   **Action:** `insulate gable roof`
*   **Reasoning:** Upgrading the roof from moderately to well-insulated prevents heat loss through the highest point of the building, which in turn allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heat pump in the next step.

#### Step 2: Transition to Sustainable Heating
The building currently utilizes electric convectors (which are generally inefficient) and the data explicitly indicates a `hasReasonToChangeEmissionSystem`. Since geothermal options and heat grids are unavailable for this site, an air-to-water system is the most viable high-efficiency alternative.
*   **Action A:** `place emission system on low temperature`
*   **Action B:** `place air-water heatpump` (or `place air-water heatpump with cooling` for added long-term comfort)
*   **Action C:** `place heatpumpboiler`
*   **Reasoning:** Heat pumps are significantly more energy-efficient than electric convectors. However, they require low-temperature emission systems to operate at peak COP (Coefficient of Performance). Installing the low-temperature emitters first ensures the heat pump does not have to work harder (and cost more) to reach temperature set-points. A heat pump boiler further optimizes domestic hot water production.

#### Step 3: Passive Energy Management
To improve long-term operational performance and reduce the need for active cooling (which consumes electricity), passive measures should be implemented.
*   **Action:** `place sunshade`
*   **Reasoning:** This is a low-cost, high-impact upgrade that prevents solar gain during summer months, reducing the energy load on any cooling systems and improving thermal comfort.

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Focus | Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `insulate gable roof` | Envelope | Reduce heat loss/demand |
| **2** | `place emission system on low temperature` | Distribution | Prepare for high-efficiency heating |
| **3** | `place air-water heatpump` $\rightarrow$ `place heatpumpboiler` | Generation | Shift to sustainable, low-carbon energy |
| **4** | `place sunshade` | Passive Control | Reduce summer cooling loads |
