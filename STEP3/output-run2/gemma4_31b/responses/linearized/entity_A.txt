To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. By prioritizing the building envelope (insulation) before upgrading the technical systems, we ensure that the new heating system is appropriately sized and operates at peak efficiency.

### **Building Analysis Summary**
*   **Current State:** EPC score of 388 (D label), indicating significant room for improvement.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The main gable roof, all exterior walls (full brick), and the floor above the basement are **not insulated**.
*   **Systems:** Uses an outdated boiler and Ventilation System A. Geothermal options are not viable for this site.
*   **Assets:** Windows are already double-glazed and in good condition; the building has high potential for PV panels.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Immediate Priority)**
*Goal: Stop heat loss to reduce the required heating capacity.*
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the most cost-effective way to reduce energy loss as heat rises.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Since the walls are full brick (not cavity walls), exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve the EPC score significantly.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This prevents cold drafts from the basement, improving the thermal comfort of the living areas.

#### **Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation**
*Goal: Ensure healthy indoor air after the building is sealed.*
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Once the envelope is insulated, the building becomes more airtight. Upgrading from Ventilation System A to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) is essential to prevent condensation and mold.

#### **Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Upgrade**
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to sustainable electricity.*
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With the heat loss reduced by Phase 1, a heat pump can now operate efficiently. Since the building already has floor heating and radiators, an air-water heat pump is the optimal replacement for the existing boiler.

#### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation**
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*
6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower long-term operational costs and further improve the EPC label.

---

### **Summary of Sequence & Logic**

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Wall $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | **Fabric First:** Reduces energy demand before installing new equipment. |
| **2** | **Replace Ventilation System** | **Health:** Prevents moisture buildup in a newly insulated home. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | **Efficiency:** Replaces the boiler with a system sized for the *new* lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **PV-Panels on Roof** | **Sustainability:** Generates free electricity to power the heat pump. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By avoiding the replacement of windows (which are already double-glazed and in good state) and focusing on the uninsulated shell, the budget is allocated to the areas with the highest return on investment (ROI) in terms of kWh saved.
