To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building (currently with an EPC score of 388), the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By reducing heat loss first, we can downsize the required heating capacity, thereby reducing both installation and operational costs.

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority)**
The building has several "Not Insulated" elements. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment by drastically reducing energy demand.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the most critical step, as heat rises. 
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. This prevents cold drafts and improves the thermal comfort of the living spaces.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** All three main walls (Front, Right, Rear) are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve the EPC score.

#### **Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality**
Once the building is "sealed" with insulation, air exchange must be managed to prevent moisture buildup and ensure health.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current System A (natural supply, mechanical exhaust) is inefficient. Upgrading to a more modern system (such as System C or D) will ensure a constant supply of fresh air without losing excessive heat.

#### **Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition**
With the heat load significantly reduced by Phase 1, we can now replace the outdated boiler.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the building already has floor heating and radiators, an air-water heat pump is the most compatible and efficient choice. Because geothermal options are unavailable for this site and no heat grid is present, this is the optimal low-carbon alternative to the existing boiler.

#### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation**
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.
6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will reduce operational energy costs and further improve the EPC label.

---

### **Summary of the Optimal Sequence**

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof & Floor Insulation** | Stops the largest sources of heat loss immediately. |
| **2** | **Exterior Wall Insulation** | Completes the thermal shell; prevents "cold wall" effect. |
| **3** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains air quality in a now airtight building. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces the boiler with a system sized for the *new* lower heat load. |
| **5** | **Roof PV Panels** | Provides green energy to power the new efficient systems. |

### **Cost-Effectiveness Note**
*   **Avoided Costs:** We are **not** recommending the replacement of windows or frames. The existing wooden frames are in "Good State" and the glazing is already double-glazed; the marginal gain of triple glazing is lower than the gain from insulating the uninsulated walls and roof.
*   **Operational Gain:** This sequence ensures the heat pump is not oversized, saving on initial capital expenditure and monthly electricity bills.
