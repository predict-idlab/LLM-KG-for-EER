### Analysis of Building State
The building is a modern **Semi-Detached Building** constructed in **2017**. It is already performing at a very high level of energy efficiency, with an **EPC score of 87** (which falls within the **A label** range). 

**Current Strengths:**
*   **Walls & Floors:** All exterior walls (Front, Left, Rear) and the floor above the basement are already **Well Insulated**.
*   **Systems:** The building is equipped with a **Heat Pump** for generation and a **Ventilation System D** (the highest standard of mechanical ventilation), which significantly reduces energy waste.
*   **Roofing:** The flat roof extension is well insulated and in good state.
*   **Windows:** Currently utilizes **Double Glazing** with wooden frames in good condition.

**Identified Weaknesses:**
*   **Roof Insulation:** While the flat roof is well insulated, the **Main Gable Roof** is only **Moderately Insulated**. This is the primary area where thermal energy loss occurs.
*   **Summer Comfort:** There is no mention of solar shading, which can lead to overheating in modern, well-insulated homes, increasing the load on cooling systems.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path
Given that the building is already highly efficient, a "deep renovation" is neither cost-effective nor necessary. Instead, the focus is on **fine-tuning** to maximize the EPC and long-term operational performance.

#### Step 1: Roof Thermal Optimization
*   **Action:** `insulate gable roof`
*   **Reasoning:** The Gable Roof is the only major building envelope element that is not "Well Insulated." Upgrading this from "Moderately" to "Well Insulated" will further lower the energy score and reduce the heating load on the heat pump.
*   **Sequence Note:** This should be the first priority as it provides the most significant reduction in energy consumption.

#### Step 2: Passive Cooling & Comfort
*   **Action:** `place sunshade`
*   **Reasoning:** In highly insulated modern homes (like this one), the risk shifts from winter heat loss to summer overheating. Installing external sunshades is a cost-effective way to reduce the need for active cooling (air conditioning), thereby lowering electricity consumption during summer months.

#### Step 3: Glazing Upgrade (Optional/Long-term)
*   **Action:** `replace glazing` (Upgrade Double $\rightarrow$ Triple Glazing)
*   **Reasoning:** While double glazing is sufficient for an 'A' label, triple glazing is the gold standard for energy efficiency. However, given the 2017 construction and current EPC of 87, the return on investment (ROI) is low. This is suggested only if the owner wishes to push the energy score toward the absolute minimum.

---

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Intervention | Priority | Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | High | High (Thermal Loss) | High |
| **2** | **Place Sunshades** | Medium | Medium (Summer Load) | Very High |
| **3** | **Replace Glazing** | Low | Low (Marginal Gain) | Low |

**Final Recommendation:** Focus exclusively on the **Gable Roof insulation** and **Sunshading**. Because the building already possesses a Heat Pump and Ventilation System D, and the site lacks geothermal potential, further mechanical upgrades are unnecessary.
