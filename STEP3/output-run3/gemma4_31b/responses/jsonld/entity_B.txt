To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1832 attached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation path. By first reducing the energy demand through insulation, we can then install a smaller, more efficient heating system and offset its energy use with renewables.

### **Building Analysis Summary**
*   **Current Status:** EPC score of 262 (C label).
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The flat roof extension, exterior walls (full brick), and the floor above the basement are all **not insulated**.
*   **Systems:** Uses a basic Ventilation System A and a boiler/radiator setup. There is a confirmed need to change the emission system.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal and heat grid options are unavailable.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Demand Reduction)**
*Goal: Stop heat loss to minimize the size and cost of future heating installations.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (RoofExtension):** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority "quick win" as heat rises and flat roofs often have poor thermal performance.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior (Wall_Front & Wall_Rear):** Both full-brick walls are not insulated. Since they are full brick (no cavity), exterior insulation is the most effective way to improve the EPC score and thermal comfort without sacrificing interior space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This area is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement, significantly improving the perceived thermal comfort of the living areas.

#### **Phase 2: Technical Systems (Efficiency Upgrade)**
*Goal: Replace outdated systems with high-efficiency alternatives tailored to the now-insulated building.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** After sealing the building with insulation, air quality and moisture control become critical. Upgrading from System A to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) prevents condensation and improves energy recovery.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With the building's heat demand significantly lowered by Phase 1, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective sustainable alternative, as geothermal is not possible at this site. This replaces the existing boiler.

#### **Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Energy Offset)**
*Goal: Lower operational costs and achieve a higher EPC label.*

6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, moving the building toward a net-zero operational footprint.

---

### **Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic**

| Sequence | Action | Logic | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof & Wall Insulation** | Reduces total heat load. | High (Prevents oversized HVAC) |
| **2** | **Floor Insulation** | Eliminates basement heat loss. | Moderate |
| **3** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures air quality post-insulation. | Moderate |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to sustainable heating. | High (Long-term savings) |
| **5** | **PV-Panels** | Generates free electricity. | High (ROI via bills) |

**Why this path?**
If you were to install the heat pump *before* the insulation, you would need a much larger, more expensive unit to compensate for the heat leaking through the walls and roof. By following this sequence, you minimize capital expenditure on equipment and maximize the long-term energy performance (EPC) of the home.
