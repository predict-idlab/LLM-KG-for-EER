### 1. Current State Analysis
The building is a modern **Semi-Detached Building (constructed in 2017)** and is already performing at a very high level of energy efficiency.
*   **Energy Performance:** It currently holds an **EPC score of 87**, which places it in the **A label** category (0-100 kWh/m²year).
*   **The Envelope:** Most elements are "Well Insulated," including the flat roof, the floor above the basement, and the exterior walls.
*   **Technical Systems:** The building is already equipped with high-efficiency systems, including a **Heat Pump** for generation and a **Ventilation System D** (the most advanced type).
*   **Existing Assets:** PV panels are already installed, and the window frames are in "Good State."

Despite the high efficiency, the owner's motivation is **To Improve EPC**. To move from a standard "A" toward an "A+" (score < 100, but potentially lower), we must target the few remaining "moderate" areas.

---

### 2. Proposed Renovation Path
To maximize efficiency while remaining cost-effective, I propose a "precision upgrade" strategy. Since the building is already efficient, large-scale interventions (like replacing walls or heat pumps) would have diminishing returns and be cost-ineffective.

#### **Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Roof)**
*   **Action:** `insulate gable roof`
*   **Justification:** The `RoofMain` (Gable Roof) is currently **Moderately Insulated**, whereas the rest of the building is "Well Insulated." This is the primary thermal leak in the building.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Improving the roof insulation is more affordable than wall or floor interventions and provides immediate reductions in heat loss.
*   **Ordering:** This is the first priority as per the "insulation first" principle.

#### **Step 2: Glazing Upgrade**
*   **Action:** `replace glazing` (Upgrade from Double to Triple Glazing)
*   **Justification:** The building currently has **Double Glazing**. To push the EPC score lower, Triple Glazing is the next logical step. 
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Because the `WoodenWindowFrame` is recorded in **"Good State,"** it is significantly more cost-effective to replace only the glazing rather than the entire window unit (`replace windows`).
*   **Ordering:** Performed after roof insulation to finalize the airtightness and thermal resistance of the shell.

#### **Step 3: Passive Solar Control**
*   **Action:** `place sunshade`
*   **Justification:** With a high-efficiency envelope and a heat pump, the primary energy risk shifts from winter heating to summer overheating. Sunshades reduce the need for active cooling (air conditioning), thereby lowering total yearly energy consumption.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Very high. This is a low-cost intervention with a high impact on operational energy performance.
