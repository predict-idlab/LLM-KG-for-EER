To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. The goal is to reduce the building's energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems, ensuring that the new technical installations are sized correctly and operate at peak efficiency.

### **Analysis of Current State**
*   **EPC Score:** 388 (Label D), indicating significant room for improvement.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The gable roof, all exterior walls, and the floor above the basement are **not insulated**.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal drilling and heat grid connections are not possible at this site.
*   **Strengths:** Windows are already double-glazed and in good state; the building has potential for PV panels.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Reducing Demand)**
*The most cost-effective way to improve EPC is to stop heat loss. We prioritize the roof as heat rises.*

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority for immediate energy savings.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** All exterior walls (Front, Rear, Right) are full brick and not insulated. I recommend **insulating walls on the exterior** for maximum thermal performance, though **interior insulation** is a cost-effective alternative if the facade cannot be altered.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** To eliminate cold drafts from below and improve thermal comfort in the living areas.

#### **Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation**
*As the building becomes more airtight due to insulation, ventilation must be upgraded to prevent condensation and maintain air quality.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor climate after the envelope is sealed.

#### **Phase 3: Energy Generation & Emission (Sustainable Heating)**
*With the heat demand now significantly lowered, we can replace the outdated boiler with a high-efficiency system.*

5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient replacement for the existing boiler, especially as it can integrate with the existing radiator and floor heating systems.
6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of the new heat pump, any existing radiators should be optimized for low-temperature operation.

#### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Offsetting Consumption)**
*The final step is to produce the electricity required to run the new heat pump.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing these will lower the operational cost of the heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

---

### **Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic**

| Order | Action | Logic | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof Insulation** | Highest heat loss area; immediate ROI. | Very High |
| **2** | **Wall/Floor Insulation** | Stops lateral and bottom heat loss. | High |
| **3** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents mold/moisture in a sealed house. | Medium |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces boiler with green tech; sized for *new* lower demand. | Medium |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity cost of the heat pump. | High (Long-term) |

**Estimated Outcome:** By following this sequence, the building will move from a **Label D** toward a **Label B or A**, significantly reducing yearly energy bills and increasing the market value of the property.
