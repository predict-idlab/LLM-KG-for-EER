To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388.0, a "D" label) while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reduce energy demand through insulation before upgrading heating systems and adding renewables.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Reducing Energy Demand)
The building's envelope is currently the weakest point, with most elements being "Not Insulated."
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The main roof is not insulated. Heat rises, making this the most cost-effective high-impact upgrade.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The front, rear, and right walls are full brick and not insulated. While exterior insulation has a higher unit price, it is significantly more efficient than interior insulation and avoids reducing internal living space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** This element is currently not insulated and provides a low-cost way to reduce cold drafts from below.

#### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation
As the building envelope becomes more airtight through insulation, the current natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup and ensure air quality.
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade to a mechanical system (System C or D) to maintain a healthy indoor climate and recover heat.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Transition
With the energy demand lowered, the heating system can be downsized and transitioned from a traditional boiler to a sustainable source.
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the existing radiators/heating should be optimized for low-temperature operation to maximize the efficiency of the new generation system.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grid options are unavailable for this site, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient replacement for the current boiler, leveraging the existing floor/radiator heating.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower operational costs and significantly improve the final EPC label.
