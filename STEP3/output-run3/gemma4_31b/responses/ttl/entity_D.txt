### Analysis of Building State
The building is a relatively modern (constructed in 2017) semi-detached house with an already high energy performance (EPC score of 87.0, which corresponds to an **A label**). Most critical components are already optimized:
*   **Walls & Floors:** The front, left, and rear walls, as well as the floor above the basement, are all "Well Insulated."
*   **Systems:** The home is equipped with a high-efficiency **Heat Pump** and a **Ventilation System D** (mechanical supply and exhaust), which is the most efficient ventilation type.
*   **Windows:** Currently feature double glazing and wooden frames in good condition.
*   **Roofing:** The flat roof extension is well insulated, but the **main gable roof is only "Moderately Insulated."**

Given that the owner's motivation is to further improve the EPC, the focus must shift from major systemic overhauls (which are already present) to refining the thermal envelope and passive energy management.

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Since the building is already high-performing, the goal is to eliminate the remaining thermal weak points. The most cost-effective and energy-efficient path is as follows:

#### Step 1: Optimize the Thermal Envelope (High Priority)
The main gable roof is the only significant element that is not "Well Insulated." Heat loss through the roof is a primary driver of energy consumption.
*   **Action:** **Insulate gable roof**.
*   **Reasoning:** Upgrading the insulation from "moderate" to "well" will directly lower the energy score and improve the EPC. Because the roof is in "Good State," a full replacement is unnecessary and not cost-effective.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: Implement Passive Cooling (Medium Priority)
With a high-efficiency envelope and a heat pump, the risk of summer overheating increases. Reducing the cooling load is more cost-effective than adding active air conditioning.
*   **Action:** **Place sunshades**.
*   **Reasoning:** Installing outdoor blinds/shades is a low-cost intervention that prevents solar gain, reducing the energy required for cooling during summer months.
*   **Point of Attention:** Prioritize outdoor blinds over indoor ones for maximum efficiency.

### Summary of Renovation Path

| Sequence | Action | Target Element | Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | `ex:RoofMain` | High (Reduces heat loss) | High (Targeted upgrade) |
| **2** | **Place Sunshades** | Windows/Facades | Medium (Reduces cooling load) | High (Low fixed cost) |

### Excluded Actions & Justification
*   **Wall/Floor Insulation:** Excluded because they are already "Well Insulated."
*   **Window Replacement:** Excluded because double glazing in a 2017 build is sufficient; the marginal gain of triple glazing does not justify the high cost of replacing frames in "Good State."
*   **HVAC Upgrades:** Excluded because the building already possesses a Heat Pump and Ventilation System D.
*   **Geothermal/Heat Grid:** Excluded due to lack of site potential and lack of grid availability.
*   **PV Panels:** Excluded as the building already has solar devices and the site has no further potential for additional PV.
