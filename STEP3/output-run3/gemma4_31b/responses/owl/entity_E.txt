To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective for this specific 19th-century detached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reduce energy demand through insulation and glazing before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

Based on the provided ontology and building data, here is the optimal renovation path.

### Phase 1: Building Envelope (Demand Reduction)
*Goal: Stop heat loss. This is the most cost-effective way to improve the EPC score.*

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four external walls are cavity walls and currently "Not Insulated." This is the highest priority due to the very low cost (€22/m²) and low nuisance level.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** The window frames are in "Good State" (value 8), meaning full window replacement is unnecessary and not cost-effective. However, the current "Single Glazing" is a major source of heat loss. Upgrading to double or triple glazing is essential.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The building has a floor above a basement that can be insulated to prevent cold drafts and heat loss from the ground up.

### Phase 2: Technical Systems (Efficiency Optimization)
*Goal: Replace outdated systems with high-efficiency alternatives now that the building's energy demand is lower.*

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses "Ventilation System A" (natural). Upgrading this is necessary to maintain air quality once the house is better sealed through insulation and new glazing.
5.  **Upgrade Heating Generation (Air-Water Heat Pump):** The building has a reason to change its emission system and lacks potential for geothermal drilling or a heat grid. An **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient sustainable alternative to the current boiler.
6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new heat pump, the existing radiators/emitters should be upgraded to low-temperature systems.

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Energy Generation)
*Goal: Offset remaining energy consumption with green energy.*

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building is confirmed to have potential for PV. Since the roof is already "Well Insulated," this is a straightforward installation to lower long-term operational costs and further improve the EPC label.
