### Analysis of Current State
Your home is in a very strong position for a high-efficiency upgrade. You have already tackled the most difficult "hard" renovations: **Ventilation System D** (the gold standard for air quality and heat recovery), **PV panels**, and **updated glazing**. 

The primary "energy leaks" and cost drivers are:
1.  **The Heat Emission System:** Electric convectors are the most expensive and least efficient way to heat a home.
2.  **The Pitched Roof:** "Moderate" insulation is the weakest link in your thermal envelope.
3.  **The Heat Source:** Reliance on a traditional boiler (likely gas or oil) is inconsistent with your goal of maximizing energy efficiency and utilizing your PV panels.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize the ROI and energy performance, the sequence must be: **Envelope $\rightarrow$ Distribution $\rightarrow$ Generation.**

#### Step 1: Optimize the Thermal Envelope (The "Quick Win")
Before upgrading your heating system, you must ensure the house retains as much heat as possible to allow for a smaller, cheaper, and more efficient heat pump.
*   **Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the pitched roof. 
*   **Detail:** Since the flat roof and walls are already well-insulated, focusing on the pitched roof will eliminate the remaining significant heat loss. Use high-performance mineral wool or cellulose insulation to bring it up to modern standards.
*   **Why first?** Reducing heat loss lowers the required capacity (kW) of the new heating system you will install in Step 2.

#### Step 2: Overhaul Heat Distribution (The "Efficiency Jump")
You cannot efficiently run a modern heat pump with electric convectors or poorly functioning emitters.
*   **Action:** Remove all electric convectors and repair/extend the underfloor heating.
*   **Detail:** 
    *   **Expand Underfloor Heating:** Extend the underfloor loops to the rooms currently served by electric convectors. Underfloor heating operates at low temperatures, which is essential for the efficiency of a heat pump.
    *   **Alternative:** If extending the floor is too invasive/costly, install **low-temperature radiators** (oversized radiators) in those specific rooms.
*   **Why now?** Heat pumps are inefficient if they have to "push" water at high temperatures to satisfy old radiators or supplement electric heaters.

#### Step 3: Transition the Heat Source (The "Sustainability Pivot")
Now that the house is well-insulated and the distribution system is low-temperature, you can move away from the boiler.
*   **Action:** Replace the boiler with an **Air-to-Water Heat Pump (ASHP)**.
*   **Detail:** Since geothermal is not feasible and district heating is unavailable, a high-efficiency ASHP is the optimal choice.
*   **Synergy:** Because you already have **photovoltaic panels**, you can power the heat pump with your own electricity, drastically reducing operational costs and potentially bringing your energy bill close to zero.
