To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation must follow the "fabric-first" principle: reduce heat demand through insulation before upgrading the heating systems.

### **Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Shell)**
The building currently has a high EPC score (388) and significant insulation gaps. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system is downsized and operates at peak efficiency.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof (RoofMain):** The main roof is currently not insulated. Since it is in a "GoodState," insulating it is a high-impact, low-nuisance priority.
2.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All three exterior walls (Front, Rear, Right) are full brick and not insulated. Cavity wall insulation is the most cost-effective method for this building type, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance compared to exterior wall insulation.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is not insulated. This is a practical upgrade to eliminate cold spots and reduce heat loss to the basement.

### **Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality**
Once the building is sealed via insulation, the existing Type A ventilation (natural supply/exhaust) will be insufficient and may lead to moisture issues.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading to a more modern system (such as Type C or D) is necessary to ensure a healthy indoor climate and to prevent the loss of heat generated by the new heating system.

### **Phase 3: HVAC Modernization**
With the heat demand reduced, we can now address the emission system (which the owner has already indicated a reason to change).

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of a heat pump, the radiators/heating system should be converted to low-temperature emitters.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal options are not viable for this site and there is no heat grid, an air-water heat pump is the most efficient replacement for the existing boiler. It integrates well with the existing radiator/floor heating infrastructure.

### **Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation**
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will significantly reduce operational costs and further improve the EPC label, aligning with the owner's primary motivation.

---

### **Summary of the Optimal Sequence**
| Step | Action | Rationale | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Roof, Walls (Cavity), and Floor** | Reduces overall heat demand. | High (Low nuisance/High impact) |
| **2** | **Upgrade Ventilation** | Prevents mold/dampness in a sealed house. | Medium |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump** | Efficiently heats a well-insulated home. | Medium (Long-term savings) |
| **4** | **Install PV-Panels** | Generates free electricity for the heat pump. | High (Operational cost reduction) |
