To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1832 attached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. This approach ensures that we first reduce the energy demand of the building by improving the thermal envelope before investing in high-efficiency heating systems.

### Analysis of Current State
*   **Energy Performance:** The EPC score is **262.0 (C Label)**, indicating significant room for improvement.
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** The flat roof, the floor above the basement, and both main walls (front and rear) are **not insulated**. The ventilation is **Type A** (natural), which is the least efficient.
*   **Existing Assets:** The windows are already double-glazed with wooden frames in good condition, so replacing them is not a priority.
*   **Constraints:** Geothermal and heat grid options are unavailable.

---

### Proposed Renovation Path

The sequence is designed to prevent "over-sizing" the heating system and to maximize the ROI of each step.

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (Demand Reduction)
*Goal: Stop heat loss from the most vulnerable areas.*
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority area for heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated; addressing this prevents cold drafts from the basement.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both the front and rear walls are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is preferred over interior insulation to avoid thermal bridging and maintain internal living space.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Goal: Ensure healthy air exchange without losing the heat gained in Phase 1.*
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from **Ventilation System A** to a more modern system (such as Type C or D) will reduce heat loss through uncontrolled air infiltration.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Upgrade (Efficiency)
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to a low-carbon, high-efficiency system.*
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be replaced or modified to low-temperature emitters. This is critical because heat pumps are significantly less efficient when forced to produce high-temperature water.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and efficient generation system to replace the old boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy (Operational Cost Reduction)
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will lower the long-term operational costs of the electricity-driven heat pump.
