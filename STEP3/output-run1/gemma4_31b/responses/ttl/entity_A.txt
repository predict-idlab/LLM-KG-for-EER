To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, I propose a **"Fabric First"** renovation strategy. The current EPC score of **388.0 (D Label)** indicates significant heat loss. 

The most optimal sequence is to first reduce the energy demand (insulation), then address air quality (ventilation), and finally upgrade the energy generation and emission systems to match the new, lower demand.

### Phase 1: The Building Envelope (Demand Reduction)
*Goal: Stop heat from escaping. Upgrading heating before insulation is cost-ineffective as it requires a larger, more expensive system.*

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof (`insulate gable roof`):** The main roof is currently not insulated. This is the highest priority as heat rises; insulating the roof provides the fastest return on investment.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls (`insulate wall on exterior`):** The front, rear, and right walls are full brick and not insulated. While more expensive than cavity insulation, exterior insulation is the most effective way to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement (`insulate floor above basement`):** To complete the thermal shell and prevent cold drafts from the basement, this floor should be insulated.

### Phase 2: Air Quality & Ventilation
*Goal: Prevent humidity and mold issues that arise once a building is made airtight through insulation.*

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation (`replace ventilation system`):** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). After insulating the walls and roof, the house will be more airtight. Upgrading to a mechanical system (e.g., System C or D) is essential for health and maintaining indoor air quality.

### Phase 3: Heating & Emission Systems
*Goal: Transition to sustainable, low-carbon heating now that the energy demand is minimized.*

5.  **Low-Temperature Emission (`place emission system on low temperature`):** Since there is a stated reason to change the emission system, upgrading to low-temperature emitters (like larger radiators or underfloor heating) is a prerequisite for the efficiency of a heat pump.
6.  **Air-Water Heat Pump (`place air-water heatpump`):** With geothermal potential being false and no heat grid available, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective sustainable alternative to the existing boiler.
7.  **Heat Pump Boiler (`place heatpumpboiler`):** To further reduce electricity costs for domestic hot water, a dedicated heat pump boiler should be installed.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*

8.  **Solar Energy (`place PV-panels on roof`):** The building has the potential for PV. Installing panels on the roof will allow the owner to generate their own electricity to power the heat pump, significantly reducing long-term operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
| Step | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Wall $\rightarrow$ Floor Insulation** | Reduces heat loss; lowers the capacity needed for the new heater. |
| **2** | **Replace Ventilation System** | Ensures air exchange in a now-insulated (airtight) home. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emitters $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump** | Maximizes efficiency of the new generation system. |
| **4** | **PV Panels on Roof** | Lowers the operational cost of the electricity-based heating. |
