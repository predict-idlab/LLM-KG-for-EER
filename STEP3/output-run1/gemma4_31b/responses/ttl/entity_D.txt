### Analysis of Current Building State
The building is a relatively modern **semi-detached house (constructed in 2017)** with an already high energy performance. With an **EPC score of 87.0**, it falls into the **A label** category. 

**Current Strengths:**
*   **Heating & Ventilation:** Already equipped with a high-efficiency **Heat Pump** and a **Ventilation System D** (mechanical supply and exhaust).
*   **Insulation:** The walls (front, left, and rear), the floor above the basement, and the flat roof extension are all **well insulated**.
*   **Windows:** Existing windows have wooden frames in good condition and **double glazing**.
*   **Renewables:** PV panels are already installed.

**Identified Weakness:**
*   **Roof Main (Gable Roof):** This is the only major building element that is only **"moderately insulated."** Given the owner's motivation to further improve the EPC, this is the primary target for renovation.

---

### Proposed Renovation Plan

Since the building is already highly efficient, the focus is on "marginal gains"—addressing the few remaining areas where heat loss occurs to push the EPC score from an A toward an A+.

#### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The most cost-effective way to reduce energy consumption in this specific house is to bring the gable roof up to the same standard as the walls and flat roof.
*   **Action:** **Insulate gable roof**.
*   **Reasoning:** The gable roof is currently the "weak link" in the thermal envelope. Improving this from "moderately" to "well insulated" will reduce the heating load on the heat pump.
*   **Point of Attention:** Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side of the insulation to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: Glazing Upgrade (Medium Priority / Optional)
While double glazing is efficient, triple glazing provides a further reduction in U-value.
*   **Action:** **Replace glazing** (upgrade from double to triple glazing).
*   **Reasoning:** Since the wooden frames are in "Good State" (value 8), it is more cost-effective to replace only the glass panes rather than replacing the entire window unit.
*   **Point of Attention:** Verify that the existing wooden frames have the necessary thickness to accommodate triple-pane glass.

#### Step 3: Monitoring and Maintenance (Long-term)
Because the building already possesses a Heat Pump, Ventilation System D, and PV panels, there are no further high-impact technical installations recommended from the suggestion list (geothermal is not possible at this site, and a heat grid is unavailable).

---

### Summary of Sequence & Cost-Effectiveness

| Sequence | Action | Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | High | **Very High** (Addresses the only moderate insulation point) |
| **2** | **Replace Glazing** | Low-Medium | **Moderate** (Diminishing returns, but improves EPC) |

**Final Verdict:** The building is already in excellent shape. To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the owner should focus exclusively on the **gable roof insulation**. Further upgrades (like triple glazing) should only be pursued if the budget is ample and the primary goal is a symbolic improvement of the EPC label.
