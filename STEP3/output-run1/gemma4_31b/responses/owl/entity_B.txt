To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: first reduce energy demand by improving the building envelope, then upgrade the technical systems to be more efficient, and finally integrate renewable energy.

Based on the provided ontology and building data, here is the proposed renovation path.

### Phase 1: Improving the Building Envelope (Demand Reduction)
Before upgrading heating systems, we must stop heat loss. The building currently has several "not insulated" areas that are high priorities.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Extension):** The extension roof is currently not insulated. This is a critical point of heat loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both the front and rear full-brick walls are not insulated. Since there is no historical protection on these walls, exterior insulation is the most effective way to improve the thermal bridge and EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. Insulating this area prevents cold drafts from the basement.
4.  **Review Gable Roof:** While the main roof is "moderately insulated," the owner's motivation is to improve the EPC. If budget permits, adding further insulation here would be beneficial, though it is a lower priority than the non-insulated sections.

### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
Improving the envelope makes the house more airtight, which necessitates a better ventilation system to prevent moisture and maintain air quality.

5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses **Ventilation System A** (natural supply/exhaust), which is the least efficient. Upgrading to a System C or D (mechanical) will significantly improve thermal comfort and energy efficiency.

### Phase 3: Heating and Emission System Upgrade
With the heat demand now lowered by the insulation, we can install a right-sized, high-efficiency heating system.

6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the building uses radiators, converting these to low-temperature emitters is necessary to maximize the efficiency of a heat pump.
7.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** The building has a reason to change its emission system, and the site lacks potential for geothermal drilling or a heat grid. An air-water heat pump is the most practical and cost-effective sustainable alternative to the current boiler.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Integration
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

8.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building is confirmed to have potential for PV. Installing solar panels on the roof will reduce operational costs and further improve the EPC label.
