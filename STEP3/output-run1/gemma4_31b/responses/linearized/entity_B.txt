To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1832 attached building, we must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. This means reducing the energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems. Upgrading a heating system in a poorly insulated home leads to oversized equipment and higher operational costs.

Based on the building's current state (EPC 262, Not Insulated walls/floors, and a desire to improve the EPC), here is the optimal renovation path.

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The "Fabric First" Step)
*Goal: Stop heat loss to reduce the required capacity of the future heating system.*

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof (Roof Extension):** The extension is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority, high-impact win for energy efficiency.
2.  **Insulate Gable Roof (Roof Main):** While currently "moderately insulated," upgrading this to "well insulated" will significantly reduce heat loss through the top of the house.
3.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior (Front and Rear):** Both walls are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to improve the thermal bridge and EPC score.
4.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated. This prevents cold drafts from the basement, improving ground-floor thermal comfort.

### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality
*Goal: Ensure healthy air exchange now that the building is "air-tight" due to insulation.*

5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses System A (natural). With the new insulation, the risk of condensation increases. Upgrading to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) is recommended to maintain air quality and prevent moisture buildup.

### Phase 3: Heating System Upgrade
*Goal: Transition from fossil fuels to a high-efficiency, low-carbon system.*

6.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The current system uses radiators. To maximize the efficiency of a heat pump, the emission system should be optimized for lower temperatures.
7.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site is not suitable for geothermal drilling and there is no heat grid available, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most cost-effective and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
*Goal: Offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.*

8.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has high potential for PV. Installing solar panels will reduce the operational cost of the heat pump and further lower the energy score of the building.

---

### Summary of the Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Roofs** | Insulate Flat $\rightarrow$ Insulate Gable | Heat rises; fastest way to reduce energy loss. |
| **2. Walls/Floors** | Insulate Exterior Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor | Eliminates the largest areas of heat loss in an 1832 structure. |
| **3. Ventilation** | Replace Ventilation System | Prevents humidity/mold issues after insulation. |
| **4. Heating** | Low-Temp Emission $\rightarrow$ Air-Water HP | Right-sizing the heat pump based on the *new* lower energy demand. |
| **5. Energy** | PV Panels on Roof | Lowers the carbon footprint and monthly utility bills. |

**Cost-Effectiveness Note:** By insulating first, you can install a smaller, cheaper heat pump that operates at a higher COP (Coefficient of Performance), ensuring the lowest possible long-term operational costs.
