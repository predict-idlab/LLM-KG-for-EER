To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1951 semi-detached building, the renovation must follow the "fabric first" principle. By reducing heat loss through the building envelope before upgrading the heating system, we can install a smaller, more efficient heat pump and significantly lower long-term operational costs.

### **Phase 1: Building Envelope (Heat Loss Reduction)**
The building currently has an EPC score of 388 (D Label), indicating significant energy leakage. The priority is to seal the shell.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is a gable roof and is currently **not insulated**. Since heat rises, this is the most critical step for immediate energy savings.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Right`, and `Wall_Rear` are all full brick walls and are **not insulated**. Because there is no historical protection, **insulating the walls on the exterior** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve the EPC label.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently **not insulated**. Adding insulation here prevents cold air from entering the living spaces from below.

*Note: The windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good state, so replacing them is not a priority compared to the walls and roof.*

### **Phase 2: Heating & Ventilation Systems (Efficiency Upgrade)**
Once the building is well-insulated, the energy demand will drop, allowing for a transition from the current boiler/air heating system to a sustainable alternative.

4.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** The site constraints explicitly rule out the heat grid and both horizontal and vertical geothermal options. An **air-water heat pump** is the most viable and cost-effective high-efficiency alternative to replace the existing generation system.
5.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The current system is Type A (natural supply). Upgrading to a more modern ventilation system will improve indoor air quality and, if a heat-recovery system is chosen, further reduce heat loss.

### **Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Operational Cost Reduction)**
The final step is to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has **potential for PV**. Installing solar panels on the roof will provide the electricity needed for the heat pump, drastically reducing monthly energy bills and pushing the building toward a higher EPC label (A or B).

### **Summary of Optimal Sequence**
| Sequence | Action | Target Element | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | RoofMain | Highest heat loss area; highest ROI. |
| **2** | **Insulate Wall Exterior** | Walls (Front, Right, Rear) | Eliminates massive heat loss through brickwork. |
| **3** | **Insulate Floor** | FloorAboveBasement | Prevents cold drafts from the basement. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Generation System | Efficient heating for a now-insulated shell. |
| **5** | **Replace Ventilation** | Ventilation System | Ensures air quality and thermal efficiency. |
| **6** | **PV-Panels on Roof** | Roof | Offsets electrical costs of the heat pump. |
