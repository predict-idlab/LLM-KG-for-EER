### Analysis of Building Status
The building is a very modern **Attached Building (constructed in 2023)** with an already impressive **EPC score of 50**. Most of the building envelope is high-performing: the walls (front and rear), the floor above the basement, and the flat roof extension are all **Well Insulated**. The windows are equipped with double glazing and wooden frames in good condition.

However, there are two primary areas for improvement to satisfy the owner's motivation to further improve the EPC:
1.  **The Thermal Envelope:** The main gable roof is only **Moderately Insulated**, representing the primary point of heat loss.
2.  **The Emission System:** The building currently relies on **Electric Convectors** (which are energy-intensive) and a standard boiler. There is an explicit "reason to change the emission system."

### Proposed Renovation Path

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow a "Fabric First" approach—optimizing the shell before upgrading the technical installations.

#### Step 1: Thermal Shell Optimization
*   **Action:** **Insulate Gable Roof**
*   **Reasoning:** This is the only remaining element of the building envelope that is not "Well Insulated." Improving the gable roof from moderately to well-insulated will reduce the overall heating load of the building.
*   **Practical Note:** Since the roof is in a "Good State," this can be done efficiently. Ensure a vapour barrier is installed on the warm side to prevent condensation.

#### Step 2: High-Efficiency Heating Upgrade
*   **Action:** **Place Air-Water Heat Pump**
*   **Reasoning:** The building has a reason to change its emission system and currently uses inefficient electric convectors. Since geothermal potential is unavailable (both vertical and horizontal) and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most efficient alternative.
*   **Synergy:** The building already possesses **Floor Heating**, which is the ideal emission system for a heat pump, as it allows the system to operate at lower temperatures and higher COP (Coefficient of Performance).

#### Step 3: System Optimization
*   **Action:** **Place emission system on low temperature**
*   **Reasoning:** To maximize the efficiency of the newly installed Air-Water Heat Pump, the emission system should be configured for low-temperature operation. This reduces the energy required by the heat pump to maintain the desired indoor temperature, directly lowering operational costs and further improving the EPC score.

### Summary of the Renovation Sequence

| Sequence | Renovation Action | Target Element | Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Gable Roof** | RoofMain | Minimize heat loss from the building shell. |
| **2** | **Place Air-Water Heat Pump** | Generation System | Replace inefficient electric heating with sustainable tech. |
| **3** | **Low-Temperature Emission** | Emission System | Maximize the operational efficiency of the heat pump. |

**Conclusion:** Because the building is nearly new (2023), this plan avoids costly structural replacements (like windows or walls) and focuses on the "low-hanging fruit": the gable roof and the transition from electric resistance heating to a heat pump. This path provides the highest return on investment in terms of both EPC improvement and long-term energy savings.
