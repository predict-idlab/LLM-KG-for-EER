To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 1862 detached building, we must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: first reduce the energy demand by improving the building envelope, and then upgrade the technical systems to meet that reduced demand.

### 1. Building Analysis
*   **The Envelope:** The roof is already well-insulated, but the walls (all cavity walls) and the floor (above crawl space) are not insulated. The windows are currently **single-glazed**, which is a significant source of heat loss.
*   **The Systems:** The building uses a boiler with a mix of emission systems (floor heating, radiators, electric convectors). The owner is motivated to improve the EPC and has a reason to change the emission system.
*   **Constraints:** There is no access to a heat grid and no potential for geothermal drilling. However, there is potential for PV panels.

---

### 2. Proposed Renovation Path (Optimal Sequence)

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope (The "Quick Wins")
*   **Step 1: Insulate Wall Cavities**
    *   **Action:** Inject insulation into the cavity walls.
    *   **Reasoning:** All four walls are cavity walls and currently uninsulated. Cavity insulation is one of the most cost-effective ways to reduce heat loss without altering the building's facade (important for a house from 1862).
*   **Step 2: Insulate Floor Above Basement**
    *   **Action:** Install insulation in the floor above the crawl space.
    *   **Reasoning:** This prevents cold air from the crawl space from entering the living areas, significantly improving thermal comfort and reducing energy demand.
*   **Step 3: Replace Glazing**
    *   **Action:** Upgrade single glazing to double or triple glazing.
    *   **Reasoning:** Since the existing wooden window frames are in a "Good State" (Value: 8), replacing only the glass (glazing) is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit while achieving nearly the same energy gain.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*   **Step 4: Replace Ventilation System**
    *   **Action:** Upgrade from System A to a more efficient system (e.g., System D).
    *   **Reasoning:** Once the building is sealed with new glazing and insulation, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery is essential to maintain air quality and prevent moisture buildup.

#### Phase 3: Energy Generation & Emission
*   **Step 5: Install Air-Water Heat Pump**
    *   **Action:** Replace the existing boiler with an air-water heat pump.
    *   **Reasoning:** Geothermal and heat grids are unavailable. Since the building already possesses **floor heating** (a low-temperature emission system), an air-water heat pump is the most efficient and logical choice to replace the boiler.
*   **Step 6: Place PV-Panels on Roof**
    *   **Action:** Install solar photovoltaic panels.
    *   **Reasoning:** This is the final step to offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump, moving the building toward energy independence and further improving the EPC score.

---

### 3. Summary of Benefits
| Priority | Action | Energy Impact | Cost-Effectiveness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High** | Wall & Floor Insulation | Very High $\downarrow$ Demand | Excellent (Low cost/sqm) |
| **High** | Glazing Upgrade | High $\downarrow$ Demand | High (Reuse frames) |
| **Medium** | Ventilation Upgrade | Medium $\downarrow$ Loss | Moderate |
| **Medium** | Air-Water Heat Pump | High $\downarrow$ Carbon | High (Pairs with Floor Heating) |
| **Low** | PV Panels | High $\downarrow$ Cost | Long-term ROI |

**Final Result:** By insulating first, the required capacity (and cost) of the heat pump is reduced, and the overall EPC label will move significantly toward the owner's goal.
