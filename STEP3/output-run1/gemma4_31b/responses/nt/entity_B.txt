To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness for this 19th-century attached building, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. By improving the building envelope first, we reduce the overall energy demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### **Building Analysis Summary**
*   **Current Status:** EPC Score 262 (C Label).
*   **Critical Weaknesses:** Not insulated walls (Full Brick), not insulated flat roof extension, and not insulated floor above basement.
*   **Constraints:** No potential for geothermal drilling and no access to a district heat grid.
*   **Strengths:** Main gable roof is moderately insulated; windows are already double-glazed with frames in good condition.

---

### **Proposed Renovation Path**

#### **Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The Priority)**
Before upgrading the heating system, we must stop heat loss. 
1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a high-priority "quick win" for energy reduction.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** Both the front and rear full-brick walls are not insulated. Exterior insulation is recommended over interior insulation to prevent mold and maximize usable internal space.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor is currently not insulated; adding insulation here prevents significant heat loss to the basement.
4.  **Upgrade Gable Roof Insulation:** While moderately insulated, upgrading the main roof to "well insulated" will further lower the energy score.

#### **Step 2: Ventilation Upgrade**
Improving the airtightness of the building (via insulation) requires a better ventilation strategy to maintain air quality.
*   **Replace Ventilation System:** Transition from System A (natural supply) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D) to reduce heat loss through uncontrolled air exchange.

#### **Step 3: Heating System Transformation**
With the energy demand now significantly lowered, we can replace the outdated boiler.
1.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Since the building currently uses radiators, upgrading to low-temperature emitters is essential to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
2.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and heat grids are unavailable, an air-water heat pump is the most cost-effective and sustainable alternative to the existing boiler.

#### **Step 4: Renewable Energy Integration**
To move the building toward a net-zero energy balance and lower operational costs:
*   **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed PV potential. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump.

---

### **Optimal Execution Sequence & Logic**

| Sequence | Action | Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation** (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor) | Reduces the "load." Prevents over-sizing the future heat pump. |
| **2** | **Ventilation** | Ensures healthy indoor air quality after the building is sealed. |
| **3** | **Low-Temp Emitters** | Prepares the building for high-efficiency heat generation. |
| **4** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches from fossil fuels to electricity in a highly insulated shell. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Provides the green electricity needed to power the heat pump. |

### **Final Expected Outcome**
By following this sequence, the building will transition from a **C label** toward an **A or B label**. This path is cost-effective because it avoids the expensive mistake of installing a high-capacity heating system in a "leaky" house, ensuring the long-term operational performance of the home.
