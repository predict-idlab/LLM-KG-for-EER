Given that your home was built in 2017 and is already equipped with a heat pump, Ventilation System D, and photovoltaic panels, you are starting from a very strong baseline. An EPC score of 87 is already quite efficient for a house of this size.

However, to further drive down energy consumption and improve the EPC score while remaining cost-effective, the focus must shift from "major overhauls" to **"precision targeting"** and **"system optimization."**

Here is the recommended renovation path, ordered by priority and return on investment (ROI).

---

### Step 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Quick Win")
The only identified weakness in your building envelope is the **moderate insulation of the main pitched roof**. Since heat rises, this is your primary area for heat loss.

*   **The Action:** Upgrade the insulation of the main pitched roof to "well-insulated" status. 
*   **Method:** Depending on the current construction, consider adding a layer of high-performance insulation (such as PIR boards or cellulose fiber) in the attic space or via the roof slope.
*   **Why:** This is the most cost-effective way to lower your EPC score because it reduces the load on your heat pump, allowing it to operate at a lower, more efficient temperature.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High. Roof insulation typically has one of the fastest payback periods in energy renovations.

### Step 2: Energy Management & Smart Integration (The "Efficiency Gain")
Since you already have the hardware (PV panels + Heat Pump), the next gain is not in *adding* equipment, but in *optimizing* how they communicate.

*   **The Action:** Install a **Home Energy Management System (HEMS)**.
*   **Method:** Implement smart controls that allow your heat pump to "load" the thermal mass of the house (or your hot water tank) during peak PV production hours.
*   **Why:** This maximizes "self-consumption" of your solar energy, reducing your reliance on the grid and lowering operational costs.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Very High. This is a software/small-hardware upgrade rather than a structural renovation.

### Step 3: Glazing Audit & Targeted Upgrades (The "Long-Term Play")
Your windows were renewed in 2020 with double glazing. Replacing them now with triple glazing would be **cost-ineffective** due to the short lifespan of the current windows.

*   **The Action:** Perform a thermal imaging scan (thermography) during winter.
*   **Method:** Identify if there are specific "cold bridges" or leaks around the wooden frames. If leaks are found, apply high-quality weather-stripping or professional sealing.
*   **Why:** Ensuring the 2020 installation is perfectly airtight prevents the Ventilation System D from working harder than necessary to maintain air quality and temperature.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** Moderate. Low cost for sealing; high cost if you replace glass (which is not recommended at this stage).

### Step 4: System Maintenance (The "Performance Guard")
A house with Ventilation System D and a heat pump relies heavily on mechanical efficiency to maintain its EPC rating.

*   **The Action:** Establish a rigorous preventative maintenance schedule.
*   **Method:** 
    *   **Ventilation D:** Replace filters every 6 months to ensure airflow resistance doesn't increase (which would increase electricity use).
    *   **Heat Pump:** Annual professional check of refrigerant levels and cleaning of the exterior unit.
*   **Why:** Degraded filters or a dirty heat pump coil can increase energy consumption by 5–15% without the owner noticing.
*   **Cost-Effectiveness:** High (prevents expensive future repairs and energy spikes).

| Priority | Action | Target | Impact on EPC | Cost |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof Insulation** | Pitched Roof | High $\downarrow$ | Medium |
| **2** | **HEMS Integration** | PV $\leftrightarrow$ Heat Pump | Medium $\downarrow$ | Low |
| **3** | **Airtightness Audit** | Window Seals | Low $\downarrow$ | Low |
| **4** | **System Maintenance** | Vent D / Heat Pump | Stability $\leftrightarrow$ | Low |

**Expert Final Tip:** Because your home is already high-performing, the "law of diminishing returns" applies. Do not invest in expensive window replacements or wall insulation at this stage; the marginal gain in EPC points would not justify the capital expenditure. Focus entirely on the **roof** and **smart energy orchestration**.
