Based on the data provided, your home is in a very strong starting position. You already have a **Type D ventilation system** (the gold standard for air quality and heat recovery), **PV panels**, and **well-insulated walls and floors**. 

The primary "energy leaks" and inefficiencies are the **moderate roof insulation** and the **outdated/inefficient heating system** (specifically the electric convectors, which are among the most expensive ways to heat a home).

To maximize your EPC score and long-term cost-effectiveness, I propose the following phased renovation path. **The sequence is critical:** we optimize the "shell" before upgrading the "engine" to ensure the new heating system is not oversized (and thus overpriced).

---

### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Quick Win")
Before changing your heating system, you must address the only remaining weakness in your insulation.

*   **Action:** **Upgrade the main pitched roof insulation.**
*   **Why:** You noted this is only "moderate." Heat rises; a pitched roof is the primary exit point for thermal energy. Improving this to "well insulated" (matching your flat roof) will lower the total heat load of the house.
*   **Benefit:** This allows you to install a smaller, cheaper, and more efficient heat pump in Phase 3. It is the most cost-effective way to jump several points on the EPC scale.

### Phase 2: Heat Emission Modernization
Replacing the boiler is useless if the heat is delivered via inefficient methods.

*   **Action A: Eliminate Electric Convectors.** These are energy sinks. Replace them with either:
    *   **Extension of Underfloor Heating (UFH):** If the floor allows, extend the UFH to the rooms currently served by convectors.
    *   **Low-Temperature Radiators:** If UFH is not possible, install oversized low-temperature radiators designed for heat pumps.
*   **Action B: Refurbish/Replace UFH Manifolds and Piping.** Since you mentioned the system is in poor condition, ensure the piping is flushed and the manifolds are modernized to allow for precise zone control.
*   **Why:** Heat pumps operate most efficiently at low flow temperatures. Electric convectors are incompatible with a sustainable energy strategy.

### Phase 3: Heat Generation Transition
Now that the house is tightly insulated and the emission system is optimized for low temperatures, you can replace the boiler.

*   **Action:** **Install an Air-to-Water Heat Pump.**
*   **Why:** Since geothermal is not an option and district heating is unavailable, an Air-to-Water heat pump is the most logical choice. 
*   **Synergy:** Because you already have **PV panels**, you can use your own generated electricity to power the heat pump, potentially bringing your operational heating costs close to zero.
*   **EPC Impact:** Moving from a traditional boiler to a heat pump provides one of the largest possible leaps in EPC scoring.
