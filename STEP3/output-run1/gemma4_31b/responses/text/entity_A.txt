To move a house from an EPC score of 388 to a high-performance rating, you must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle. The most common mistake is replacing the heating system before insulating; this leads to installing an oversized, expensive system that remains inefficient.

Given the 1951 construction in Ghent and the specific constraints of your home, here is the optimal, sequenced renovation path.

---

### Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Stopping the Leak)
*Goal: Reduce the total energy demand of the house so that the future heating system can be smaller and cheaper.*

1.  **Roof Insulation (Highest Priority):** Since heat rises, the uninsulated pitched roof is your biggest source of energy loss. 
    *   **Action:** Install high-performance mineral wool or PIR insulation. If the roof is in good condition, insulating from the inside (between rafters) is cost-effective, though insulating from the outside (sarking) is more efficient.
2.  **Floor Insulation (Basement Ceiling):** The floor above the basement is a "low-hanging fruit."
    *   **Action:** Insulate the ceiling of the basement. This is significantly cheaper than digging up floors and prevents "cold feet" syndrome.
3.  **Wall Insulation:** Solid brick walls from 1951 have poor thermal resistance.
    *   **Action:** Since the house is not protected, **Exterior Wall Insulation (EWI)** is the gold standard. It eliminates thermal bridges and protects the brickwork. If budget is a constraint, prioritize the walls facing North/East.

### Phase 2: Ventilation & Air Quality
*Goal: Prevent mold and moisture buildup caused by the newly sealed envelope.*

4.  **Upgrade Ventilation:** You currently have basic natural ventilation. Once you insulate the walls and roof, the house becomes "airtight," which can lead to condensation.
    *   **Action:** Install a **Mechanical Ventilation system with Heat Recovery (MVHR)** or at least a balanced ventilation system. This ensures fresh air while recovering heat from the exhaust air, preventing the heat you just "saved" from leaking out through vents.

### Phase 3: The Heating Transition
*Goal: Move from fossil fuels to a low-carbon, high-efficiency system.*

5.  **Emitter Replacement:** You mentioned your radiators and air heating are in poor condition.
    *   **Action:** Replace old radiators with **low-temperature radiators** or expand the **underfloor heating** system. Heat pumps are significantly more efficient when they can operate at lower flow temperatures (e.g., 35-45°C instead of 70°C).
6.  **Heat Pump Installation:** Now that the house is insulated and the emitters are optimized, the energy load is low.
    *   **Action:** Install an **Air-to-Water Heat Pump**. Since geothermal is not possible, this is your most sustainable option. Because you insulated first, the heat pump will be smaller, cheaper to install, and cheaper to run.

### Phase 4: Energy Generation
*Goal: Offset the electricity cost of the heat pump and reach a near-zero energy balance.*

7.  **Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** 
    *   **Action:** Install solar panels on the pitched roof. 
    *   **Synergy:** The electricity generated will power the heat pump and the ventilation system, drastically reducing your monthly operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence & Logic

| Step | Action | Logic | Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Roof $\rightarrow$ Floor $\rightarrow$ Walls** | Reduce heat loss first. | $\downarrow$ Energy Demand |
| **2** | **Ventilation** | Prevent humidity/mold in a sealed house. | $\uparrow$ Air Quality |
| **3** | **Emitters $\rightarrow$ Heat Pump** | Size the system to the *new* low demand. | $\downarrow$ Operational Cost |
| **4** | **PV Panels** | Power the new electrical systems. | $\rightarrow$ Energy Independence |

### Expert Tips for Ghent/Flanders:
*   **Subsidies:** Check the **"Mijn VerbouwPremie"** (My Renovation Premium). The Flemish government provides significant grants for these specific steps, but they often require a certified EPC advisor to verify the works.
*   **Windows:** Since your wooden frames and double glazing are in good condition, **do not replace them yet**. They are a lower priority than the roof and walls in terms of ROI.
*   **The "Semi-Detached" Advantage:** You share one wall with a neighbor, which acts as a natural insulator. Focus your wall insulation efforts on the exterior-facing sides.
