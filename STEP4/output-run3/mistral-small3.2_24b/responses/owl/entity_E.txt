### Renovation Plan

The building is a detached house from 1862 with a current EPC score of 165 (D Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC score.

Given the age of the building and the current EPC score, the renovation should follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

#### Proposed Renovation Path

**Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)**

1. **Insulate Cavity Walls:**
   - The building has four cavity walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) that are currently not insulated.
   - Insulating these walls is a priority as it is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
   - **Insulating the wall cavity** is more cost-effective than exterior insulation and provides a substantial increase in thermal retention.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:**
   - The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is currently not insulated.
   - Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the crawl space.

**Phase 2: Window Performance**
The window performance should be enhanced, since the single glazing leads to significant heat losses.

3. **Replace Glazing:**
   - All glazing should be replaced, as all windows still have single glazing.
   - Adding double or even triple glazing would drastically improve the thermal performance.

**Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition**
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

**Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

5. **Replace Ventilation System:**
   - Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D).
   - This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

**Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:**
   - The building has confirmed potential for PV.
   - Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

#### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Reduce the remaining thermal heat losses. |
| **3** | **Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the heat pump and further improves the EPC score. |

This plan ensures that the building is made as energy-efficient as possible while remaining cost-effective and aligned with the owner's motivation to improve the EPC score.
