### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate heat leakage. The building has several critical weaknesses in its envelope despite the roof being well-insulated.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into these cavities is one of the most cost-effective renovations available, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated. Addressing this prevents cold drafts from the ground and improves the overall thermal comfort of the living areas.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** All windows currently utilize **Single Glazing**, which is a major source of heat loss. Since the wooden frames are in a **"Good State" (Value 8)**, it is more cost-effective to **Replace the Glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) rather than replacing the entire window unit.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation and Air Quality (Priority: Medium)
As the building becomes more airtight through wall and window upgrades, natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from **Ventilation System A** (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures a healthy indoor climate and prevents heat loss through uncontrolled air infiltration.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened thermal envelope, the building's heat demand is lowered, allowing for a more efficient, low-temperature heating system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before upgrading the boiler, the existing emission systems (radiators/convectors) should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to ensure the new heat pump operates at maximum efficiency (COP).
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further drive down the EPC score toward an 'A' label.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the heat pump and ventilation system.
