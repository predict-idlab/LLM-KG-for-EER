**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data, the property is an attached house from 1832 with a significant floor area (212 m²) and an EPC score of 262. Given the age of the building and the current energy performance, the most cost-effective and energy-efficient strategy is to follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating and energy systems. This prevents the installation of oversized equipment and ensures the lowest possible operational costs.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to minimize heat loss. Because the house is not historically protected, we have the flexibility to use the most effective insulation methods.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Heat rises rapidly; the uninsulated flat roof is a major source of thermal leakage. Insulating this section is a high-priority, high-impact move.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. **Exterior wall insulation** is highly recommended here to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the 19th-century brickwork from moisture and temperature fluctuations.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The uninsulated floor above the basement leads to significant heat loss and discomfort ("cold feet"). Adding insulation here completes the thermal shell.

*Note: The windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition, so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the heat demand of the house is reduced through insulation, the heating system can be modernized to a low-temperature system.

4.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission Systems:** Since the current radiators are in poor condition and need replacement, they should be replaced with **low-temperature radiators or underfloor heating**. This is a critical prerequisite for the efficiency of a heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no district heating or geothermal potential, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler. It will operate at a much higher Coefficient of Performance (COP) thanks to the new insulation and low-temp emitters.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
By sealing the building envelope in Phase 1, the natural air exchange rate will decrease. To prevent humidity buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from a basic natural system (System A) to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). A balanced ventilation system with heat recovery (System D) would be the gold standard for maximizing energy efficiency.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity required to run the heat pump and ventilation system, the house should transition to self-generation.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Utilizing the confirmed potential for PV panels on the roof will reduce reliance on the grid and further lower the EPC score.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the building. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Ensures air quality and prevents moisture in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Generates renewable electricity to power the new HVAC systems. |
