Based on the provided building data, this is a very modern home (built in 2023) with an already excellent EPC score of 50. The thermal envelope is largely optimized, and high-efficiency systems (Ventilation D and PV panels) are already in place. 

The primary inefficiency lies in the current heating generation and the presence of electric convectors, which are energy-intensive. To maximize efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation should focus on transitioning to a sustainable heating system while slightly refining the remaining thermal weakness.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Refinement (Priority: Low)
Since the house is nearly airtight and well-insulated, the gains here are marginal but necessary to ensure the heating system is right-sized.
1. **Improve Pitched Roof Insulation:** While the flat roof and walls are "well insulated," the main pitched roof is only "moderately insulated." Upgrading this to a "high" level of insulation will minimize the remaining heat loss through the top of the building.

#### Phase 2: Heating Emission Upgrade (Priority: High)
The current mix of emission systems is inconsistent. Electric convectors are highly inefficient and often lead to higher electricity bills.
2. **Eliminate Electric Convectors:** Replace the electric convectors with a more efficient system. Since the house already has underfloor heating, the goal should be to extend the hydronic (water-based) system to those rooms or install high-efficiency low-temperature radiators.

#### Phase 3: Sustainable Heat Generation (Priority: High)
With the envelope optimized and the emission systems updated to low-temperature standards, the heat generator can be replaced.
3. **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no district heating or geothermal potential, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most logical replacement for the existing boiler. Because the house already has underfloor heating and a tight envelope, the heat pump will operate at a very high Coefficient of Performance (COP), maximizing the energy produced per unit of electricity consumed.
