**Renovation Plan**

To maximize energy efficiency and significantly lower the EPC score (currently 388) for this 1951 semi-detached house, the renovation must follow a **"Fabric-First"** strategy. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating system, we reduce the total heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more cost-effective, and more efficient heat pump.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The priority is to eliminate the primary sources of heat loss. Since the house is from 1951 and has several uninsulated components, this phase provides the highest return on investment.

1.  **Insulate Main Pitched Roof:** The main roof currently has no insulation. Because heat rises, this is the most critical priority to prevent massive thermal leakage.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The solid brick walls are not insulated. Given that the house is not historically protected, **exterior wall insulation** is recommended as it eliminates thermal bridges more effectively than interior insulation.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the lower level.
*Note: The flat-roof extension already has moderate insulation and is a lower priority than the main roof.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Upgrade
Once the building is sealed and the heat demand is lowered, the outdated and inefficient heating system can be replaced.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** Since the current radiators and air heating systems are in poor condition, they should be replaced with **low-temperature emission systems** (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators). This is a prerequisite for maximizing the efficiency of a heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no district heating or geothermal potential, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient choice to replace the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality
Improving the insulation and sealing the envelope reduces natural air infiltration, which can lead to moisture problems and poor indoor air quality.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). This ensures a controlled flow of fresh air without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation
To offset the electrical demand of the new heat pump and ventilation system, the house should transition to self-generation.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the property has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof is the final step to minimize operational costs and further improve the EPC rating.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Main Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces total energy demand and stops heat leakage. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents condensation and maintains air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets electricity consumption and reduces long-term operational costs. |

**Note on Windows:** Since the windows already have double glazing and the frames are in good condition, replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.
