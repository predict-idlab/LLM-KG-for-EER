**Renovation Plan: Energy Efficiency Optimization**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building constructed in 1862. While it currently holds an EPC score of 165 (B Label), there are significant opportunities to further reduce energy consumption and modernize the systems. 

To maximize long-term operational performance and cost-effectiveness, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate the primary sources of heat loss. Since the building is from 1862, the current "B" label is likely due to the already well-insulated roof, but the walls and windows remain critical weak points.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavities:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into these cavities is the most cost-effective wall renovation method, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the bedrooms and living area utilize **Single Glazing**. This is a major source of thermal bridging. Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State," replacing the single panes with double or triple glazing is significantly more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space should be insulated to prevent cold air infiltration from the ground, improving the overall thermal comfort of the living areas.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building's heat demand is lowered through insulation and glazing, the heating system can be downsized and modernized.

4.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission:** The current emission system is a mix of radiators and electric convectors. To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the system should be transitioned to **low-temperature emissions**.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no potential for geothermal drilling or access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable replacement for the existing boiler. This shift eliminates reliance on fossil fuels and leverages the now-efficient thermal envelope.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Renewable Energy (Priority: Long-term)
The final phase ensures the building remains healthy and energy-independent.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). As the envelope is sealed via insulation and new glazing, the air exchange rate will drop. Upgrading to a **Ventilation System C or D** is necessary to prevent moisture buildup and ensure indoor air quality.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for solar energy. Installing **PV-panels on the roof** will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, further improving the EPC score and lowering operational costs.
