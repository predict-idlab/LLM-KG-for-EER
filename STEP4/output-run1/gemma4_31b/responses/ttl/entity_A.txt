### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first prevents the need for an oversized heating system and provides the most significant jump in EPC performance.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the highest priority for reducing energy consumption.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all full brick and not insulated. To maximize efficiency and eliminate thermal bridges, **exterior insulation** is recommended over interior insulation.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here reduces heat loss to the basement and improves ground-floor thermal comfort.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state, so these are not priorities for this phase.*

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
Once the roof, walls, and floor are insulated, the building becomes significantly more airtight. To prevent moisture buildup and ensure indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from **Ventilation System A (natural)** to a more efficient mechanical system (System C or D). This ensures that the heat retained by the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 3: Transition to Low-Carbon Heating (Priority: Medium)
With a reduced thermal load, the building can now transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the existing radiators/emission systems should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is critical to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of a heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, and to further drive down the EPC score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable energy required to power the new energy-efficient installations.
