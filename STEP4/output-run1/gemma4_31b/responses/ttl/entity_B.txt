**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a historic attached building from 1832 with an EPC score of 262 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating. 

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: **Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate**. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical points of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment and is essential before upgrading the heating system.
1. **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. Because heat rises, insulating the flat roof is a high-priority action to prevent significant thermal loss.
2. **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the historic brickwork from internal condensation.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves the thermal comfort of the ground floor.
*Note: The main gable roof is already moderately insulated, and the windows are double-glazed and in a "Good State," so these do not require immediate intervention.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the heat demand is significantly lower, enabling the transition from a traditional boiler to a low-temperature sustainable system.
4. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump, the existing radiators should be upgraded or replaced with low-temperature emission systems.
5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid available and no potential for geothermal captation, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Insulating a building from 1832 significantly reduces natural air infiltration. To prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation must be modernized.
6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the energy gains from Phase 1 are not lost through uncontrolled drafts.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the home's new high-efficiency systems.
