### Phase 1: The Thermal Envelope (Priority: High)
Before investing in a new heating system, the "shell" of the building must be sealed to prevent heat loss.

*   **Wall Insulation:** The data indicates the walls are currently uninsulated. **Cavity wall insulation** should be the first priority. This is typically the most cost-effective way to reduce heat loss and is essential before installing a heat pump, as heat pumps require a well-insulated building to operate efficiently.
*   **Window Upgrades:** The property currently uses **single glazing**. These should be replaced with **double or triple glazing**. Single-pane windows are a major source of drafts and thermal bridging, significantly lowering the overall U-value of the home.
*   **Draft Proofing:** Ensure all doors and window frames are sealed to prevent uncontrolled air infiltration.

### Phase 2: Heating and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is thermally efficient, the heating system can be modernized.

*   **Heat Pump Installation:** With the improved insulation from Phase 1, the property is now a candidate for an **Air Source Heat Pump (ASHP)**. This will replace the traditional boiler, significantly reducing carbon emissions and long-term energy costs.
*   **Emitter Optimization:** If moving to a heat pump, ensure that radiators are upgraded to **larger, low-temperature radiators** or underfloor heating, as heat pumps operate at lower flow temperatures than traditional boilers.
*   **Ventilation:** Because the house will be much more airtight after Phase 1, a **Mechanical Ventilation with Heat Recovery (MVHR)** system is recommended to maintain indoor air quality and recover heat from exhausted air.

### Phase 3: Renewable Energy (Priority: Long-term)
To further reduce the carbon footprint and energy bills, integrate on-site generation.

*   **Solar PV Panels:** Install **Solar Photovoltaic (PV) panels** on the roof. This allows the property to generate terms of its own electricity, which can then be used to power the heat pump installed in Phase 2, creating a highly sustainable, low-carbon energy loop.
