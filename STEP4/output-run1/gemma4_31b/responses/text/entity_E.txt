### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to minimize heat loss. Addressing the envelope first prevents the installation of an oversized, more expensive heating system.

1.  **Cavity Wall Insulation:** The walls are cavity walls and currently uninsulated. Filling these cavities is one of the most cost-effective renovations available, as it requires no major structural changes and provides an immediate reduction in heat loss.
2.  **Glazing Upgrade:** The existing windows have wooden frames in good condition but are fitted with **single glazing**. This is a major source of thermal leakage. Replacing the single glass with double or triple glazing (while keeping the frames if possible) is essential for improving the EPC and indoor comfort.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space is likely uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and stops cold air from infiltrating the living spaces from below.

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened envelope, the building's total heat load is reduced, allowing for a transition to a modern, low-temperature system.

4.  **Replace Heat Emission Systems:** The existing radiators, air heating, and electric convectors are in poor condition. These should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (such as oversized radiators or expanded underfloor heating) to maximize the efficiency of a future heat pump.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal and district heating are not options, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Once the house is insulated and the glazing is replaced, the building becomes more airtight. To prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system (System A) to a mechanical system (System C or D). This ensures controlled air exchange without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity required to run the heat pump and ventilation system, the house should move toward energy autonomy.

7.  **Install PV Panels:** The property has confirmed potential for photovoltaic panels. Installing these on the roof will lower operational costs and further improve the EPC rating.
