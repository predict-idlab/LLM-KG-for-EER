### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is an 1832 attached house with several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Flat Roof:** The flat-roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises; insulating the flat roof is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
2. **Insulate Exterior Walls:** Both the front and rear exterior walls are solid brick and not insulated. **Insulating the walls on the exterior** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve thermal retention.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the basement.

#### Phase 2: Heating System Transition
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

4. **Replace Boiler with Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.
5. **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission System:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Replace the basic natural ventilation system with a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Exterior Wall Insulation** → **Floor Insulation** → **Flat Roof Insulation** → **Air-Water Heat Pump** → **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** → **Ventilation Upgrade** → **PV Panels**.

This sequence ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized first, reducing the overall energy demand before transitioning to a more efficient heating system and adding renewable energy generation.
