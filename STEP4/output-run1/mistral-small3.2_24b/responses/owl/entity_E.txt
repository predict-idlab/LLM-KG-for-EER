To maximize energy efficiency while maintaining cost-effectiveness for this 1862 detached building with a current EPC score of 165 (C label), the renovation should follow a "Fabric-First" approach. This prioritizes improving the building envelope before addressing heating systems or other upgrades.

### Proposed Renovation Path

**Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)**
The primary goal here is to minimize heat loss through the building's envelope. Since the building has several uninsulated cavity walls and single-glazed windows, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are cavity walls and not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is the most cost-effective way to significantly reduce heat loss.
2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here reduces heat loss to unconditioned spaces.
3. **Replace Glazing:** All windows still have single glazing. Adding double glazing would drastically improve the thermal performance of the windows.

**Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition**
With a tighter thermal envelope, the building can operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

**Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

5. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

**Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Wall Cavity Insulation** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Floor Insulation** | Prevents heat loss to unconditioned spaces. |
| **3** | **Glazing Replacement** | Reduce the remaining thermal heat losses. |
| **4** | **Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **5** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **6** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the heat pump. |

This sequence ensures that the building is optimized for energy efficiency in a cost-effective manner, addressing the highest priority issues first.
