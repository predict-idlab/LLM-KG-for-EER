### Renovation Plan for Example 3

#### Building Information:
- **Building Type:** Attached
- **Construction Year:** 1832
- **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) Score:** 262 (D Label)
- **Net Floor Area:** 212 m²
- **Building Elements:**
  - **Walls:** Two full brick walls (`Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear`) not insulated.
  - **Roof:**
    - `RoofMain` (Gable Roof) - Moderately Insulated
    - `RoofExtension` (Flat Roof) - Not Insulated
  - **Windows:**
    - `WindowsLivingArea` (Double Glazing)
    - `WindowsBedrooms` (Double Glazing)
  - **Floor Above Basement:** Not Insulated
  - **Ventilation System:** Type A (Natural Air Supply and Exhaust)
  - **Emission System:** Radiators
  - **Generation System:** Boiler
- **Potential for PV:** True
- **Potential for Geothermal:** False
- **Heat Grid Availability:** False
- **Owner's Motivation:** Improve EPC

#### Renovation Strategy:
To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Insulate → Ventilate → Generate"** sequence. This approach ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before investing in high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for an oversized and more expensive HVAC system.

#### Proposed Renovation Path:

**Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)**
The building has significant heat loss through non-insulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1. **Insulate Walls On Exterior:**
   - **Elements:** `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear`
   - **Rationale:** Both walls are full brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve thermal retention.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:**
   - **Element:** `FloorAboveBasement`
   - **Rationale:** Adding insulation prevents cold drafts from the basement and reduces heat loss.

3. **Insulate Flat Roof:**
   - **Element:** `RoofExtension`
   - **Rationale:** The flat roof is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises rapidly through flat sections.

**Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)**
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - **Rationale:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:**
   - **Rationale:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

**Phase 3: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:**
   - **Rationale:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

**Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Post-Renovation)**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

7. **Replace Ventilation System:**
   - **Rationale:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Summary of Optimal Sequence:

**Exterior Wall Insulation** → **Floor Insulation** → **Flat Roof Insulation** → **Air-Water Heat Pump** → **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** → **PV Panels** → **Ventilation System Upgrade**

This plan ensures that the building achieves optimal energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective and aligned with the owner's motivation to improve the EPC.
