### 1. The "Fabric-First" Approach (Priority: High)
Before installing new systems, we must reduce the energy demand of the building.
*   **Insulation:** Focus on the attic/roof and walls. If the building has single-pane windows, upgrading to double or triple glazing is a priority.
*   **Air Sealing:** Identify and seal drafts around windows, doors, and floor-to-wall junctions to prevent uncontrolled air leakage.
*   **Ventilation:** As the building becomes more airtight, a Mechanical Ventilation with Heat Recovery (MVHR) system should be installed to ensure a healthy indoor air quality while recovering heat.

### 2. High-Efficiency Systems (Priority: Medium)
Once the envelope is optimized, replace outdated systems with high-efficiency alternatives.
*   **Heating:** Transition from traditional boilers to an Air Source Heat Pump (ASHP) or Ground Source Heat Pump (GSHP).
*   **Lighting:** Replace all remaining incandescent or fluorescent lighting with high-efficacy LED lighting.
*   **Water:** Install low-flow fixtures and a heat-pump water heater.

### 3. Renewable Energy Integration (Priority: Final Step)
The final step is to offset the remaining energy load with clean energy.
*   **Solar PV:** Install a Solar Photovoltaic (PV) array on the roof. Because the building's demand has been lowered in Step 1 and 2, a smaller, more cost-effective array will be sufficient to achieve "Net Zero" or "Net Positive" energy status.

**Envelope $\rightarrow$ Systems $\rightarrow$ Renewables**
