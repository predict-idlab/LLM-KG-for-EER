### Recommended Strategy for Marginal Gains

#### 1. Insulation & Envelope Optimization
While the envelope is already high-performance, small gaps can occur over time.
* **Air Sealing:** Conduct a "blower door test" to identify any new drafts or leaks around window frames and door seals. Use high-quality weatherstripping or sealant to close these gaps.
* **Thermal Bridging:** Check for any "cold spots" using a thermal camera, particularly around joints or penetrations, and apply targeted insulation (e.g., spray foam or rigid foam inserts) to eliminate thermal bridges.

#### 2. Window & Glazing Enhancements
Since the building already has high-performance glazing, the focus should be on the "active" or "temporary" or "secondary" layers.
* **Thermal/Blackout Curtains:** Install heavy thermal curtains or cellular shades. These provide an additional layer of insulation during winter and reduce solar heat gain during summer, reducing the load on the HVAC system.
* **Window Films:** For south-facing windows, consider applying low-emissivity (Low-E) or heat-reflective films to further reduce cooling costs in the summer.

#### 3. HVAC & System Tuning
With a modern system, the goal is to ensure it is operating at peak efficiency.
* **Smart Thermostats:** If not already present, install a smart thermostat to optimize heating/cooling schedules based on occupancy.
* **Filter Maintenance:** Establish a strict schedule for replacing HVAC filters (every 1-3 months) to ensure airflow is not restricted, which forces the system to work harder.
* **Duct Inspection:** Periodesically check ductwork for leaks or blockages to ensure the air is delivered efficiently to all rooms.

#### 4. Behavioral & Small-Scale Upgrades
* **LED Lighting:** Ensure 100% transition to high-efficiency LED lighting throughout the property.
* **Smart Power Strips:** Use smart power strips for home office or entertainment centers to eliminate "phantom loads" (energy consumed by devices in standby mode).
* **Water Heater Temp:** Lower the temperature of the water heater to $120^\circ\text{F}$ ($49^\circ\text{C}$) to save energy without sacrificing utility.
