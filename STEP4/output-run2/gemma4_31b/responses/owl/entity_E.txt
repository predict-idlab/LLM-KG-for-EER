Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building from 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (B Label). While the building is already performing relatively well, the owner's motivation is to further improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, I propose a renovation path based on the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce energy demand before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate heat loss through the walls, floor, and windows. Because the building is from 1862, these upgrades provide the most significant long-term operational gains.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Rear, Left, and Right) are identified as cavity walls and are currently **not insulated**. Cavity wall insulation is highly cost-effective, has a low nuisance level, and is the most efficient way to treat these specific walls.
2.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the living area and bedroom windows currently use **single glazing**, which is a major source of thermal leakage. Since the wooden frames are in a "Good State," replacing only the glazing (rather than the entire window) is the most cost-effective way to improve the U-value.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space/basement should be insulated to prevent heat loss to the ground and eliminate cold drafts.

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural ventilation). Once the walls and windows are sealed/insulated, the air exchange rate will drop. Upgrading to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) is necessary to maintain indoor air quality and prevent moisture buildup.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a significantly reduced heat demand, the building can now transition from a traditional boiler to a sustainable, low-temperature system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump, the existing emission systems (radiators/floor heating) should be optimized for low-temperature operation.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal drilling, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and energy-efficient replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, further driving down the EPC score.
