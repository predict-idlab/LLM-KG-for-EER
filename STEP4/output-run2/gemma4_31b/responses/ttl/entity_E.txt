To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several uninsulated areas that allow significant heat loss. Addressing these first reduces the total energy demand, allowing for a smaller, more efficient heating system.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are **cavity walls** and are currently **not insulated**. Injecting insulation into the cavities is one of the most cost-effective renovations available, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the ground.
*Note: The Gable Roof is already "Well Insulated," so no further action is required there.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: Medium)
The current windows use **single glazing**, which acts as a major thermal bridge.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Since the wooden window frames are in a **"Good State" (Value 8)**, it is not cost-effective to replace the entire window unit. Instead, replacing only the single glazing with double or triple glazing will drastically improve thermal retention while preserving the existing frames.

#### Phase 3: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the heat demand will drop, enabling a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature heating system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the existing radiators and heating elements should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is critical to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Low)
As the building's envelope becomes more airtight through insulation and new glazing, the natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to prevent moisture buildup and ensure a healthy indoor air quality.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable electricity, further lowering the energy score and operational costs.
