The building is a detached structure from 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating. The renovation plan must prioritize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness while maintaining the property's historical integrity.

Proposed Renovation Path

Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)
Since the building has cavity walls and is not historically protected, the most cost-effective way to improve energy performance is through insulation.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity (4x):** All four walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Right`) are cavity walls and currently not insulated. Insulating the cavity is significantly more cost-effective than interior or exterior insulation.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the crawl space.

Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: Medium)
The windows are currently single-glazed, which leads to significant heat loss.

3.  **Replace Glazing (2x):** All glazing should be replaced. Adding double or even triple glazing would drastically improve the thermal performance.

Phase 3: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators and electric convectors should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Wall Cavity Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Glazing Replacement** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.
