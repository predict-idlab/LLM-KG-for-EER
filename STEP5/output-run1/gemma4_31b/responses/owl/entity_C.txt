Despite the high current efficiency, the owner has expressed a desire to further improve the EPC and specifically indicated a reason to change the emission system. The current use of electric convectors is the primary inefficiency in an otherwise high-performance home.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the following renovation path is proposed:

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Refinement (Priority: Low)
Since the building is nearly optimized, this phase focuses on the only remaining element that is not "Well Insulated."
1. **Insulate Gable Roof:** While the flat roof and walls are well insulated, the main gable roof is currently "Moderately Insulated." To achieve a near-zero energy standard and further lower the EPC, adding additional insulation to the gable roof is the final step in sealing the thermal envelope.

#### Phase 2: Heating System Modernization (Priority: High)
The most significant opportunity for operational cost reduction is replacing the inefficient electric convectors.
2. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before upgrading the generator, the emission system should be optimized. Transitioning to low-temperature emitters (such as expanding the existing floor heating) is essential to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump.
3. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site lacks potential for geothermal drilling and there is no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most cost-effective and sustainable replacement for the current generation system. This will drastically reduce the electricity consumption compared to electric convectors.

#### Phase 3: Domestic Hot Water Optimization (Priority: Medium)
4. **Place Heat Pump Boiler:** To complement the space heating upgrade, installing a dedicated heat pump boiler for domestic hot water will further reduce energy consumption and improve the overall energy score.
