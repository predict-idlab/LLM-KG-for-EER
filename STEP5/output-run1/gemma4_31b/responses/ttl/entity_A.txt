**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached house built in 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (D Label). The owner's primary goal is to improve the EPC rating. 

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the building envelope to reduce heat demand before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems. This prevents the installation of oversized, expensive HVAC systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant heat loss through several uninsulated structural elements. Addressing these first provides the most immediate and cost-effective reduction in energy consumption.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, this is the highest priority for reducing energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The three external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) are full brick and not insulated. For full brick constructions, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and drastically improve the EPC score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the lower level.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed in a good state; therefore, replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the natural air exchange rate will decrease. To prevent moisture buildup and ensure indoor air quality, the ventilation must be modernized.

4.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D) to ensure that heat is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a reduced heat demand, the building can now transition from an outdated boiler to a sustainable, low-temperature system.

5.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the existing radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the efficiency (COP) of the future heat pump.
6.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the home's new high-efficiency systems.
