### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to eliminate the primary sources of heat loss. The roof is already well-insulated, so focus shifts to the walls, floor, and windows.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Right`) are cavity walls and currently not insulated. Injecting insulation into these cavities is one of the most cost-effective ways to drastically reduce heat loss without altering the building's exterior.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is a significant point of thermal leakage. Insulating this floor prevents cold air from the crawl space from entering the living areas.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the living area and bedroom windows currently use single glazing, which acts as a thermal bridge. Since the wooden frames are in a "Good State," **replacing only the glazing** (upgrading to double or triple glazing) is more cost-effective than replacing the entire window unit.

#### Phase 2: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the thermal envelope is sealed, the total heat demand of the house is lowered, allowing for a smaller, more efficient heating system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To prepare for a heat pump, the existing radiators/convectors should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions. This ensures the new generator operates at maximum efficiency (higher COP).
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid available and the site has no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Medium Priority)
With the installation of new glazing and wall insulation, the house becomes more airtight. To prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural ventilation) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to maintain air quality without losing the heat gains achieved in Phase 1.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the EPC score.
