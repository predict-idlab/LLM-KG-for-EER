**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached building from 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (D Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating. 

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the building envelope to reduce energy demand before upgrading the heating and generation systems. This prevents the installation of oversized, expensive equipment.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant heat loss through several uninsulated structural elements. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment for the EPC score.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, insulating the gable roof is the most critical step in reducing thermal loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all full brick and not insulated. Because they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and significantly lower the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves ground-floor comfort.
*Note: The flat roof extension is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed and in a good state; therefore, these are not priorities for this phase to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the envelope is sealed, the building's total heat demand is lowered, allowing for a more efficient, low-temperature heating system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before replacing the generator, the current radiators/emission systems should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is essential to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no potential for geothermal energy and no access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). After adding significant insulation, the house becomes more airtight. Upgrading to a more modern system (e.g., System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and ventilation system, further pushing the EPC label toward a higher grade (A or B).
