To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation must follow a **"Fabric First"** approach. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating system, we ensure that the new HVAC equipment is correctly sized and operates at maximum efficiency.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first provides the highest return on investment.
1. **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as **Cavity Walls** and are currently **Not Insulated**. Injecting insulation into these cavities is the most cost-effective method to drastically reduce thermal bridging without altering the building's exterior.
2. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the ground and eliminates cold drafts from below.
*Note: The gable roof is already "Well Insulated," so no action is required there.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (High Priority)
The windows currently utilize **Single Glazing**, which is a major source of energy leakage.
3. **Replace Glazing:** Since the wooden window frames are in a **"Good State" (Value: 8)**, it is not cost-effective to replace the entire window unit. Instead, replacing the single glass panes with double or triple glazing will significantly improve thermal retention while preserving the existing frames.

#### Phase 3: Heating & Emission System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the overall heat demand is lowered, allowing for a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature system.
4. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the existing mixed emission system (radiators/convectors) should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions.
5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and there is no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality & Ventilation (Medium Priority)
As the building becomes more airtight through insulation and glazing upgrades, mechanical ventilation becomes necessary to prevent moisture buildup.
6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D) to maintain indoor air quality without losing excessive heat.

#### Phase 5: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the EPC score.
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.
