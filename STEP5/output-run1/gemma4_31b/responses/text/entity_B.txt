**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data, this is an attached house from 1832 with an EPC score of 262. Given the age of the property and its current state, the most cost-effective and energy-efficient strategy is to follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing the energy demand of the building before upgrading the systems that provide the energy.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to minimize heat loss. Because the house was built in 1832, the walls are solid brick without cavities, meaning traditional cavity wall insulation is not an option.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** The flat-roof extension is currently uninsulated. Since heat rises, this is the most immediate priority to prevent significant energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. To maximize efficiency and eliminate thermal bridges, **exterior wall insulation** is recommended. If the facade cannot be altered, interior insulation is the alternative, though slightly less efficient.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The uninsulated floor above the basement is a major source of heat loss and discomfort ("cold feet"). Adding insulation here completes the thermal shell.

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the thermal envelope is sealed, the total heat load of the house is significantly reduced. This allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system.

4.  **Replace Radiators with Low-Temperature Emitters:** The current radiators are in poor condition. To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, these should be replaced with low-temperature emitters (such as underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators).
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no district heating or geothermal potential, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Priority: Medium)
Insulating an old house increases its airtightness. To prevent moisture buildup, mold, and poor indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Move from a basic natural ventilation system to a mechanical system (such as System C or a balanced System D with heat recovery). This ensures a constant flow of fresh air without losing the heat gained from the new insulation.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
The final step is to transition from consuming grid energy to generating your own to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will significantly lower operational costs and further improve the EPC score.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the energy required to heat the home. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new, lower demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Protects the building structure and health in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets the electricity costs of the heat pump and ventilation. |

*Note: Because the windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition, replacing them is not recommended at this time, as the return on investment would be low compared to the insulation and heating upgrades.*
