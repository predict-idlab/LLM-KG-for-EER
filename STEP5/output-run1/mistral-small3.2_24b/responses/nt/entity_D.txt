### Renovation Plan:
Given the building data, the property is a semi-detached house built in 2017 with an EPC score of 87 (B Label). The primary motivation of the owner is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

Proposed Renovation Path:

**Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)**
The building has significant heat loss through non-insulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.
1. **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises rapidly through flat sections.
2. **Insulate Wall on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Left` and `Wall_Front` are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently well insulated, so no action is needed here.

**Phase 2: Window Performance**
The window performance should be enhanced, since single glazing leads to significant heat losses.
4. **Replace Glazing:** All glazing should be replaced, as all windows still have double glazing. Adding double or even triple glazing would drastically improve the thermal performance.

**Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition**
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.
5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

**Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.
6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System D to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

**Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Exterior Wall Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Flat Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

### Implementation Notes:
- **Replace Glazing** should be done in a phased approach to minimize disruption.
- **Air-Water Heat Pump** installation should be coordinated with the replacement of the existing boiler.
- **PV-Panels on Roof** should be oriented and tilted to maximize solar gain.
- **Replace Ventilation System** should be done last to ensure the building is properly sealed before the new system is installed.

This plan ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before installing high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for an oversized and more expensive HVAC system. The sequence is designed to maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective.
