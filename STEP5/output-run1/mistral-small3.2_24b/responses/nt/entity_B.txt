### Renovation Plan

#### Building Overview
- **Building Type:** Attached building
- **Construction Year:** 1832
- **Current EPC Score:** 262 (D Label)
- **Building Envelope:**
  - **RoofMain:** Moderately Insulated (Gable Roof)
  - **RoofExtension:** Not Insulated (Flat Roof)
  - **Wall_Front:** Not Insulated (Full Brick Wall)
  - **Wall_Rear:** Not Insulated (Full Brick Wall)
  - **FloorAboveBasement:** Not Insulated
- **Windows:**
  - **Living Area:** Double Glazing
  - **Bedrooms:** Double Glazing
- **Heating System:** Radiator (Air Heating)
- **Ventilation System:** Ventilation System A
- **PV Potential:** True
- **Geothermal Potential:** False
- **Heat Grid Availability:** False

#### Renovation Motivation
The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC score.

#### Renovation Path
To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation should follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

**Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)**
The building has significant heat loss through non-insulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.

1. **Insulate Flat Roof:** The `RoofExtension` is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises rapidly through flat sections.
2. **Insulate Wall On Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement.

**Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)**
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

**Phase 3: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

6. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

#### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Exterior Wall Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Flat Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

#### Rationale
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** Prioritizing the building envelope first ensures that the heat demand is minimized before installing expensive HVAC systems.
- **Energy Efficiency:** Insulating the roof, walls, and floor reduces heat loss, making the building more efficient.
- **Sustainability:** Transitioning to an Air-Water Heat Pump and installing PV panels ensures that the building uses renewable energy sources.
- **Air Quality:** Upgrading the ventilation system maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope.

This plan ensures that the building is renovated in a way that maximizes energy efficiency, reduces energy costs, and aligns with the owner's motivation to improve the EPC score.
