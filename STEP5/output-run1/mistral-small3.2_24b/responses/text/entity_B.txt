Given the house's age (1832) and the existing conditions, the proposed renovation path is as follows:

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building has several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Flat Roof:** The flat-roof extension is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises; insulating the flat roof is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
2. **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are solid brick and not insulated. Exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and improve thermal retention.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the basement.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the basic natural ventilation system to a more efficient system (such as System D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
**Exterior Wall Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Flat Roof Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **Ventilation Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

This sequence ensures that each renovation step builds on the previous one, maximizing energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness.
