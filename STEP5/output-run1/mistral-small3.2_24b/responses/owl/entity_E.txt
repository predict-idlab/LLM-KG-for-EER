### Renovation Plan for the Detached Building (Constructed in 1862)

**Current EPC Score:** 165 (D Label)
**Building Type:** Detached
**Construction Year:** 1862
**Potential for PV:** True
**Presence of Heat Grid:** False

**Primary Motivation:** Improve the EPC rating

Given the age of the building and the current EPC rating (D Label), a multi-phase renovation is recommended to optimize energy efficiency while maintaining cost-effectiveness. The renovation strategy should follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence to ensure maximum energy savings and a sustainable transition to renewable energy sources.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is an 1862 detached house with several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Cavity Walls (Front, Rear, Left, and Right):** All four walls are cavity walls and currently uninsulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is a cost-effective way to improve thermal retention. Since the walls are cavity walls, cavity insulation is the most suitable method.
2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The floor above the crawl space is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the crawl space.
3. **Replace Glazing (All Windows):** All windows have single glazing, which leads to significant heat losses. **Replacing single glazing with double or triple glazing** would drastically improve the thermal performance of the windows.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.
5. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Cavity Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor Above Crawl Space)** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Reduce the remaining thermal heat losses. |
| **3** | **Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Generates renewable electricity to power the heat pump and ventilation system. |

This renovation plan ensures that the building's energy efficiency is maximized while maintaining cost-effectiveness and sustainability. The sequence of actions is designed to prioritize high-impact improvements first, followed by complementary upgrades to further enhance performance.
