### Renovation Plan:
Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building from 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

Proposed Renovation Path

**Phase 1: Strengthening the Building Envelope (Priority: High)**
The building has significant heat loss through non-insulated elements. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (`Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right`) are cavity walls and not insulated. Since they are cavity walls, **wall cavity insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:** The `FloorAboveCrawlSpace` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts from the crawl space.
*Note: Windows are single-glazed and frames are in a "Good State," so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

**Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)**
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.
3.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since geothermal potential is "false" and there is no heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a heat pump, the current radiators should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new generator.

**Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.
5.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

**Summary of Optimal Sequence**
**Wall Cavity Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Floor Insulation** $\rightarrow$ **Air-Water Heat Pump** $\rightarrow$ **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** $\rightarrow$ **PV Panels**.

This plan ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before installing high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for an oversized and more expensive HVAC system.
