To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce overall energy demand before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant potential for improvement in its shell. Addressing these first ensures that any subsequent heating system is correctly sized and operates at maximum efficiency.

1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are cavity walls and are currently not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is one of the most cost-effective upgrades available, offering a high return on investment with low nuisance levels.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is currently uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the ground and eliminates cold drafts.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** The bedrooms and living area currently utilize single glazing, which is a major source of thermal leakage. Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State" (value 8), **replacing the glazing** (rather than the entire window) is the most cost-effective way to drastically improve thermal performance.

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the total heat demand will drop, allowing for a transition from a traditional boiler to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To ensure the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the current emission system (radiators/convectors) should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no potential for geothermal captation and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Sustainable Generation (Priority: Long-term)
The final phase focuses on maintaining a healthy indoor environment and offsetting the electrical load of the new heat pump.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** The building currently uses Ventilation System A (natural). After the envelope is sealed with new glazing and wall insulation, air exchange is reduced. Upgrading to a more controlled ventilation system (System C or D) is necessary to prevent moisture buildup.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the heat pump and ventilation system, further lowering the EPC score and operational costs.
