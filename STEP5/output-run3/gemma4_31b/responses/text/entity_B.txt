Based on the provided building data, this is an attached house from 1832 with a significant floor area (212 m²) and an EPC score of 262. To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing the energy demand of the building envelope before upgrading the heating systems to avoid installing oversized and inefficient equipment.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to eliminate heat leakage. Given the house was built in 1832 with solid brick walls, the thermal mass is high, but the insulation is non-existent in key areas.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Since heat rises and the flat roof is currently uninsulated, this is the most immediate priority to reduce energy loss.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. Since the building is not historically protected, **exterior wall insulation** is highly recommended as it is more effective than interior insulation at eliminating thermal bridges and protecting the old brickwork.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The uninsulated floor above the basement is a primary source of "cold feet" and heat loss. Adding insulation here will significantly improve thermal comfort.
*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and the frames are in good condition; replacing them now would not be cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Upgrade (Priority: Medium)
Once the building is sealed, the total heat load is drastically reduced, allowing for a transition to low-temperature heating.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The current radiators are in poor condition. They should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (e.g., oversized radiators or underfloor heating) to ensure the new heat pump can operate at maximum efficiency.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given the lack of geothermal potential and district heating, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Ventilation and Air Quality (Priority: Medium)
As the house becomes more airtight through insulation, the existing natural ventilation will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). This prevents heat loss from uncontrolled drafts while maintaining a healthy living environment.

#### Phase 4: Renewable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To further lower the EPC score and offset the electricity costs of the new heat pump.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Utilizing the confirmed potential for PV panels will allow the house to generate its own clean electricity, maximizing the operational performance of the heat pump.
