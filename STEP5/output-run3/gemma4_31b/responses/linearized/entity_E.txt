To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC rating cost-effectively, the renovation should follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing energy demand through insulation before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The goal is to seal the building and reduce the energy required for heating.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four external walls are cavity walls and are currently not insulated. Injecting insulation into the cavities is the most cost-effective method to reduce thermal loss without altering the building's facade.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space should be insulated to eliminate cold drafts and prevent heat loss to the ground.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Both the living area and bedrooms currently have single glazing, which is a major source of heat leakage. Since the wooden frames are in a "Good State" (value 8), **replacing the glazing** (rather than the entire window) is the most cost-effective way to improve thermal performance.

#### Phase 2: Heating System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the heat demand is lowered through insulation, the heating system can be downsized and modernized.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the current radiators/emission system should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** With no potential for geothermal energy or access to a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Renewables (Priority: Long-term)
Final steps to ensure a healthy living environment and reduce operational costs.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrading from Ventilation System A (natural) to a mechanical system is necessary once the building is sealed to prevent moisture buildup and ensure air quality.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and significantly lower the energy score.
