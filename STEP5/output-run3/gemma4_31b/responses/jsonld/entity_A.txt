**Renovation Plan**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached building constructed in 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (D Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation will follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: optimizing the thermal envelope to reduce energy demand before upgrading the heating and generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has several critical areas of heat loss. Addressing these first prevents the installation of an oversized heating system and provides the most immediate impact on the EPC score.
1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Since heat rises, insulating the gable roof is the highest priority for reducing thermal loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** The `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all full brick and not insulated. As these are not cavity walls, exterior insulation is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the structure.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the basement and improves ground-floor thermal comfort.
*Note: The flat roof (`RoofExtension`) is already moderately insulated and the windows are double-glazed with frames in a good state; therefore, these are not prioritized to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the envelope is sealed, the building's total heat demand will be significantly lower, allowing for a transition to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before upgrading the boiler, the existing radiators and heating elements should be transitioned to low-temperature emissions. This is crucial to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal energy and no available heat grid, an Air-Water Heat Pump is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Increasing the airtightness of the building through insulation necessitates a ventilation upgrade to prevent humidity buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to ensure that the energy gains from insulation are not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further drive down the EPC score toward a B or A label.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Drastically reduces the total energy demand of the building. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the building for high-efficiency heating. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains air quality in a now-sealed thermal envelope. |
| **5** | **PV-Panels on Roof** | Offsets operational costs and maximizes EPC improvement. |
