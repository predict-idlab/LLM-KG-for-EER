### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant heat loss through several uninsulated elements. Addressing these first ensures that any future heating system is sized correctly and operates at peak efficiency.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof:** While the main gable roof is moderately insulated, the `RoofExtension` (flat roof) is not. Since heat rises, this is a high-priority intervention to stop significant energy leakage.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** Both `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` are full brick and currently not insulated. Because they are not cavity walls and have no historical protection, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and drastically lower the energy score.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the ground.

*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and the wooden frames are in a "Good State," so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the envelope is sealed, the building's heat demand will be significantly lower, allowing for a transition to a sustainable, low-temperature system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** The current system uses radiators. To maximize the efficiency (COP) of a modern heat pump, the emission system should be transitioned to low-temperature output first.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no potential for geothermal drilling/captation and no available heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
Increasing the airtightness of the building through insulation necessitates a better ventilation strategy to prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the current Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient mechanical system (such as System C or D).

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further drive down the EPC score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity required to power the new heat pump and ventilation system.
