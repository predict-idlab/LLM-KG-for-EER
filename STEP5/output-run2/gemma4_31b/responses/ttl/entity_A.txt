**Renovation Plan: Energy Efficiency Optimization**

Based on the provided building data and the RENO ontology, the property is a semi-detached house from 1951 with an EPC score of 388 (D Label). The owner's primary objective is to improve the EPC rating. 

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, this plan adopts the **"Fabric First"** approach. By optimizing the thermal envelope first, we reduce the overall heat demand, which allows for the installation of a smaller, more efficient heating system and prevents energy waste.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: Critical)
The building has significant heat loss through its primary structure. Addressing these elements first provides the highest return on investment for the EPC score.

1.  **Insulate Gable Roof:** The `RoofMain` is currently not insulated. Because heat rises, this is the most critical intervention to prevent thermal loss.
2.  **Insulate Walls on Exterior:** `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are all full brick and not insulated. Since they are not cavity walls, **exterior insulation** is the most effective method to eliminate thermal bridges and maximize efficiency. (If budget is strictly limited, interior insulation is a viable but slightly less efficient alternative).
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The `FloorAboveBasement` is not insulated. Adding insulation here prevents cold drafts and reduces heat loss to the ground.

*Note: Windows are already double-glazed and frames are in a "Good State" (Value 8), so replacing them is not recommended at this stage to maintain cost-effectiveness.*

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition (Priority: High)
Once the building is sealed, the heat demand is significantly lower. We can now transition from the outdated boiler to a sustainable system.

4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** Before installing a new generator, the current radiators/emission systems should be upgraded to low-temperature emissions. This is essential to maximize the efficiency (COP) of a heat pump.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no geothermal potential (horizontal or vertical), an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
A well-insulated house is an airtight house. To prevent moisture buildup and maintain indoor air quality, the ventilation must be upgraded.

6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more modern system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the thermal gains from Phase 1 are not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further drive down the EPC score.

7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the heat pump and ventilation system, significantly reducing operational costs.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Minimizes heat loss and reduces the required capacity of the heating system. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches to a high-efficiency, sustainable energy source. |
