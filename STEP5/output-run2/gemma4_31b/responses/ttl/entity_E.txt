### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)
The goal is to minimize heat loss through the building's shell. This is the most cost-effective way to reduce long-term operational costs.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** All four exterior walls (Front, Left, Rear, and Right) are identified as cavity walls and are currently not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is the most cost-effective and least disruptive method for this specific wall type.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is a primary source of heat loss and drafts. Adding insulation here will significantly improve thermal comfort and efficiency.
*Note: The gable roof is already well-insulated, so no further action is required there.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (High Priority)
The building currently relies on single glazing, which acts as a "thermal hole" in the envelope.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Because the wooden window frames are in a "Good State" (Value 8), it is not cost-effective to replace the entire window unit. Instead, **replacing the glazing** (upgrading from single to double or triple glazing) provides the maximum energy gain for the lowest cost.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Transition (Medium Priority)
Once the heat demand is lowered via insulation and glazing, the heating system can be downsized and modernized.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the current radiators/convectors should be transitioned to a low-temperature emission system.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since there is no heat grid and no potential for geothermal energy, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the existing boiler.

#### Phase 4: Ventilation and Air Quality (Medium Priority)
As the house becomes more airtight through insulation and new glazing, the natural ventilation (System A) may become insufficient.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from Ventilation System A to a more controlled system (such as System C or D) to prevent moisture buildup and ensure a healthy indoor climate.

#### Phase 5: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the EPC score.
