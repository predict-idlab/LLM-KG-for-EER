### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building has significant heat loss through its walls and floor. Addressing these first is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy score.
1.  **Insulate Wall Cavity:** The Front, Left, and Rear walls are identified as cavity walls and are currently not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is the most cost-effective method for this wall type, offering a high return on investment with minimal nuisance.
2.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the crawl space is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents heat loss to the ground and improves thermal comfort in the living areas.
*Note: The roof is already well-insulated, so no further roof insulation is required.*

#### Phase 2: Window Performance (Priority: High)
The building currently uses single glazing, which is a major source of thermal bridging.
3.  **Replace Glazing:** Since the wooden window frames are in a "Good State," it is not cost-effective to replace the entire window. Instead, **replacing the glazing** (upgrading from single to double or triple glazing) will drastically improve the U-value while preserving the existing frames.

#### Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition (Priority: Medium)
With a tightened envelope, the building's heat demand is reduced, allowing for a transition to a sustainable heating system.
4.  **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:** To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, the existing mix of radiators and electric convectors should be transitioned to a low-temperature emission system.
5.  **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that there is no potential for geothermal energy or a heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable replacement for the current boiler.

#### Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
As the building becomes more airtight through insulation and new glazing, natural ventilation (System A) becomes insufficient.
6.  **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade to a more efficient ventilation system (e.g., System C or D) to ensure healthy indoor air quality and prevent moisture buildup.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further drive down the EPC score.
7.  **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide renewable energy to power the new HVAC systems.
