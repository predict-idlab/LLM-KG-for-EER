Based on the provided building data, this is an attached property from 1832 with a significant floor area (212 m²) and an EPC score of 262. To maximize energy efficiency and cost-effectiveness, the renovation must follow the **"Fabric First"** principle: reducing the energy demand of the building envelope before upgrading the heating and energy generation systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
Since the house is from 1832 and has solid brick walls, it likely suffers from significant thermal bridging and heat loss. Addressing the envelope first prevents the installation of an oversized (and therefore more expensive) heating system.

1.  **Insulate Flat Roof Extension:** Because heat rises, the uninsulated flat roof is a primary source of energy loss. This is a high-impact, relatively low-cost priority.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The front and rear walls are solid brick and uninsulated. **Exterior wall insulation** is highly recommended here to eliminate thermal bridges and protect the old brickwork from temperature fluctuations.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The current lack of insulation in the floor above the basement leads to "cold feet" and heat loss. Adding insulation here will significantly improve thermal comfort.

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Once the thermal envelope is sealed, the building's total heat demand will drop, allowing for a transition to a high-efficiency, low-temperature system.

4.  **Replace Emission Systems:** The existing radiators are in poor condition. To maximize the efficiency of a modern heat pump, these should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (such as oversized radiators or underfloor heating).
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most sustainable and cost-effective replacement for the old boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
As the house becomes more airtight due to insulation and new heating components, the existing natural ventilation (System A) will no longer be sufficient to prevent moisture buildup and ensure healthy indoor air quality.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from natural ventilation to a mechanical system (such as **System C or D**). A System D (balanced ventilation with heat recovery) would be the most energy-efficient choice, as it recovers heat from outgoing air.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the increased electricity consumption from the heat pump and ventilation system, the property should leverage its capacity for renewable energy.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will lower operational costs and further improve the EPC score.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Flat Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Minimizes heat loss and lowers the required capacity of the future heating system. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Switches the energy source from fossil fuels to a high-efficiency electric system. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents humidity and mold issues in a now-insulated, airtight home. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Provides renewable energy to power the new electrical systems. |
