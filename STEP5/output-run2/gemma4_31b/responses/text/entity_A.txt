**Renovation Plan**

To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388) for this 1951 semi-detached house, the renovation must follow a **"Fabric First"** strategy. By optimizing the thermal envelope before upgrading the heating system, we reduce the total heat demand, allowing for the installation of a smaller, more efficient, and less expensive heat pump.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Priority: High)
The building currently has significant heat loss through the roof, walls, and floor. Addressing these is the most cost-effective way to lower the energy demand.

1.  **Insulate Pitched Roof:** The main roof has no insulation. Since heat rises, this is the highest priority. Adding high-quality insulation here will provide the most immediate impact on energy reduction.
2.  **Insulate Exterior Walls:** The solid brick walls are currently uninsulated. To eliminate thermal bridges and significantly reduce heat loss, **exterior wall insulation** is recommended. If exterior modification is not possible due to the semi-detached nature of the property, interior insulation is the alternative.
3.  **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is uninsulated. Adding insulation here prevents "cold feet" and stops heat from leaking into the basement.
*Note: The flat-roof extension already has moderate insulation and the windows are already double-glazed in good condition; these will be left as-is to remain cost-effective.*

#### Phase 2: Heating and Emission System Transition (Priority: Medium)
Now that the heat demand is lowered, the outdated and inefficient heating system can be replaced with a sustainable alternative.

4.  **Upgrade to Low-Temperature Emission Systems:** Since the current radiators and air heating systems are in poor condition, they should be replaced with **low-temperature emitters** (e.g., underfloor heating or oversized low-temp radiators). This is critical to ensure the efficiency of the next step.
5.  **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:** Given that geothermal energy and district heating are unavailable, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most efficient and sustainable choice to replace the old boiler.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation (Priority: Medium)
A 1951 house is naturally "leaky." Once the walls, roof, and floor are insulated, the house becomes more airtight, which increases the risk of humidity and mold.

6.  **Upgrade Ventilation System:** Transition from a basic natural ventilation system to a more controlled system (such as **System C or D**). This ensures a healthy indoor climate and prevents the loss of heat through uncontrolled drafts.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further lower the EPC score.

7.  **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:** Since the house has confirmed potential for PV, installing solar panels on the roof will provide renewable energy to power the heat pump and ventilation system, reducing operational costs.

---

### Summary of Optimal Sequence and Rationale

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Dramatically reduces the total energy required to heat the home. |
| **2** | **Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Prepares the house to operate efficiently with a heat pump. |
| **3** | **Air-Water Heat Pump** | Replaces fossil fuel heating with a sustainable, high-efficiency source. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Prevents moisture buildup in the now-sealed thermal envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panel Installation** | Offsets electricity costs and maximizes the EPC improvement. |
