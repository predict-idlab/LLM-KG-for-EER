### Renovation Plan for Attached Building (Example 3)

**Property Overview:**
- **Type:** Attached building (1832)
- **EPC Score:** 262 (C Label)
- **Owner's Motivation:** To improve EPC rating
- **Current Systems:**
  - **Generation System:** Boiler
  - **Emission System:** Radiator (Air Heating)
  - **Ventilation System:** Type A (Natural)
  - **Windows:** Double-glazed, wooden frames in good state
  - **Walls:** Two full brick walls (`Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear`) not insulated
  - **Roof:** Flat section (`RoofExtension`) not insulated
  - **Floor:** Floor above basement (`FloorAboveBasement`) not insulated

**Renovation Strategy:**
The plan follows the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence, prioritizing cost-effective, energy-efficient upgrades that will maximize the EPC score improvement.

**Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (High Priority)**
1. **Insulate Walls on Exterior:**
   - **Action:** Insulate `Wall_Front` and `Wall_Rear` on the exterior.
   - **Rationale:** Full brick walls are not cavity walls, so exterior insulation is the most effective method. This addresses the largest source of heat loss while maintaining cost-effectiveness.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#InsulateWallExterior>`

2. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:**
   - **Action:** Add insulation to `FloorAboveBasement`.
   - **Rationale:** Eliminates cold drafts from the basement and improves overall comfort.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#InsulateFloorAboveBasement>`

3. **Insulate Flat Roof:**
   - **Action:** Insulate `RoofExtension`.
   - **Rationale:** Flat roofs lose heat rapidly; insulation here is a priority for lowering energy consumption.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#InsulateFlatRoof>`

**Phase 2: Ventilation Upgrade (Medium Priority)**
4. **Replace Ventilation System:**
   - **Action:** Upgrade from Ventilation System Type A (natural) to Type C or D.
   - **Rationale:** A sealed envelope reduces uncontrolled air leakage, making a more efficient ventilation system necessary to maintain indoor air quality.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#ReplaceVentilationSystem>`

**Phase 3: Heating System Transition (Medium Priority)**
5. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - **Action:** Install an Air-Water Heat Pump.
   - **Rationale:** With geothermal potential "false" and no heat grid, this is the most sustainable and efficient replacement for the existing boiler.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#PlaceAirWaterHeatpump>`

**Phase 4: Emission System Upgrade (Medium Priority)**
6. **Place Emission System on Low Temperature:**
   - **Action:** Transition the existing radiators to low-temperature emissions.
   - **Rationale:** Maximizes the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new heat pump by ensuring the system operates at optimal temperatures.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#PlaceLowTemperatureEmission>`

**Phase 5: Renewable Energy Generation (Long-term Priority)**
7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:**
   - **Action:** Install solar panels on the roof.
   - **Rationale:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Solar panels will offset the electricity consumption of the heat pump and ventilation system, further improving the EPC score.
   - **Ontology Reference:** `<https://w3id.org/reno#PlacePVPanelsOnRoof>`

**Summary of Optimal Sequence:**
**Insulate Walls $\rightarrow$ Insulate Floor $\rightarrow$ Insulate Roof $\rightarrow$ Replace Ventilation System $\rightarrow$ Air-Water Heat Pump $\rightarrow$ Low-Temperature Emission Upgrade $\rightarrow$ PV Panels**

**Expected Outcome:**
The proposed plan will significantly improve the EPC score (from C to B or higher) while ensuring cost-effectiveness and energy efficiency. The "fabric-first" approach ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before transitioning to high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for oversized and more expensive HVAC equipment.
