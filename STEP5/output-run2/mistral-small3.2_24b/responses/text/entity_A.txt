To maximize energy efficiency and improve the EPC score (currently 388) in a cost-effective manner, the renovation should follow a "fabric-first" approach, ensuring the building's thermal envelope is optimized before upgrading heating systems.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is a 1951 semi-detached house with several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Pitched Roof:** The pitched roof is currently not insulated. This is a priority as heat rises; insulating the pitched roof is one of the most effective ways to lower energy consumption.
2. **Insulate Walls on Interior:** The exterior walls are solid brick and not insulated. **Insulating the wall on the interior** is significantly more cost-effective than wall cavity insulation and provides a substantial increase in thermal retention.
3. **Insulate Floor Above Basement:** The floor above the basement is currently not insulated. Adding insulation here eliminates "cold feet" and reduces heat loss to the basement.

#### Phase 2: Heating & Emission System Transition
With a sealed envelope, the building's heat demand is now significantly lower, allowing for the installation of a more efficient, low-temperature system.

4. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:** Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.
5. **Upgrade Emission Systems:** Replace the existing radiators, underfloor heating, and air heating systems with low-temperature emission systems to maximize the Coefficient of Performance (COP) of the new heat pump.

#### Phase 3: Air Quality and Ventilation
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

6. **Replace Ventilation System:** Upgrade from the natural ventilation system to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

#### Phase 4: Sustainable Energy Generation (Priority: Long-term)
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

7. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:** The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence

| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Roof $\rightarrow$ Walls $\rightarrow$ Floor)** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Heat Pump and Low-Temp Emission Upgrade** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **3** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **4** | **PV Panels** | Provides renewable energy to power the heat pump and ventilation system. |

This plan ensures a cost-effective and efficient path to improving the energy performance of your home.
