Given the details of your 1862 detached house in Ghent, Belgium, with a current EPC score of 165, here’s a cost-effective and energy-efficient renovation plan. The focus will be on optimizing the thermal envelope first, followed by upgrading heating systems and ventilation.

### Proposed Renovation Path

#### Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (Fabric First Approach)
The goal is to reduce heat loss through the building's structure.

1. **Insulate Cavity Walls (Front, Rear, Left, Right):**
   - Since the walls are cavity walls, injecting insulation into the cavities is a cost-effective solution. This will significantly improve thermal performance.
   - Use high-performance cavity wall insulation to minimize thermal bridges.

2. **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space:**
   - If the floor is not already insulated, adding insulation (e.g., rigid foam boards or mineral wool) will prevent heat loss to the crawl space.
   - Ensure proper sealing to prevent drafts and moisture issues.

3. **Upgrade Roof Insulation (if needed):**
   - While the main roof is already insulated, check if additional insulation can be added to meet modern standards. If not, ensure it is well-maintained.

#### Phase 2: Window Performance Upgrade
Single-glazed windows are a major source of heat loss.

4. **Replace Glazing with Double or Triple Glazing:**
   - Replace all single-glazed windows with double or triple-glazed units. This will drastically reduce heat loss through windows.
   - Consider low-emissivity (Low-E) coatings and argon or krypton gas fill for better performance.

#### Phase 3: Heating System Transition
With a tighter thermal envelope, the heating demand will be lower, allowing for more efficient systems.

5. **Install Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - Replace the old boiler with an air-water heat pump, which is more energy-efficient and sustainable.
   - Ensure the heat pump is sized correctly for the reduced heating demand after insulation upgrades.

6. **Upgrade Emission Systems to Low-Temperature:**
   - Replace or upgrade radiators, underfloor heating, and air heating systems to work efficiently with the heat pump’s lower water temperatures.
   - Consider adding smart thermostats and zoning controls for better energy management.

#### Phase 4: Ventilation Upgrade
A sealed building envelope requires controlled ventilation to maintain air quality.

7. **Upgrade to Mechanical Ventilation with Heat Recovery (MVHR):**
   - Replace the basic natural ventilation system with an MVHR system to recover heat from exhaust air, improving energy efficiency and indoor air quality.

#### Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation (Long-Term)
To further improve the EPC score and reduce energy bills.

8. **Install Photovoltaic (PV) Panels:**
   - Add PV panels to the roof to generate renewable electricity, which can power the heat pump and other electrical systems.
   - Consider battery storage to maximize self-consumption of generated electricity.

### Summary of Optimal Sequence
| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulate Cavity Walls** | Reduces heat loss through the largest surface area of the house. |
| **2** | **Insulate Floor Above Crawl Space** | Prevents heat loss to unconditioned spaces below. |
| **3** | **Replace Glazing** | Reduces heat loss through windows, which are significant thermal bridges. |
| **4** | **Install Air-Water Heat Pump** | Provides efficient heating tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **5** | **Upgrade Emission Systems** | Ensures compatibility with the heat pump and optimizes energy use. |
| **6** | **Upgrade to MVHR** | Maintains indoor air quality in a sealed envelope. |
| **7** | **Install PV Panels** | Generates renewable energy to offset the heat pump’s electricity consumption. |

This plan prioritizes cost-effective upgrades that maximize energy efficiency, ensuring long-term operational performance and a significant improvement in the EPC score.
