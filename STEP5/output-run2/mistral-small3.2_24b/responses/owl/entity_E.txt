### Renovation Plan

Given the building's data and the RENO ontology, the property is a detached building constructed in 1862 with an EPC score of 165 (C Label). The owner's primary motivation is to improve the EPC rating.

To maximize energy efficiency while remaining cost-effective, the renovation must follow the **"Insulate $\rightarrow$ Ventilate $\rightarrow$ Generate"** sequence. Upgrading heating systems before the building envelope is sealed leads to oversized equipment and wasted energy.

#### Proposed Renovation Path

**Phase 1: Thermal Envelope Optimization (The "Fabric First" Stage)**
The primary goal here is to stop heat leakage. Since the building is an old detached house with several uninsulated elements, this phase offers the highest return on investment.

1. **Insulate Wall Cavity:**
   - Both the `Wall_Front`, `Wall_Left`, `Wall_Rear`, and `Wall_Right` are cavity walls and not insulated. **Insulating the wall cavity** is significantly more cost-effective than exterior or interior insulation and provides a substantial increase in thermal retention.

**Phase 2: Window Performance**
The window performance should be enhanced, since the single glazing leads to significant heat losses.

2. **Replace Glazing:**
   - All glazing should be replaced, as all windows still have single glazing. Adding double or even triple glazing would drastically improve the thermal performance.

**Phase 3: High-Efficiency Heating Transition**
With a tight thermal envelope, the building can now operate on lower temperatures, allowing for a transition away from the existing boiler.

3. **Place Air-Water Heat Pump:**
   - Since the site has no potential for geothermal energy and no heat grid, an **Air-Water Heat Pump** is the most viable and efficient alternative to the current boiler.

**Phase 4: Air Quality and Ventilation**
Once the building is sealed and insulated, the air exchange rate decreases, making a modern ventilation system necessary to prevent moisture buildup and maintain air quality.

4. **Replace Ventilation System:**
   - Upgrade from Ventilation System A (natural) to a more efficient system (such as System C or D). This ensures that the heat gained from the new insulation is not lost through uncontrolled air leakage.

**Phase 5: Sustainable Energy Generation**
To offset the electricity consumption of the new heat pump and further improve the EPC label.

5. **Place PV-Panels on Roof:**
   - The building has confirmed potential for PV. Installing solar panels will provide the renewable electricity needed to power the heat pump and ventilation system.

#### Summary of Optimal Sequence
| Sequence | Action | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Insulation (Wall Cavity)** | Reduces the total energy demand of the house. |
| **2** | **Glazing Replacement** | Reduce the remaining thermal heat losses. |
| **3** | **Heat Pump** | Switches to a sustainable energy source tailored to the new lower heat demand. |
| **4** | **Ventilation Upgrade** | Maintains indoor air quality in a now-sealed envelope. |
| **5** | **PV Panels** | Offsets the electricity consumption of the new heat pump. |

This plan ensures that the building's thermal envelope is optimized before installing high-efficiency heating systems, preventing the need for an oversized and more expensive HVAC system.
